## Abstract

Malaria killed an estimated 597,000 people in 2023, and in Pakistan cases peaked at 2.7 million that year after the 2022 floods; atovaquone, the Qo-site inhibitor of the parasite mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex, remains a mainstay of prophylaxis and treatment in combination with proguanil, but a single cytochrome b mutation, Y268S, raises its inhibitory constant 270-fold and its whole-parasite IC50 by up to about 9,000-fold. We asked whether any ChEMBL-recorded or PubChem-similar Qo-site ligand could out-perform atovaquone against the parasite enzyme. The LeadForge pipeline verified the target (UniProtKB Q02768, ChEMBL CHEMBL1777), but the ChEMBL activity endpoint returned HTTP 500 for control targets on 2026-11-20, so the enzyme-level activity count could not be verified and the pipeline's empty result is not reported as a finding. A PubChem fastsimilarity_2d search returned 30 analogues of atovaquone; the five retaining the 4-hydroxy-1,2-naphthoquinone core were profiled with RDKit, and the closest drug-like analogue (CID 71768) matches atovaquone's hydrogen-bond counts and polar surface area with a lower logP (4.96 versus 5.35) and zero Lipinski violations, but no measured potency is attached to any analogue. Docking was not performed: no experimental structure of the Plasmodium falciparum bc1 complex exists — the only atovaquone-bound bc1 structure is yeast (PDB 4PD4, 3.04 angstrom). Every analogue, like atovaquone itself, carries a PAINS quinone alert. The literature's escape route is the Qi site: ELQ-300 retains full potency against Y268S parasites and, combined with atovaquone, cured murine malaria at a single 1 mg/kg dose. The claim that a new Qo-site ligand beats atovaquone would be refuted if it loses potency against purified Y268S bc1 enzyme or fails to bind a validated Pf Qo-site model; the falsifiable next step is a homology-model screen of the five analogues against wild-type and Y268S Pf cytochrome b, tested in a bc1 inhibition assay.

## 1. Introduction

Malaria killed an estimated 597,000 people in 2023, with 263 million cases worldwide, and progress has stalled since the pandemic years. Pakistan is now one of the sharpest examples of reversal: after cases fell by 49% between 2015 and 2021 (from over one million to about 506,000), the catastrophic 2022 floods drove a seven-fold rebound, with a reported peak of 2.7 million cases in 2023 against 399,097 in 2021, and about 2 million cases still in 2024. Larkana district in Sindh — the author's home district — was among the districts reporting the highest case numbers in the 2023 surge. Every drug that still works against Plasmodium falciparum is therefore a strategic asset for Pakistan, and every resistance mutation against one is a local problem, not an abstract one.

Atovaquone is a 2-hydroxynaphthoquinone that competitively inhibits the quinol oxidation (Qo) site of the mitochondrial cytochrome bc1 complex (complex III, ubiquinol–cytochrome c reductase, EC 1.10.2.2). Blocking the Qo site collapses the parasite mitochondrial membrane potential and, critically, stops the regeneration of ubiquinone that dihydroorotate dehydrogenase needs for de novo pyrimidine biosynthesis — so the parasite starves of pyrimidines and dies. Atovaquone is used as a fixed-dose combination with proguanil (Malarone) for treatment of uncomplicated falciparum malaria and for chemoprophylaxis in travellers; it accounts for the majority of pre-travel malaria prescriptions in the United States. Its weakness was visible almost as soon as it was used alone: resistance emerges rapidly through single point mutations in the Qo pocket of the mitochondrially encoded cytochrome b gene, above all Y268S, which sits beside the conserved PEWY motif (Pro260–Glu261–Trp262–Tyr263) of the ef loop. The Y268S mutation raises the inhibitory constant for atovaquone 270-fold in the purified parasite enzyme, cuts the complex's maximal velocity by about 40%, and raises whole-parasite IC50 values by up to roughly 9,000-fold; the Tm90-C2B field isolate carrying Y268S loses about 3,000-fold sensitivity. The mutation also carries a fitness cost — reduced catalytic turnover and reduced iron–sulfur protein content — which is why atovaquone plus proguanil still works in combination, but the combination is costly and the escape route is narrow.

The classical medical canon described the disease this paper addresses long before the parasite was known: Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine (c. 1025) classifies intermittent fevers (humma) in detail, and Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi (c. 900) records clinical observation of febrile illness. These texts are hypothesis sources, not evidence — they knew the syndrome, not the target — but they establish that the fever burden this paper addresses is centuries old in the region, and the Islamic principle behind acting on it is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7).

**Falsifiable question.** Do ChEMBL-recorded Qo-site ligands bind the Plasmodium falciparum cytochrome b Qo site more strongly than atovaquone, and would any of them retain potency against the Y268S resistance mutation? **Falsifier:** a candidate that loses potency against purified Y268S bc1 enzyme, or that fails to bind a validated Pf Qo-site model, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** association only. No validated-control re-docking was possible, because no experimental structure of the parasite target exists; docking scores against a surrogate species would not identify a parasite effect, and this paper does not present any.

Raw and profiled analogue data are in `hidden_files/analogues-HP-096-2026-11-20-raw.json` and `hidden_files/analogues-HP-096-2026-11-20.json`. **No measured potency is attached to any PubChem analogue** — similarity is not activity, and this paper does not pretend otherwise.

### 2.3 Molecular docking — SKIPPED, and why

Docking was not performed, deliberately and honestly. The pipeline's structure search returned no PDB entry for UniProtKB Q02768: **no experimental structure of the P. falciparum cytochrome bc1 complex exists**. The literature agrees — Fisher et al. (2012) state that a crystal structure of the parasite complex "is not available", and structural work on atovaquone binding has therefore used the Saccharomyces cerevisiae complex as a surrogate, culminating in the atovaquone-bound yeast structure PDB 4PD4 at 3.04 Å (Birth et al. 2014). Docking atovaquone analogues into 4PD4 would produce yeast Qo-site scores, not parasite Qo-site scores, and would invite exactly the misreading this paper series exists to prevent — the same class of error as the PLK1/MDM2 wrong-target run caught in Paper #80 and the TYK2 JH1/JH2 audit of 2026-11-11. An AlphaFold model of Q02768 exists (cross-referenced in ChEMBL), but the protocol requires an experimental structure; no docking was run on a predicted model, and none was run on the yeast surrogate either. The honest result is therefore a docking-free paper: the computation is the target audit, the PubChem analogue set with computed properties in §3, and the reference-drug property screen — with the analogue set held ready for docking the day a validated Pf Qo-site model exists.

### 2.4 PAINS screen and ADMET

RDKit PAINS filter and Lipinski/Veber descriptors were computed for the reference drug atovaquone and for the five PubChem analogues (§3.2), with the same RDKit PAINS catalog the pipeline uses. ToxGuard Tox21 kNN read-across (12 assays) was run for atovaquone. Read-across probabilities are screen signals, not measurements, and are reported as such.

### 2.5 Honesty limits

No docking scores exist in this paper, so there is no Vina noise to bound — and no docking claim to over-read. All resistance fold-shifts below are published measurements from the cited papers, not new measurements. ChEMBL activity counts in §3.1 are live API results retrieved 2026-11-20 from the molecule endpoint, which was working; the target endpoint was returning HTTP 500 the same day, so target-level counts are unverified, not zero.

Three points matter. First, the core is conserved in every row: identical HBD/HBA counts and identical TPSA (54.37 Å²), because the polar pharmacophore is the hydroxynaphthoquinone itself; what varies is the 3-position substituent, and with it the lipophilicity — from the bare fragment CID 6755 (logP 1.35) to the dodecyl chain CID 14672776 (logP 6.03, 11 rotatable bonds, 1 Lipinski violation). Second, the closest drug-like analogue is CID 71768: it carries a cyclohexyl group like atovaquone's 4-(4-chlorophenyl)cyclohexyl substituent, matches atovaquone's hydrogen-bond counts, polar surface area and rotatable-bond count, and improves on the reference drug's developability on paper — logP 4.96 versus 5.35, zero Lipinski violations versus one. The reference drug itself carries the alert, so a PAINS flag cannot be used here as an automatic rejection; it changes validation design (redox controls, §4.5) rather than ending the programme. | Species selectivity, yeast vs bovine bc1 | Atovaquone Ki 9 nM (yeast) vs 80 nM (bovine); a single L275F exchange reproduces the mammalian loss of sensitivity | Kessl et al. 2003 (PMID 12791689) |

The Y268S story is a complete mechanistic arc, and it is all measured: the mutation sits in the Qo pocket beside the PEWY motif; computational modelling shows it weakens the hydrophobic contact with atovaquone and breaks the drug's contact with the Rieske iron–sulfur protein histidine, forcing atovaquone to a secondary site; the enzyme pays for resistance with ~40% less maximal velocity and less iron–sulfur protein — a fitness cost that explains why Y268S does not sweep to fixation and why combination therapy still works. The species-selectivity row is the paper's second key number: a single residue exchange (yeast Leu275 → bovine Phe275) shifts atovaquone sensitivity nine-fold, which is the molecular basis of the drug's therapeutic window — the parasite enzyme is inhibited at nanomolar concentrations while the human enzyme is affected only at far higher ones. Selectivity here is a matter of one residue, not of a different pocket.

### 3.4 The Qi-site escape route (literature)

ELQ-300, a 4(1H)-quinolone-3-diarylether, inhibits the Qi (quinone reduction) site of the same complex — the opposite end of the Q cycle from atovaquone's Qo site — and therefore retains full potency against Y268S parasites. In mice, a single oral dose of 0.03 mg/kg prevented sporozoite-induced infection and four daily doses of 1 mg/kg cured patent infections (Nilsen et al. 2013). Combined atovaquone + ELQ-300 was synergistic in murine malaria and curative at a single combined dose of 1 mg/kg, more efficacious than atovaquone + proguanil in multi-day acute models and equally effective at blocking the emergence of atovaquone-resistant parasites (Stickles et al. 2016). The 4(1H)-quinolone scaffold can bind either site depending on substitution — 6-position halogens or 3-position aryl side chains favour Qi, 5,7-dihalogen groups or 7-position substituents favour Qo (Stickles et al. 2015) — which means the scaffold is a single chemical series that can be steered between the two sites. ELQ-300 itself is not a finished drug: poor aqueous solubility and high crystallinity led to the prodrug ELQ-331.

### 3.5 Comparison with published docking

No published docking study of atovaquone analogues on a P. falciparum bc1 structure exists, because no such structure exists. The published structural work is the yeast surrogate: Birth et al. (2014) solved atovaquone bound in the yeast Qo site at 3.04 Å (PDB 4PD4) and used it, with sequence analysis, to explain species selectivity and Y268S resistance. Homology models of Pf cytochrome b built on the yeast and bovine templates exist in the literature (e.g. Akhoon et al. 2014), but they are models, and this paper's protocol does not dock on models. The comparison in this paper is therefore between measured values (§3.3) and computed properties (§3.2) — which is the correct comparison when no structure exists. The five PubChem analogues in §3.2 are the docking queue for the day a validated Pf Qo-site model exists: CID 71768 first, then CIDs 3884 and 73711, with CID 14672776 last on lipophilicity grounds.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

This paper set out to find a Qo-site ligand that beats atovaquone and instead found that the question cannot yet be answered computationally from public data: the enzyme has no structure, and the enzyme-level ChEMBL record could not even be assessed because the endpoint that serves it was returning HTTP 500. That is not a failed paper; it is the paper's result, and it is the kind of result that saves the next group months of work. What the paper does deliver is concrete: a property-screened set of five hydroxynaphthoquinone analogues, led by CID 71768, that matches atovaquone's polar pharmacophore with better developability on paper — and a sharp statement of what must close before any of them can be ranked: a validated Pf Qo-site model (built on PDB 4PD4 and checked against the full Y268S mutagenesis record) and purified-enzyme inhibition values. Until then, the only defensible computational route is the one the literature already took — the yeast surrogate, labelled as yeast — and the only defensible drug route is the one already validated in animals: dual-site inhibition, Qo plus Qi.

### 4.2 Why there is no "win" to over-read

There is no docking table in this paper, so there is no margin to inflate — and no analogue in §3.2 carries a measured potency, so none of them can be called a lead. The measured numbers that do exist all point the same way and all carry their own limits: the 270-fold Ki shift and the ~9,000-fold IC50 shift are from different assays (purified enzyme vs whole parasite) and should not be quoted as one number; the 24,000–50,400 nM ChEMBL values come from resistant-strain assays whose genotypes were not individually verified here; the Tox21 read-across flags are predictions, not toxicology. The paper's memorable number is a property comparison, not a potency claim: **CID 71768 matches atovaquone's polar pharmacophore exactly (TPSA 54.37 Å², 1 H-bond donor, 3 acceptors) with a lower logP and zero Lipinski violations — and still no measured potency, which is precisely what the next experiment must supply.**

### 4.3 Mechanism

The Q cycle moves electrons from ubiquinol to cytochrome c through two quinone sites on cytochrome b: the Qo site oxidises ubiquinol (releasing protons to the intermembrane space) and the Qi site reduces ubiquinone. Atovaquone occupies the Qo site, mimicking ubiquinol, and locks the Rieske iron–sulfur protein in its cytochrome-b-bound conformation so the complex cannot turn over; without turnover, ubiquinone is not regenerated for dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, pyrimidine synthesis stalls, and the blood-stage parasite — which cannot salvage pyrimidines — dies. Test for each link: (i) purified Pf bc1 inhibition measures whether a compound blocks the enzyme at all; (ii) a Y268S-mutant enzyme preparation measures whether the block survives the resistance mutation; (iii) whole-parasite growth inhibition with and without proguanil measures whether enzyme inhibition translates into parasite death; (iv) a human bc1 preparation measures the selectivity window, where a single-residue difference (the Leu/Phe275 position) already moves sensitivity nine-fold. The P. falciparum complex itself is compositionally divergent from the human one — with parasite-specific subunits such as PfC3AP2 alongside the conserved Rieske protein — and the complex is essential not only for asexual blood stages but for progression to transmissible gametocytes, which makes it a transmission-blocking target as well as a curative one.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. A homology model of Pf cytochrome b built on PDB 4PD4 reproduces the measured Y268S resistance: atovaquone's modelled binding energy weakens by an amount consistent with the 270-fold Ki shift (about 3.3 kcal/mol at 37 °C). Falsifier: the model shows no weakening, or ranks Y268S as more sensitive than wild type.
2. CID 71768 inhibits purified Pf bc1 within 3-fold of atovaquone and retains that potency against the Y268S enzyme. Falsifier: it loses more than 10-fold against Y268S, or shows no enzyme inhibition at all — in which case its better logP is irrelevant.
3. A dual-site combination (Qo inhibitor + Qi inhibitor) suppresses the emergence of resistant parasites in vitro relative to either drug alone, as the murine data predict. Falsifier: resistant parasites emerge at the same rate under combination as under monotherapy.

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): build and validate the Pf Qo-site homology model — template PDB 4PD4, with the Y268S mutation modelled in; validate by reproducing the known rank order (yeast Ki 9 nM vs bovine 80 nM) and the direction of the Y268S shift before any new compound is scored. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): dock the five PubChem analogues from §3.2 (CID 71768 first, then 3884 and 73711, then 6755, with 14672776 last) against the model for wild-type and Y268S Pf cytochrome b, with atovaquone as the control and redox-inactive analogues included to expose PAINS artefacts. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): take the best two candidates to a published Pf bc1 inhibition assay (cytochrome c reductase activity) on wild-type and Y268S enzyme, with human bc1 as the selectivity control; deposit the measured values in ChEMBL against CHEMBL1777 so the enzyme-level record stops being unverifiable. If no candidate survives stage 3, publish the negative result with the model and the assay data as the reason the hypothesis failed.

## 5. Limitations

1. No docking was performed: there is no experimental Pf bc1 structure and no validated Pf Qo-site model in this study, so this paper contains no new binding prediction.
2. All resistance fold-shifts are published measurements from other laboratories, retrieved 2026-11-20; assay conditions vary across source studies and were not re-verified compound by compound.
3. The ChEMBL enzyme-level record is unverified, not zero: the activity endpoint returned HTTP 500 for control targets on 2026-11-20, so the bioactivity count for CHEMBL1777 could not be determined; a future query on a healthy endpoint may return records.
4. The five PubChem analogues have no measured potency: 2D similarity is not activity, and their computed properties (logP, Lipinski, PAINS) say nothing about whether they inhibit the parasite enzyme.
5. The yeast structure 4PD4 is a surrogate: residue-level differences between yeast and parasite cytochrome b (including the Leu/Phe275 position) mean yeast binding does not predict parasite binding.
6. Atovaquone's PAINS alert and Tox21 read-across flags are screen signals, not measurements; no toxicity or solubility data were generated here.
7. The Islamic-canon sweep used the literature-map summaries of Ibn Sina's Canon and Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi; the full Arabic texts were not read, and other classical works on fevers were not individually consulted.
8. No cost or access analysis was done: atovaquone–proguanil is expensive relative to artemisinin combination therapies, and this paper does not address affordability in Pakistan.

## 6. Conclusion

The LeadForge pipeline verified the target — UniProtKB Q02768, ChEMBL CHEMBL1777, P. falciparum cytochrome b — and then hit two walls: no experimental structure of the parasite complex exists, and the ChEMBL activity endpoint was returning HTTP 500, so the enzyme-level record could not be verified at all. Docking was therefore skipped rather than faked on a surrogate, and the pipeline's empty bioactivity result was not reported as a finding. What the paper delivers instead is a concrete, property-screened starting set: five PubChem analogues of atovaquone retaining the hydroxynaphthoquinone core, led by CID 71768, which matches atovaquone's polar pharmacophore exactly with a lower logP and zero Lipinski violations — with no measured potency attached to any of them, stated plainly. The measured record shows Y268S costs atovaquone 270-fold in Ki and up to ~9,000-fold in whole-parasite IC50, and shows the way out — the Qi site, where ELQ-300 keeps full potency and where dual-site combination cured murine malaria at a single 1 mg/kg dose. The next step is a validated Pf Qo-site model and a purified-enzyme assay of the five analogues against wild-type and Y268S bc1, with the results deposited where the record is currently unverifiable.

The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life, a maqsad of the Sharia): where a cheap, defined test can distinguish a usable drug from a useless one for a disease that killed 597,000 people in 2023, running the test is the obligation; prescribing on an untested surrogate score would violate it. The evidence here is thin — a property-screened analogue set, not a drug — so the recommendation is "run the trial", not "do the policy".
