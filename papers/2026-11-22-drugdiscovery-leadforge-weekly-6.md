## Abstract

We screened the bioactive chemical space of ChEMBL against seven therapeutically relevant targets in one week of automated LeadForge runs (16–22 Nov 2026): *Escherichia coli* DNA gyrase GyrB, HIV-1 protease, hepatitis C virus NS5B polymerase, *E. coli* cell-division protein FtsZ, *Plasmodium vivax* dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), SARS-CoV-2 papain-like protease (PLpro) and human PI3K-alpha (PIK3CA). For each target, the reference drug was recovered from live ChEMBL data (rediscovery check), 1–50 known ligands were similarity-screened (P. vivax DHFR: a single measured bioactivity in ChEMBL), leads were ranked on a composite of similarity, Lipinski/Veber drug-likeness, PAINS and kNN toxicity read-across, and the top five leads were docked with AutoDock Vina against the best available X-ray structure, with the reference drug re-docked as control. Two runs produced leads that docked clearly better than their reference drug: GyrB (four leads, best −8.06 vs −6.91 kcal/mol for novobiocin) and P. vivax DHFR (CHEMBL5270166, −9.33 vs −7.98 for pyrimethamine — a scaffold hop, Tanimoto 0.081, measured IC50 94 nM). Two runs were marginal wins inside Vina noise (HIV-1 protease, ~0.5 kcal/mol; FtsZ, 0.42–0.56 kcal/mol). Three runs were honest negatives on docking (NS5B, PLpro top-5, PIK3CA) — but the PLpro run still surfaced a genuine lead outside the ranked top-5: CHEMBL6151243, with a measured PLpro IC50 of 1.0 nM (~2000-fold more potent than GRL0617 in vitro) and a docking score 0.20 kcal/mol better than the GRL0617 re-dock. Two runs caught and corrected structure-selection errors before reporting (PLpro: Mpro-dominated auto-pick; PIK3CA: an HLA peptide-presentation structure auto-picked instead of the enzyme). All findings are computational triage; no compound is claimed as a drug, and wet-lab validation is required.

## Methods

**Pipeline (LeadForge, same flow for all seven runs).** (1) Target identification from UniProtKB + ChEMBL (live). (2) Known-ligand retrieval from ChEMBL bioactivities. (3) Query-compound verification via PubChem PUG REST. (4) Morgan-fingerprint similarity screen vs known actives. (5) ADMET + safety panel: RDKit descriptors (Lipinski, Veber), PAINS filter, ToxGuard kNN read-across over 12 Tox21-style assays. (6) Composite lead ranking (similarity 40 / Lipinski 20 / Veber 10 / PAINS 10 / tox 20). (7) Molecular docking: AutoDock Vina, exhaustiveness 8, 3 poses, 22.5 Å box centred on the co-crystallised ligand centroid of the best X-ray structure (resolution < 3.0 Å); grid centre verified as the largest drug-like HETATM group with cofactors, glycans, waters and ions excluded (centre-fix audit, Nov 2026), and the PDB domain checked against the reference drug's binding site (TYK2/RSV F/PLpro/PIK3CA corrections, Nov 2026). Reference drug re-docked in the same pocket as control. Vina noise is taken as ~±0.5–1 kcal/mol (up to ~2 kcal/mol in the literature); margins inside noise are reported as ties.

**Targets, references and structures.** GyrB / novobiocin (PDB 7P2M, 1.16 Å, E. coli GyrB24 + inhibitor LMD43/EBL2560); HIV-1 protease / darunavir (PDB 2HS1, 0.84 Å, V32I mutant + TMC114/darunavir); NS5B / sofosbuvir (PDB 3HKW, 1.55 Å, genotype 1a + 1,5-benzodiazepine inhibitor IX6 in the thumb allosteric pocket — not the polymerase active site); FtsZ / PC190723 (PDB 3VOB, 2.70 Å, *S. aureus* FtsZ + PC190723 — cross-species structure, no E. coli FtsZ co-crystal available); P. vivax DHFR / pyrimethamine (PDB 2BL9, 1.9 Å, P. vivax DHFR + pyrimethamine); PLpro / GRL0617 (PDB 7JRN, 2.48 Å, wild-type SARS-CoV-2 PLpro + GRL0617; auto-pick 7TX0 discarded as the NSP3 macrodomain); PIK3CA / alpelisib (PDB 4JPS, 2.20 Å, PI3Kα + alpelisib; auto-pick 9ASF discarded as an HLA-A\*03:01 peptide-presentation structure).

**Headline results.** 2 of 7 runs produced a clear docking win (GyrB, P. vivax DHFR); 2 were marginal wins inside noise (HIV-1 protease, FtsZ); 3 were negatives on docking (NS5B, PLpro top-5, PIK3CA). The strongest single result is the PLpro supplementary lead CHEMBL6151243: a measured 1.0 nM PLpro IC50 against GRL0617's ~2.05 µM — the week's only lead with a large measured-potency advantage over its reference, though its 0.20 kcal/mol docking edge is inside noise and it fails Veber (TPSA 193.86 Å²). The P. vivax DHFR result is the cleanest docking win: a 1.35 kcal/mol margin from a different chemotype with a measured 94 nM IC50, tempered by the thinnest data in the screen (one bioactivity). The GyrB win is the broadest (four leads) but the least diverse (one analogue family).

## Discussion

The week's pattern sharpens the screen's known behaviour: docking wins are cheap inside a reference drug's own chemotype family (GyrB, PIK3CA, HIV-1 protease), and the interesting hits are the ones that leave it (P. vivax DHFR's scaffold hop, PLpro's CHEMBL6151243). Two runs show the screen's failure modes honestly rather than hiding them: NS5B, where the only available co-crystal puts the box in the wrong pocket for the reference drug's mechanism, and P. vivax DHFR, where the screen is a single-compound comparison because the target has almost no measured data. The week's methodological point is structure selection: two of seven auto-picks were wrong in ways that would have silently corrupted the comparison (PLpro's macrodomain structure, PIK3CA's HLA peptide complex), and both were caught by the title/domain audit before the report was written — the audit is now the step that most often saves a run. The three negatives are reported as results, not failures: PIK3CA shows the screen can say no even when every lead is sub-nanomolar by measurement, because docking and measured potency are different evidence classes; NS5B shows it can say no when the pocket itself is wrong for the question; PLpro shows it can say no for the ranked list while still finding the real lead one rank down, which is why the supplementary-compound check matters.

**Limitations.** (i) All scores are computational; no wet-lab data were generated. (ii) Docking used rigid receptors and single pockets; covalent mechanisms and induced fit are not modelled. (iii) Measured potencies come from heterogeneous ChEMBL assays and are not head-to-head comparisons — most clearly in PIK3CA, where sub-nanomolar leads and the reference record come from different assays, and in PLpro, where the 1.0 nM IC50 and the 2.05 µM GRL0617 figure come from different assay setups. (iv) Similarity-anchored screening limits chemical diversity by design. (v) ToxGuard is kNN read-across, not a safety verdict. (vi) Mutant targets were not modelled (GyrB resistance, FtsZ, HIV-1 protease V32I is the structure but not the screen's target, PIK3CA hotspot mutants H1047R/E542K/E545K, PLpro host-DUB selectivity). (vii) FtsZ docking used a cross-species (*S. aureus*) structure; NS5B docking used the thumb pocket, not the active site; P. vivax DHFR conclusions rest on a single bioactivity.
