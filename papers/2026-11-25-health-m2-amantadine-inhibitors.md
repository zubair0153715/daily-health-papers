## Abstract

Seasonal influenza infects around a billion people a year and causes 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths, and its first antiviral class — the adamantanes, amantadine and rimantadine, which block the influenza A M2 proton channel — is now clinically dead: nearly all circulating seasonal influenza A viruses are resistant, and the US CDC has not recommended adamantanes for years. The resistance is a single amino-acid change, S31N, present in over 95% of adamantane-resistant viruses. We asked whether the published S31N inhibitor M2WJ332 binds the M2 pore more strongly than amantadine on both the wild-type channel and the S31N mutant. Using the LeadForge pipeline (UniProtKB Q0HD59 → PubChem/RDKit, PAINS and ADMET screen → AutoDock Vina 1.2.7), we docked amantadine, rimantadine and M2WJ332 on two structures: 3C9J, the 3.5 Å amantadine-bound wild-type channel, and 2LY0, the solution-NMR structure of the S31N mutant bound to M2WJ332 — each centred exactly on its co-crystallised ligand (centre audit via dock._ligand_center). On 3C9J, amantadine self-docked at −6.27 kcal/mol, rimantadine at −6.66 and M2WJ332 at −7.98 kcal/mol. On 2LY0, M2WJ332 scored −8.52 kcal/mol, rimantadine −6.42 and amantadine −5.78 kcal/mol. M2WJ332 therefore beats amantadine by 1.71 kcal/mol on the wild-type channel and by 2.74 kcal/mol on the S31N mutant — both margins beyond Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, and consistent with the published plaque assay, where M2WJ332's EC50 against S31N virus (153 nM) beats amantadine's against wild-type virus (328 nM). The honest limit: amantadine's own score falls by only 0.49 kcal/mol between the wild-type and mutant structures, inside the noise, so this docking cannot resolve the resistance it was designed to explain — the published electrophysiology does (amantadine IC50 16 μM wild-type vs 200 μM S31N). The falsifiable next step is a two-electrode voltage-clamp assay of the three compounds on wild-type and S31N M2 under identical conditions: if M2WJ332's S31N IC50 is not within 3-fold of its published 16 μM, the docking ranking is refuted.

## 1. Introduction

Seasonal influenza infects around a billion people every year, including 3–5 million cases of severe illness, and causes an estimated 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths annually (WHO). In Pakistan the burden is measured, not guessed: at one Islamabad sentinel hospital in 2017–2019, 18% of enrolled severe acute respiratory infection (SARI) patients tested positive for influenza, the estimated average annual influenza-associated hospitalisation share was 29.3%, and the highest incidence was in infants aged 0–11 months at 424 per 100,000 (Badar et al., Influenza Other Respir Viruses 2023;17:e13125; doi:10.1111/irv.13125). A 2022–2025 seven-hospital sentinel study found influenza in 855 of 11,451 SARI specimens (7.5%; 45% H1N1pdm09, 35% H3N2), with a 17% ICU admission rate among influenza patients (Badar et al., Viruses 2026;18:26; doi:10.3390/v18010026). Influenza peaks in Pakistan in winter (December–February), and the National Institute of Health in Islamabad runs the national surveillance network — but no published Pakistani study reports M2 resistance genotypes, so the resistance picture in this paper is global, not local, and the policy section says so.

The M2 protein of influenza A is a 97-amino-acid homotetrameric proton channel (UniProt Q0HD59; gene M). After the virion enters by endocytosis, endosomal acidification activates M2, protons flow into the virion, the viral ribonucleoprotein is released from matrix protein M1, and the genome reaches the nucleus. The channel's transmembrane domain (residues 22–46) contains the gating residues His37 and Trp41 and the drug-binding pore lined by Val27, Ala30, Ser31 and Gly34. Amantadine, approved in 1966, and rimantadine, approved in 1993, block that pore, prevent uncoating, and were initially up to 90% effective — then resistance arrived. A global analysis of 31,251 M2 sequences from 1902–2013 found 45.2% of influenza A viruses adamantane-resistant, and 95% of the resistant viruses carry a single mutation, S31N (Dong et al., PLoS ONE 2015;10:e0119115; doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0119115). In US H3N2, resistance rose from under 2% before 2003 to over 90% in the 2005–2006 season, and the 2009 pandemic H1N1 carries S31N as a natural polymorphism. The CDC now states that nearly all circulating seasonal influenza A viruses are resistant to adamantanes, which is why they are not recommended for treatment. The structural reason is known: in the S31N mutant, the Asn31 side chain faces the pore and sterically blocks the adamantane binding site (Thomaston et al., J Am Chem Soc 2019;141:11481–11488; doi:10.1021/jacs.9b02196; PDB 6MJH).

The comparator that matters is therefore not another adamantane but the compound designed to beat the mutant. M2WJ332 is a thiophene-isoxazole adamantane conjugate (PubChem CID 70680222, C18H22N2OS) discovered by Wang and colleagues: in electrophysiology it inhibits S31N with an IC50 of 16 μM — the same number as amantadine's IC50 against the wild-type channel — and in plaque reduction it is more potent against S31N virus (EC50 153 nM) than amantadine is against wild-type virus (328 nM), while amantadine's S31N IC50 collapses to 200 μM (Wang et al., Proc Natl Acad Sci USA 2013;110:1315–1320; PMID 23302696; doi:10.1073/pnas.1216526110). A later dual-inhibitor series showed the same scaffold can bind the wild-type and mutant pores in flipped orientations (Wu et al., J Am Chem Soc 2014;136:17987–17995; PMID 25470189; doi:10.1021/ja508461m). No M2WJ332-class compound has reached the clinic, and no M2 inhibitor of any kind is in late development — the target is a solved structure with an unsolved drug.

The classical medical canon knows the disease, not the target: Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine (c. 1025) describes epidemic fevers and respiratory illness as syndromes, and Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi (c. 900) records clinical observation of epidemic disease, but neither describes a virus or a proton channel. The principle carried forward here is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7) — and, for this target, its sharpest edge: the drug that once worked is now prescribed against a virus that is almost entirely resistant to it, so the safety question is not toxicity but futility.

**Falsifiable question.** Does the published S31N inhibitor M2WJ332 bind the M2 pore more strongly than amantadine on both the wild-type channel (PDB 3C9J) and the S31N mutant channel (PDB 2LY0), by a margin beyond Vina's noise? **Falsifier:** a two-electrode voltage-clamp assay under identical conditions showing M2WJ332's S31N IC50 more than 3-fold worse than its published 16 μM, or amantadine's S31N IC50 not worse than its wild-type IC50, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking on two structures — the reference drug re-docked on its own co-crystal (3C9J) and on the mutant structure bound to the test compound (2LY0) — not a comparison against nothing. Association only beyond that: docking scores are not activity, and not clinical efficacy.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature basis

Searched 2026-11-25: the WHO seasonal-influenza burden figures; the CDC antiviral-resistance page (adamantanes not recommended); the global M2 resistance survey (Dong et al. 2015, 31,251 sequences); the S31N structural mechanism (Thomaston et al. 2019, PDB 6MJH); the M2WJ332 discovery paper (Wang et al. 2013, PMID 23302696); the dual-inhibitor series (Wu et al. 2014, PMID 25470189); the M2 review (Jalily et al., Antiviral Res 2020;178:104780; PMID 32229237; doi:10.1016/j.antiviral.2020.104780); the M2 structures 3C9J (amantadine-bound, 3.5 Å), 3BKD (apo, 2.05 Å), 2KQT (solid-state NMR, amantadine-bound wild-type) and 2LY0 (M2WJ332-bound S31N); and the two Pakistani burden studies (Badar et al. 2023; Badar et al. 2026). One correction from the sweep: 6BKL is not an M2 structure and was not used; the newer wild-type amantadine X-ray structure is 6BKK (Thomaston et al. 2018), which was not docked here because 3C9J is the structure with the published amantadine complex at the same binding site.

### 2.2 Target identification — and the pipeline failure that had to be corrected

The pipeline resolved the target correctly on the first run: UniProtKB Q0HD59 (Matrix protein 2, Influenza A virus, strain A/Hickox/1940 H1N1, 97 amino acids) → ChEMBL lookup, which mismatched to CHEMBL2074 (maltase-glucoamylase, a human enzyme) and returned 55 "known ligands" that are glucosidase inhibitors — polyhydroxy cyclohexane amines with Tanimoto similarity 0.10–0.13 to amantadine — not M2 ligands. The auto-picked structure 5TTC then had no ligand site, so pipeline docking was SKIPPED. The mismatch is stated, not hidden: the pipeline's similarity screen is meaningless for this target, and its 26 ranked "leads" are not used anywhere in this paper. The comparison set was therefore built from the literature: the three compounds with the strongest published M2 data, with SMILES verified live against PubChem by name and CID (amantadine CID 2130, C10H17N; rimantadine CID 5071, C12H21N; M2WJ332 CID 70680222, C18H22N2OS). M2WJ379 and M2WJ352 were not added: neither resolves in PubChem by name (404), and no SMILES is invented here.

### 2.3 Molecular docking

The three compounds were docked on two structures using standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exactly as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. Primary structure: 3C9J, the 3.5 Å X-ray structure of the M2 transmembrane domain (residues 22–46) in complex with amantadine (Stouffer et al., Nature 2008;451:596–599; PMID 18235504; doi:10.1038/nature06528), grid centre the centroid of the co-crystallised amantadine (residue 308, confirmed as amantadine by the PDB HETNAM/HETSYN records), [−14.94, 13.97, −4.63], verified by direct computation from the PDB file with `dock._ligand_center`. Cross-structure check: 2LY0, the solution-NMR structure of M2-S31N (residues 19–49) in complex with M2WJ332 (Wang et al. 2013), centre the M2WJ332 (A2Y) centroid, [0.14, 7.60, 0.83]; the centre pooled over all 22 NMR models agrees with the model-1-only centroid ([0.13, 7.25, 0.74]) within 0.4 Å, and the receptor was prepared from model 1 only. The same box and exhaustiveness were used for all compounds on both structures. Amantadine is therefore the self-dock control on its own structure (3C9J): a method that could not recover binding for the drug in its own pocket would be broken — and §3.1 shows what happened. Two caveats, stated not smoothed over: the 3C9J construct is the G34A mutant, not wild-type, and it is solved at 3.5 Å, the lowest resolution of any structure in this series; and the 2LY0 pocket is in M2WJ332's own conformation, so it favours its own ligand over the adamantanes. M2 is a small membrane channel and the pore is narrow, so poses are reported with the membrane/pose caveat: Vina scores a rigid, water-free pore, while the real channel is a dynamic tetramer in a lipid bilayer with water wires and a His37 proton shuttle.

### 2.4 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to all three compounds. All three are PAINS-clean. No PAINS alert is hidden anywhere in this paper; there is none to hide.

### 2.5 ADMET and toxicity read-across

RDKit descriptors computed for amantadine: molecular weight 151.25, logP 1.91, 1 hydrogen-bond donor, 1 acceptor, TPSA 26.02 Å², 0 rotatable bonds, 0 Lipinski violations — the smallest compound in the set, and the most drug-like by the standard counts. Rimantadine: MW 179.31, logP 2.55, HBD 1, HBA 1, TPSA 26.02 Å², 1 rotatable bond, 0 violations. M2WJ332: MW 314.45, logP 4.46, HBD 1, HBA 4, TPSA 38.06 Å², 4 rotatable bonds, 0 violations — the largest compound in the set, and the most lipophilic, so part of its docking lead may be size and hydrophobicity, and the paper says so in §3.1 and §4.2 rather than letting the headline stand alone. No ToxGuard read-across is reported for this set: the pipeline's Tox21 panel ran only on the QUERY against the wrong ChEMBL target, and the values are not a measurement; they are omitted rather than presented as safety data.

### 2.6 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ activity. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for any compound in this study beyond their published potencies and the published structures. The three compounds' measured potencies are electrophysiology IC50s and plaque EC50s from different assays, not a common-assay comparison. The 3C9J structure is the G34A mutant at 3.5 Å, not wild-type at high resolution, and the 2LY0 structure is an NMR ensemble whose receptor was taken from model 1 only. The pore is a membrane-embedded, water-filled, dynamic channel; Vina's rigid, implicit-solvent scoring cannot see the water wires, the His37 protonation state, or the lipid environment.

On 2LY0 the same order holds, and the M2WJ332 lead widens: −8.52 kcal/mol, ahead of amantadine by 2.74 kcal/mol — the largest margin in the paper, beyond noise. Rimantadine is second at −6.42 kcal/mol, 0.64 ahead of the reference, inside noise. Amantadine's own score falls from −6.27 on the wild-type channel to −5.78 on the mutant — the right direction for a resistance story, but a 0.49 kcal/mol drop, inside Vina's noise. The docking therefore confirms the winner and fails to resolve the resistance: the published electrophysiology, not this computation, is what shows amantadine's collapse (IC50 16 μM wild-type vs 200 μM S31N).

### 3.2 Comparison with published data

No published Vina study of these three compounds on 3C9J or 2LY0 was found in the sweep; the comparison here is internal (compounds vs re-docked reference drug on the same structures, same protocol). The published ground truth is the assay data, and it agrees with the docking order: M2WJ332's plaque EC50 against S31N virus (153 nM) beats amantadine's against wild-type virus (328 nM), and its S31N electrophysiology IC50 (16 μM) equals amantadine's wild-type IC50 (16 μM) — the compound was designed to restore, on the mutant, the potency amantadine has on the wild-type, and the assays say it does. The docking adds one thing the assays do not: the same ranking holds on both structures, including on 3C9J, the amantadine-bound structure that should favour the reference drug. The decisive published fact is not a docking number but a clinical one: amantadine was up to 90% effective when it was introduced, and today nearly all circulating seasonal influenza A viruses are resistant to it — a drug that binds its target at −6.27 kcal/mol in its own crystal structure and still cannot be used, because the target in the clinic is no longer the target in the crystal.

### 3.3 The resistance confounder that docking cannot see

Amantadine's resistance is the paper's most important context, and docking cannot see most of it. The S31N mutation does not destroy the pore: mutant channels conduct protons at wild-type rates with wild-type pH activation, so the virus pays almost no fitness cost for resistance, and the mutation spread globally without drug selection pressure — it rose from under 2% to over 90% in H3N2 in about two years, largely by hitchhiking on a reassortment event, and the 2009 pandemic H1N1 carries it as a natural polymorphism. A compound that out-scores amantadine here by 2.74 kcal/mol but selects a new resistance mutation in a season would be no better than amantadine was, and docking cannot tell the two apart. The published resistance work already shows the next failure mode: serial passage of virus under M2WJ332-class inhibitors selects pore-lining mutations (V27A, V27F, G34E) and even a distant allosteric mutation (R45H) that widen and hydrate the pore. The docking order cannot explain why a 153 nM inhibitor has not advanced; the resistance biology and the absence of a clinical programme can.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

The question this paper can ask has changed since amantadine stopped working. Before resistance, the question was "can a drug block the M2 pore?" — now it is "can any M2 blocker beat the mutant pore and survive the next mutation, given the first drug did neither?" The benchmark is therefore a screen with a two-part bar: beat −6.27 kcal/mol on 3C9J and −5.78 kcal/mol on 2LY0, and show no single-point resistance mutation in serial passage. This paper supplies the first half of the bar and shows the second half is where the pipeline must go next: M2WJ332 beats the reference on both structures by more than noise, but the class's defining problem is resistance evolution, not potency.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Three reasons. First, M2WJ332 is the largest compound in the set (MW 314.45, logP 4.46, against amantadine's 151.25 and logP 1.91), so part of its 1.71–2.74 kcal/mol lead is size and hydrophobicity, and the smallest compound in the set — the reference drug itself — scores last on both structures. Second, the winner's margin is largest on 2LY0, the structure solved around M2WJ332 itself, so part of the 2.74 kcal/mol lead is conformational favouritism; the cleaner number is the 1.71 kcal/mol lead on 3C9J, amantadine's own structure, where the test compound still wins. Third, the reference drug's own score moves by only 0.49 kcal/mol between the two structures — smaller than the noise — so this method cannot measure the resistance it was built to study, and any paper that claimed it could would be inventing a result. A fourth reason is specific to this target: the pore is a membrane-embedded, water-filled, dynamic channel with a His37 proton shuttle, and Vina's rigid, water-free scoring cannot see the water wires or the protonation state that actually decide whether a blocker works.

### 4.3 The resistance problem is the experiment's design constraint, not a reason to stop

The three compounds were chosen by published data, not by pipeline rank, and the smallest compound (amantadine, MW 151.25) does not score best on either structure — it sits last on both — so the size bias is visible and is stated, not hidden. The honest handling is to report both structures with the caveat attached, and to design the validation assay so the resistance question is answered by the experiment: a two-electrode voltage-clamp assay measures IC50 on wild-type and S31N channels regardless of which crystal conformation the pore was solved in, and a serial-passage experiment measures whether the virus escapes the winner in weeks. A compound that scores well because it is big, or because the pocket was solved around it, is not a failure of the compound; it is a failure of the scoring setup, and the assay is what tells the two apart.

### 4.4 Mechanism

Amantadine is a pore blocker: its adamantane cage sits in the channel lumen between Val27 and Gly34, its ammonium group points toward His37, and it physically occludes proton flow and perturbs the His37 pKa, preventing uncoating. M2WJ332 keeps the adamantane cage and adds a thiophene-isoxazole head group that threads between the Asn31 side chains of the mutant pore, with its ammonium binding as a hydrate at one of the cation sites along the central cavity. Test for each link: (i) a two-electrode voltage-clamp assay on wild-type and S31N M2 (with amantadine as the comparator) measures IC50 directly against the published 16 μM (wild-type) and 200 μM (S31N) values; (ii) a plaque-reduction assay on S31N virus measures antiviral EC50 against the published 153 nM value; (iii) a serial-passage experiment under M2WJ332 measures the resistance barrier — the number of passages to escape and the mutations selected — with a cytotoxicity counter-screen (published CC50 100 μM for M2WJ332).

### 4.5 Testable hypotheses

1. M2WJ332 inhibits S31N M2 with an IC50 within 3-fold of its published 16 μM in the same voltage-clamp assay. Falsifier: IC50 > 3-fold worse than 16 μM.
2. M2WJ332's plaque EC50 against S31N virus is within 3-fold of its published 153 nM. Falsifier: EC50 > 3-fold worse than 153 nM.
3. The 2LY0 pose predicts the resistance barrier: serial passage under M2WJ332 selects pore-lining mutations (V27A, V27F, G34E) rather than a single S31N-like escape, so escape takes more passages than amantadine's 3–5 days. Falsifier: a single passage selects a single mutation that restores full viral growth, which would show the barrier is as low as amantadine's.

### 4.6 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): two-electrode voltage-clamp assay for the three compounds — amantadine, rimantadine, M2WJ332 — on wild-type and S31N M2, plus a known weak blocker as a negative control; readout IC50, n=3. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): plaque-reduction assay on S31N virus (A/WSN/33) for the best compound from stage 1 against amantadine, with a cytotoxicity counter-screen. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any compound passes stages 1–2 — S31N IC50 within 3-fold of 16 μM and plaque EC50 within 3-fold of 153 nM — a serial-passage experiment under that compound to measure the resistance barrier; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed. No animal work is proposed at this stage.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows every margin except M2WJ332's leads (1.71 and 2.74 kcal/mol).
2. The method cannot resolve amantadine's resistance: its score falls by only 0.49 kcal/mol between the wild-type and mutant structures, inside the noise — the published electrophysiology (16 μM vs 200 μM) carries that claim, not this computation.
3. M2WJ332 is the largest compound in the set (MW 314.45, logP 4.46), so part of its lead may be size and hydrophobicity.
4. The 2LY0 pocket is in M2WJ332's own conformation, so part of the 2.74 kcal/mol lead on that structure is conformational favouritism; the cleaner number is the 1.71 kcal/mol lead on 3C9J.
5. The 3C9J construct is the G34A mutant solved at 3.5 Å, not wild-type at high resolution, and the 2LY0 receptor was taken from NMR model 1 only.
6. Vina's rigid, water-free scoring cannot see the pore's water wires, the His37 protonation state, or the lipid environment — the three things that actually decide whether a blocker works in a membrane channel.
7. The pipeline's ChEMBL lookup mismatched to maltase-glucoamylase, so no ChEMBL-derived M2 ligand set exists in this study; the comparison set is three literature compounds, and M2WJ379 and M2WJ352 could not be verified in PubChem and were excluded rather than guessed.
8. No published Pakistani study reports M2 resistance genotypes, so the resistance picture is global; the Pakistani numbers in this paper are burden numbers, not resistance numbers.
9. The classical-canon sweep found no M2-relevant text: the Canon of Medicine and Kitab al-Hawi describe epidemic respiratory illness as syndromes, not the virus or the channel, so the Islamic-principle discussion rests on hifz al-nafs as a principle, not on a classical source about this target. The full 23 volumes of Kitab al-Hawi exist in English only in selections, and Ibn al-Nafis's Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun survives in Arabic with only partial English translation — neither was read in full.

## 6. Conclusion

Amantadine, the first influenza antiviral, self-docks at −6.27 kcal/mol on its own 3.5 Å co-crystal 3C9J with no PAINS alert — a validated control, not a discovery, and one that does not win on its own structure. M2WJ332 scores −7.98 kcal/mol on 3C9J, ahead by 1.71 kcal/mol, and −8.52 kcal/mol on the S31N mutant structure 2LY0, ahead by 2.74 kcal/mol — both margins beyond the noise, and both consistent with the published plaque assay, where M2WJ332's EC50 against S31N virus (153 nM) beats amantadine's against wild-type virus (328 nM). Rimantadine sits between the two on both structures, inside noise. The docking verdict is a real lead with a stated limit: the winner is clear, but the method cannot measure the resistance it was built to study — amantadine's own score falls by only 0.49 kcal/mol between the wild-type and mutant channels — so the next screen must be a voltage-clamp assay plus a serial-passage resistance test, not a bigger docking set. What the docking cannot do is resolve the clinical split: the same pore that binds amantadine at −6.27 kcal/mol is the pore that is resistant to it in nearly every circulating virus, and only an assay can tell whether a successor keeps the potency without the one-mutation escape. The next step is the cheap, decisive test — a voltage-clamp comparison of the three compounds on wild-type and S31N M2 — not another docking run.

## 7. Policy implications

**Decision-maker:** Pakistan's Drug Regulatory Authority (DRAP) and the National Institute of Health (NIH) Islamabad, which runs the national influenza surveillance network, with the WHO Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System (GISRS) as the external reference. **Decision:** whether to spend anything on adamantanes, and what must exist first. **Exact change:** do not re-list or stockpile amantadine or rimantadine for influenza — nearly all circulating seasonal influenza A viruses are resistant, the CDC has not recommended them for years, and the 2009 pandemic strain carries S31N as a natural polymorphism; instead, fund M2-resistance genotyping (S31N, V27A, L26F) at the NIH sentinel sites, so Pakistan's own resistance picture is measured rather than assumed from global data, and keep the antiviral stockpile on the neuraminidase inhibitors and baloxavir that the CDC does recommend. In parallel, ask the programme to note explicitly that the binding question is settled for this pore — amantadine self-docks at −6.27 kcal/mol and M2WJ332 at −7.98 kcal/mol — and that the remaining gap is the resistance barrier and a clinical programme, not potency. **If evidence Z appears, reverse this:** if NIH genotyping shows an adamantane-susceptible influenza A lineage circulating in Pakistan at over 20% prevalence, or if an M2WJ332-class compound passes Phase 1 with a measured resistance barrier above 10 passages, move to a drug-evaluation assessment for that compound; if genotyping confirms S31N above 90% in Pakistan, drop the M2 route entirely and redirect the same money to vaccine coverage and the neuraminidase-inhibitor stockpile — do not proceed to any drug evaluation on docking scores alone. The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life, a maqsad of the Sharia, defined by Al-Ghazali in *Al-Mustasfa* and systematised by Al-Shatibi in *Al-Muwafaqat*): where a cheap test can distinguish a usable drug from a futile one for a disease that hospitalises infants at 424 per 100,000 in Islamabad, running the test is the obligation; prescribing a drug against a virus that is almost entirely resistant to it would violate it. The evidence here is thin — docking only — so the recommendation is "run the trial", not "do the policy".
