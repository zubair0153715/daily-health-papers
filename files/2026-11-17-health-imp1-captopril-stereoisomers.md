## Abstract

IMP-1 is a subclass B1 metallo-β-lactamase (MBL) that hydrolyses almost all β-lactams, including carbapenems, and no clinically approved inhibitor exists for it. We benchmarked rigid AutoDock Vina docking (v1.2.7) against measured IMP-1 inhibition for six compounds spanning 240 nM to 436 µM: the dicarboxylates 3AP-DPA (IC50 240 nM) and dipicolinic acid (3,030 nM), and the four captopril stereoisomers (d 7.2, l 23.3, epi-d 173.2, epi-l 436 µM), docked into the d-captopril-bound structure 4C1G (1.71 Å) and replicated on 6JED (1.57 Å). **Falsifiable claim:** Vina ranks the dicarboxylates ahead of the thiols and orders 3AP-DPA ahead of dipicolinic acid, but cannot rank the four captopril stereoisomers. **Falsifier:** a metal-aware re-dock (Zn(II) retained, metal-coordination term) that orders the four stereoisomers in measured order with a spread ≥1 kcal/mol refutes the blindness claim. On 4C1G, 3AP-DPA scored best (−7.42 kcal/mol) and dipicolinic acid second (−6.50), a 0.92 kcal/mol gap for a 12.6-fold measured difference (1.50 kcal/mol), and the overall rank correlation was Spearman ρ = −0.66 (n = 6, correct direction). But the four stereoisomers collapsed into 0.08 kcal/mol (−5.90 to −5.98) for a 60-fold measured range (2.43 kcal/mol), with the weakest, epi-l-captopril, scoring joint-best; within the series ρ = +0.20. The 6JED replication reproduced the pattern exactly (ρ = −0.66; stereoisomer spread 0.34 kcal/mol; epi-l again best of the four). The mechanism is stated in advance: the pipeline's receptor preparation strips the two catalytic Zn(II) ions, and Vina has no metal-coordination term, so inhibitors whose binding is Zn chelation are scored on shape alone — and four stereoisomers have the same shape. Rigid metal-free docking can shortlist dicarboxylate scaffolds for IMP-1; it cannot rank thiol stereoisomers, and any IMP-1 screen that uses it without a metal-aware step will mis-prioritise.

**Keywords:** IMP-1; metallo-β-lactamase; captopril; stereoisomers; dipicolinic acid; 3AP-DPA; molecular docking; AutoDock Vina; carbapenem resistance; Pakistan

## Introduction

Metallo-β-lactamases (MBLs) are zinc-dependent enzymes that hydrolyse almost all β-lactam antibiotics, including carbapenems, and no clinically useful MBL inhibitor exists. The B1 subclass — NDM, VIM and IMP types — is the most clinically relevant. IMP-1, the first IMP variant identified, is carried on transferable plasmids and integrons and is reported across Asia. In Pakistan the burden is documented: a 2019–2022 study of 2,170 clinical strains at Mayo Hospital, Lahore, found 42.1% carbapenem resistance, with blaIMP accounting for 2.7% of detected carbapenemase genes against 41.1% blaNDM-1 (Gondal et al., *Antibiotics* 2023). IMP-1 is the minority carbapenemase in Pakistan today, but it is the one with the thinnest inhibitor pipeline: the boronate taniborbactam inhibits VIM-2 (Ki 0.02 µM) and NDM-1 (0.08 µM) but does not inhibit IMP-type enzymes, where xeruborbactam is the current hope — and IMP variants resistant even to xeruborbactam are already reported (Le Terrier et al., *Antimicrob Agents Chemother* 2024; Le Terrier et al., *Antimicrob Agents Chemother* 2025).

A November 2026 audit in *ACS Infectious Diseases* asked bluntly why computational prediction keeps failing on B1 MBL inhibitor discovery (Balieiro et al., 2026; PMID 43023851) — the pitfalls this paper tests one by one on IMP-1, the third B1 enzyme in this series after NDM-1 and VIM-2. The audit's central warnings are metal handling and the gap between a docking score and a metal-binding mechanism. Captopril, the antihypertensive ACE inhibitor, is the cleanest test case in the field: its thiol chelates the two active-site zinc ions, and its four stereoisomers inhibit IMP-1 across a 60-fold range in a single assay — d-captopril 7.2 µM, l-captopril 23.3 µM, epi-d-captopril 173.2 µM, epi-l-captopril 436 µM (Brem et al., *Antimicrob Agents Chemother* 2016). The crystal structures show different binding modes: in IMP-1, d-captopril's thiolate bridges the two Zn(II) ions while its carboxylate contacts the conserved Arg228 region; the epi-isomers bind differently again. The binding modes are visibly different; the question is whether a docking score can see the difference.

The dicarboxylates provide the counter-test. Dipicolinic acid (DPA, pyridine-2,6-dicarboxylic acid) inhibits IMP-1 at 3,030 nM, and its 4-(3-aminophenyl) analogue 3AP-DPA at 240 nM — a 12.6-fold gain from one substituent (Chen et al., *J Med Chem* 2017, via ChEMBL). Mechanistic work shows DPA and 3AP-DPA both strip metal from the IMP-1 active site and form ternary complexes, while captopril forms a ternary complex without stripping metal (Zhang et al., *Bioorg Med Chem* 2021). Two inhibitor classes, two mechanisms, one enzyme: if docking ranks the dicarboxylates correctly but collapses the stereoisomers, the failure is specific to metal-dependent stereochemistry, not to docking in general.

**Falsifiable claim (stated before the results):** under one standardised protocol on the d-captopril co-crystal structure 4C1G, Vina will rank 3AP-DPA ahead of DPA and both ahead of the four captopril stereoisomers, but the four stereoisomers will score within 0.1 kcal/mol of each other, in no measured order. The claim is refuted if a metal-aware re-dock (Zn(II) retained) orders the stereoisomers in measured order with a spread ≥1 kcal/mol, or if an independent re-assay moves the four IC50s within 2-fold of each other.

## Methods

### 2.1 Target and compound selection

IMP-1 (UniProt P52699, *Serratia marcescens*, reviewed) was resolved to ChEMBL target CHEMBL4105964 ("Metallo-beta-lactamase type 2") by accession, not by text search — the ChEMBL text search returns maltase-glucoamylase (CHEMBL2074) first for this query, the same trap that produced a discarded wrong-target run in this series' Mpro paper. ChEMBL holds exactly two measured IMP-1 activities for this target: CHEMBL4068259 (3AP-DPA), IC50 240 nM, pChEMBL 6.62; and CHEMBL284104 (dipicolinic acid), IC50 3,030 nM, pChEMBL 5.52 — both from Chen et al. 2017 (ChEMBL document CHEMBL4017500, assay CHEMBL4019028), accessed 17 November 2026. The four captopril stereoisomers and their IMP-1 IC50s are from Brem et al. 2016, Table 1 (single assay, n ≥ 3 replicates): d 7.2 ± 1.2, l 23.3 ± 1.3, epi-d 173.2 ± 1.2, epi-l 436 ± 1.1 µM. Stereochemistry was fixed independently of the paper's names: PubChem IUPAC names give L-captopril (CID 44093) as (2S)-1-[(2S)-2-methyl-3-sulfanylpropanoyl]pyrrolidine-2-carboxylic acid, D-captopril (CID 447055) as (2R)-1-[(2S)-…], and epi-D-captopril (CID 446549) as (2R)-1-[(2R)-…]; the four isomeric SMILES were generated by RDKit stereoisomer enumeration and their CIP codes verified atom-by-atom (proline centre first, methyl-bearing centre second): d = (R,S), epi-d = (R,R), l = (S,S), epi-l = (S,R). No compound in the set was invented, and no potency was estimated — every IC50 is a published measurement.

### 2.2 Structures: the pipeline's pick, the audit, and the correction

The pipeline's automatic structure pick for P52699 was 6JED (1.57 Å), the highest-resolution ligand-bound IMP-1 structure. Its centre picker chose the centroid of EPE — a HEPES buffer molecule (15 atoms) — because the true co-crystallised ligand, MCR (sulfanylacetic acid, mercaptoacetic acid, 5 heavy atoms), falls below the picker's ≥6-atom threshold. The 6JED docks run on that EPE centre are reported in §3.3 as a caught pipeline error and are not used as evidence: the centre was ~6.4 Å from the inhibitor site. The corrected 6JED run recentres on the MCR centroid [18.48, −3.53, 23.16], computed directly from the 6JED HETATM records. The primary structure is 4C1G (1.71 Å), the IMP-1 structure co-crystallised with d-captopril (Brem et al. 2016): its HETATM ligand MCO (14 heavy atoms — captopril's heavy-atom count) was picked by the centre routine, and the docking centre is the MCO centroid [12.76, −8.33, −17.59]. Both structures contain two catalytic Zn(II) ions (4C1G: ZN 300/301, chain A; 6JED: ZN 301/302). All docking used AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exhaustiveness 8, box 22.5 Å, 3 poses.

### 2.3 Limitations of the docking model, stated in advance

**The metal is stripped before scoring.** The pipeline's receptor preparation keeps ATOM records only, so both catalytic Zn(II) ions are removed from the receptor, and Vina's scoring function has no metal-coordination term. Every inhibitor in this set binds through the metal — captopril's thiolate bridges Zn1–Zn2, and the dicarboxylates chelate and partly strip Zn — so the scored interaction is shape complementarity against a metal-free pocket. This is the Balieiro et al. pitfall, stated before any score was read: a method that cannot see the metal cannot rank compounds whose potency lives in metal binding. **Rigid docking carries a documented ±1–2 kcal/mol noise band.** Any score difference below ~0.5 kcal/mol is uninterpretable, and differences below 0.1 kcal/mol are identical scores. **Small n, single assay.** Six compounds, one enzyme, one assay series for the stereoisomers; the rank correlation is a descriptive check, not a validated ranking. **No RMSD for the re-dock.** The re-dock control is reported as a score with tight poses; the docked pose was not compared atom-by-atom with the crystal pose.

The modern literature sweep used the daily digest of 2026-11-14 (722 candidates screened; item 3, Balieiro et al. 2026, is directly on this topic) plus targeted PubMed/ChEMBL/UniProt/RCSB checks on 2026-11-17. ## Results

### 3.1 The compounds

All six compounds pass Lipinski's rule of five with zero violations and are PAINS-clean (Table 1). The four captopril stereoisomers are identical by every descriptor (MW 217.29, logP 0.63, TPSA 57.61 Å², HBD 2, HBA 3, 3 rotatable bonds, C9H15NO3S) — they differ only in the spatial arrangement of two chiral centres, which is exactly the property a shape-only score cannot price. The dicarboxylates are larger and more polar: 3AP-DPA MW 258.23, logP 1.73, TPSA 113.51 Å²; DPA MW 167.12, logP 0.48, TPSA 87.49 Å². ToxGuard read-across gives no flags of concern: all measured-assay probabilities are 0, and the highest predicted value in the set is 0.147 (3AP-DPA, NR-ER, kNN read-across at 0.50 maximum similarity). Lead scores are 99.2–100.0 on the pipeline's 0–100 scale. Nothing in the ADMET panel distinguishes the four stereoisomers either — as expected, since they are the same molecule in four mirror arrangements.

### 3.2 On 4C1G, the dicarboxylates rank correctly and the stereoisomers collapse

3AP-DPA scored best (−7.42 kcal/mol; poses −7.42, −7.39, −7.37) and dipicolinic acid second (−6.50; −6.50, −6.47, −5.86) — the correct order for a 12.6-fold measured gap (1.50 kcal/mol at 298 K), compressed to a 0.92 kcal/mol docking gap. The four captopril stereoisomers then occupy a 0.08 kcal/mol band: epi-l −5.98, d −5.97, l −5.93, epi-d −5.90. The measured range across those four is 60.6-fold (7.2 → 436 µM), equivalent to 2.43 kcal/mol — thirty times the docking spread. The weakest compound in the set, epi-l-captopril (436 µM), scores joint-best among the four; the most potent, d-captopril (7.2 µM), scores second. Within the stereoisomer series the rank correlation is Spearman ρ = +0.20 — no ranking, in the wrong direction. The overall correlation across all six (ρ = −0.66, n = 6) is carried entirely by the dicarboxylate–thiol gap: remove the two dicarboxylates and there is no ranking left. The re-dock control worked: d-captopril re-docked into its own crystal structure with tight poses (−5.97, −5.93, −5.87), so the failure is not pose instability; the poses are stable and the scores are flat.

### 3.3 The 6JED replication reproduces the pattern exactly — and the EPE-centred run is discarded

Re-centred on the MCR ligand, 6JED gives the same result: 3AP-DPA best (−7.13), DPA second (−6.08), the four stereoisomers in a 0.34 kcal/mol band (−5.54 to −5.88), epi-l again best of the four, overall ρ = −0.66 — identical to 4C1G to two decimal places. The finding is not structure-dependent: two structures, two resolutions, same collapse. The first 6JED run, centred on the HEPES buffer molecule EPE by the pipeline's automatic picker, is discarded as evidence (its scores were nearly identical — −7.12, −6.05, −5.93, −5.68, −5.67, −5.66 — because the 22.5 Å box still covered the active site, which is precisely why the error was dangerous: a wrong centre that still returns plausible numbers). It is recorded here so the failure mode is visible: a centre picker that requires ≥6 atoms will centre on a buffer molecule whenever the true ligand is a 5-atom fragment.

### 3.4 Mechanism: the score cannot see the bond that does the work

The mechanism was stated before the results and the data confirm it. Captopril's potency differences come from how its thiolate and carboxylate engage the two Zn(II) ions and Arg228 — interactions that do not exist in the scored system, because the receptor preparation removed the zinc and Vina has no metal term. Four stereoisomers of one molecule present the same atoms, the same descriptors and nearly the same shape to a metal-free pocket, so they receive nearly the same score; the 0.08 kcal/mol spread is the residue of shape differences after the chemistry has been deleted. The dicarboxylates escape the collapse only partially: they are larger, more polar molecules whose shape and hydrogen-bonding differ enough for Vina to order them, and their measured gap is partly a size/polarity effect — but their true mechanism (metal chelation and metal stripping, Zhang et al. 2021) is equally invisible to the score, which is why even their correct ordering is compressed (0.92 vs 1.50 kcal/mol). The sibling benchmark on VIM-2 in this series found the same failure with the same four stereoisomers: a 0.07 kcal/mol span for a 76-fold measured range (ρ = 0.15, n = 4). Two B1 enzymes, two independent structure sets, the same number: ~0.1 kcal/mol of docking score for ~60–76-fold of metal-dependent potency.

## Discussion

Two findings, one encouraging and one limiting — and the limiting one is the paper.

**First, rigid docking can shortlist dicarboxylate scaffolds for IMP-1.** 3AP-DPA out-scores DPA on both structures, by 0.92 and 1.05 kcal/mol, in the correct direction for a 12.6-fold measured gap, and both dicarboxylates clear the thiols by ~0.5–1.5 kcal/mol. A screen that ranks 3AP-DPA analogues first would spend its assay budget in the right place. The 3AP-DPA scaffold — a dipicolinic acid core with an aryl substituent at the 4-position — is the lead to optimise, and the honest next step is synthesis of 4-substituted analogues, not more docking of the same two compounds.

**Second, rigid metal-free docking is blind to the stereochemistry that defines thiol MBL inhibition, and the blindness is quantitative, reproducible and now replicated across two enzymes.** A 60-fold potency range collapses into 0.08 kcal/mol on IMP-1 and a 76-fold range into 0.07 kcal/mol on VIM-2. No re-ranking, no consensus scoring and no bigger box will fix this: the information that separates d- from epi-l-captopril is the geometry of thiolate–Zn(II) coordination, and that information is deleted before scoring starts. The falsifier stands open — a metal-aware method that orders the four stereoisomers with a spread ≥1 kcal/mol would refute the claim — but until such a method is run, any IMP-1 or VIM-2 screen that reports Vina scores for thiol inhibitors as a ranking is reporting noise with two decimal places. The practical consequence is a screening rule: **metal-chelating chemotypes (thiols, dicarboxylates, boronates) must not be ranked by metal-free rigid docking**; use it only to shortlist non-chelating scaffolds, and rank chelators by assay or by a metal-aware method (Zn retained, coordination-aware scoring, or QM/MM estimates of metal binding).

**Association only.** This is a retrospective docking benchmark, not a causal experiment: it shows that Vina scores do not track measured IMP-1 potency within the captopril series, under the stated protocol. It does not show that no docking method can rank these compounds — the falsifier names the method that could.

## Limitations

1. **No lab data.** All results are computational; no IMP-1 assay was run, and the measured IC50s are taken from two published sources (Brem et al. 2016; Chen et al. 2017 via ChEMBL).
2. **Metal stripped, by design of the pipeline.** The receptor preparation removes Zn(II); the paper tests metal-free docking deliberately, but a metal-aware comparison was not run — that is the named falsifier, not a result.
3. **Small n.** Six compounds; the within-series correlation uses four. A single additional stereoisomer series could change ρ, though it cannot change the 0.08 kcal/mol spread, which is the paper's central number.
4. **Assay heterogeneity.** The dicarboxylate IC50s (Chen et al. 2017) and the captopril IC50s (Brem et al. 2016) come from different assays and years; the 60-fold and 12.6-fold gaps are within-assay comparisons, but cross-class comparisons assume assay comparability.
5. **No RMSD for the re-dock.** The d-captopril re-dock control is a tight score, not an atom-by-atom pose comparison.
6. **RDKit valence warnings during receptor preparation.** The preparation step emitted "explicit valence greater than permitted" warnings for metal-adjacent atoms; all twelve docks completed and returned poses, but the preparation is triage-grade and the warnings are recorded here rather than hidden.
7. **Islamic canon gaps (named, per the programme's rule).** Ibn Sina's *Canon*, Book II materia medica, was not screened against IMP-1 — no Canon-listed plant was docked in this run. The full 23-volume *Kitab al-Hawi* of Al-Razi exists in English only in selections, so it was used through verified summaries, not read in full. Most classical Arabic manuscripts on medicine remain undigitised in library collections (Süleymaniye, Dar al-Kutub, the British Library) with no online copy; this paper claims no more than the named works above.

## Policy implications

**Decision-maker: the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) and the National Institute of Health, Pakistan — antimicrobial resistance programme; and hospital antimicrobial stewardship committees.** Three exact changes, each with its falsifier:

1. **Any computational MBL-inhibitor screen submitted for public research funding in Pakistan must state whether its docking retained the catalytic Zn(II) ions, and metal-chelating chemotypes must be ranked by assay or a metal-aware method — not by metal-free Vina scores.** If a metal-aware method is shown not to beat Vina's ranking on a blinded set, reverse this requirement. This is the direct operational consequence of the 0.08 kcal/mol result: public money currently risks being spent ranking noise.
2. **Prioritise the 3AP-DPA dicarboxylate scaffold over thiol repurposing in the national IMP-1 research agenda.** 3AP-DPA (240 nM) is the most potent measured IMP-1 inhibitor in the ChEMBL record for this target and the only chemotype this benchmark could rank correctly; captopril repurposing for IMP-1 should be deprioritised (best stereoisomer 7.2 µM, and its ranking cannot be trusted computationally).
3. **Fund a Pakistan IMP-1 assay panel.** IMP-1 is 2.7% of carbapenemase genes today (Gondal et al. 2023) but plasmid-borne and rising wherever carbapenems are overused; the country has no domestic IC50 pipeline for MBL inhibitors, so every ranking decision is imported. A single national panel (IMP-1, NDM-1, VIM-2) at one reference laboratory would convert this paper's falsifier into a routine check.

**Islamic principle behind the recommendations.** Preservation of life (*hifz al-nafs*, one of the five objectives of the law, Al-Ghazali, *Al-Mustasfa*; systematised by Al-Shatibi, *Al-Muwafaqat*): "whoever saves a life, it is as if he had saved mankind entirely" (Qur'an 5:32). Antimicrobial resistance is a direct threat to life — in Pakistan 42.1% of clinical isolates in the Lahore study were carbapenem-resistant — so a screening method that mis-ranks inhibitors is not a technical footnote but a failure to protect life, and public interest (*maslaha*) requires the method to be fixed before it is funded. Stewardship (*khilafah*, Qur'an 2:30): antibiotics are a trust held for future generations, and a steward does not spend a trust on a tool known to mislead. Justice (*'adl*, Qur'an 4:135): the burden of carbapenem resistance falls hardest on the poorest patients, who cannot afford polymyxins or imported combinations — so the research agenda must prioritise the scaffolds with the best measured potency per rupee, not the most familiar repurposed drug. Where the Islamic sources and the modern evidence point the same way — protect life, spend the trust honestly, favour the poor — this paper says so; they do here.

## Conclusion

Rigid AutoDock Vina docking into the d-captopril-bound IMP-1 structure 4C1G ranks the dicarboxylates correctly — 3AP-DPA (−7.42 kcal/mol) ahead of dipicolinic acid (−6.50) for a 12.6-fold measured gap — with an overall Spearman ρ = −0.66 (n = 6). But the four captopril stereoisomers collapse into 0.08 kcal/mol for a 60-fold measured potency range, the weakest scoring joint-best, and the 6JED replication reproduces the pattern exactly. The mechanism is the deleted metal: the pipeline strips the two catalytic Zn(II) ions and Vina has no metal-coordination term, so inhibitors whose binding is Zn chelation are scored on shape alone. Two B1 enzymes now show the same failure (VIM-2: 0.07 kcal/mol for 76-fold). The memorable number is **0.08 kcal/mol** — that is what 60-fold of metal-dependent potency is worth to a metal-free docking score. Do not rank thiol MBL inhibitors with it.
