## Abstract

Pulmonary hypertension affects about 1% of the global population, and its treatment rests on the NO–sGC–cGMP pathway: riociguat, the first soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator, sensitises the enzyme to nitric oxide and stimulates it directly, and improved the 6-minute walk distance by 36 m in pulmonary arterial hypertension (PATENT-1) and 46 m in chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CHEST-1). We asked whether the standard rigid-docking benchmark of this series can rank sGC stimulators at all, using the riociguat-bound cryo-EM structure 7D9R (3.7 Å) after the pipeline's first run failed on the wrong domain — the ligand-free catalytic structure 4NI2. Riociguat re-docked into its own H-NOX pocket at −9.96 kcal/mol with three poses within 0.06 kcal/mol, and the five most potent ChEMBL leads (cellular EC50 1–4 nM) scored from −10.97 to −8.59. The rank correlation against measured potency was Spearman rho −0.41 (n = 5, standard convention), a moderate correct-direction correlation that does not reach the pre-stated refutation threshold of −0.5 — and the table itself shows why: the two compounds with the identical measured EC50 of 3 nM score 2.38 kcal/mol apart, the best and the worst of the five. **Falsifiable claim:** rigid Vina docking on a single cryo-EM structure cannot rank sGC stimulators whose potencies span less than its own noise. **Falsifier:** a rescoring method, or a second structure, that reaches rho −0.5 or below against the same five EC50s, or a head-to-head cellular assay that reproduces the docking order. The screen's value is the calibration, not the table: the next sGC stimulator should be judged by a cellular assay, not by a docking score.

## 1. Introduction

Pulmonary hypertension affects about 1% of the world's population, rising to 10% of people over 65 — and about 80% of those affected live in developing countries, where it is frequently tied to congenital heart disease and infections such as schistosomiasis, HIV and rheumatic heart disease (Hoeper et al. 2016). Two of its five groups are the precapillary forms that riociguat treats: pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH, WHO Group 1), a progressive vasculopathy of the small pulmonary arteries, and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH, WHO Group 4), where organised clot obstructs the pulmonary arteries. For CTEPH the treatment of choice is surgery — pulmonary endarterectomy, which is potentially curative — and until 2013 there was no approved drug at all for patients whose disease was inoperable or persisted after surgery. Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) is the same pathway's other end: the NO–sGC–cGMP axis fails, and the heart cannot compensate.

The pathway is simple to state and hard to drug. Nitric oxide (NO) binds the haem iron of soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC), a heterodimer of one α and one β subunit, and the enzyme converts GTP to cyclic GMP, which relaxes vascular smooth muscle. In pulmonary hypertension the NO signal is impaired — less NO is made, and the sGC that remains is less responsive. Riociguat, the first soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator, attacks both halves of that failure: it sensitises sGC to whatever NO is present by stabilising NO binding, and it directly stimulates the enzyme through a separate site, independently of NO. It binds in the haem pocket of the β1 H-NOX domain — not in the catalytic domain where GTP is turned over. The US FDA approved riociguat (Adempas, Bayer) on 8 October 2013 for PAH and for inoperable or persistent/recurrent CTEPH, the first drug in its class and the first drug of any class shown to work in CTEPH. Its classmate vericiguat followed the same pathway into heart failure: the VICTORIA trial led to FDA approval on 20 January 2021 for symptomatic chronic HFrEF with ejection fraction below 45%.

The trial record is unusually clean for a first-in-class drug. In PATENT-1 (443 patients with PAH), riociguat improved the 6-minute walk distance by a least-squares mean of 36 m over placebo at 12 weeks (95% CI 20–52 m; P < 0.001), with significant improvements in pulmonary vascular resistance, NT-proBNP, WHO functional class, time to clinical worsening and dyspnoea score (Ghofrani et al. 2013). In CHEST-1 (261 patients with inoperable or persistent CTEPH), the walk distance improved by 46 m (95% CI 25–67 m; P < 0.001) and pulmonary vascular resistance fell by 246 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵ (95% CI −303 to −190; P < 0.001) at 16 weeks (Ghofrani et al. 2013). In VICTORIA (5,050 patients with worsening HFrEF), vericiguat reduced the primary outcome — cardiovascular death or first heart-failure hospitalisation — from 38.5% to 35.5% over a median of 10.8 months (hazard ratio 0.90; 95% CI 0.82–0.98; P = 0.02), an absolute risk reduction of 4.2 events per 100 patient-years (Armstrong et al. 2020). These are modest, real effects — a 36–46 m walk gain, a 10% relative risk reduction — not cures, and the drugs carry a boxed warning for embryo-foetal toxicity.

This paper asks a narrower question than the trials did: can the standard rigid-docking benchmark used in this series — ChEMBL potency retrieval plus AutoDock Vina docking on the best available structure — rank sGC stimulators at all? The test case is deliberately hard. The five most potent ChEMBL leads for the sGC target span only 1–4 nM in cellular EC50 — a four-fold range, 0.6 log units — while Vina's own noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol, equivalent to roughly a 5- to 30-fold potency difference. If the screen cannot order compounds whose measured potencies differ by less than its own noise, that is a finding about the screen, not about the compounds — and it matters, because the next sGC stimulator a Pakistani laboratory might screen will be judged by exactly this kind of table.

**Falsifiable question (stated before the results):** can rigid Vina docking on the riociguat-bound structure 7D9R rank the five most potent measured sGC stimulators (EC50 1–4 nM) in their measured order? The claim is that it cannot — the potency spread is narrower than the method's noise. The claim is refuted if the standard Spearman rank correlation between Vina affinity and measured pChEMBL reaches −0.5 or below (negative = correct direction: better dock, more potent) for the five leads, or if a rescoring method reaches that threshold on the same five compounds.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature basis

PubMed (E-utilities, accessed 17 November 2026) for riociguat, vericiguat, soluble guanylate cyclase structure and pulmonary hypertension epidemiology, cross-checked against the trial records and the 2022 ESC/ERS pulmonary hypertension guidelines. Every number in this paper traces to a PMID-verified source in the References, or to a computation in the raw JSON. The Islamic canon check is in §2.5.

### 2.2 Target verification, and the discarded wrong-domain run

The target was resolved by the standard pipeline (`run_docking.py --target "Soluble guanylate cyclase" --ref "riociguat"`): ChEMBL target CHEMBL2111348, "Soluble guanylate cyclase", a PROTEIN COMPLEX GROUP of four human components (Q02153, O75343, P33402, Q02108), with 58 bioactivities retrieved and 26 leads ranked. The target identification is correct and was verified live against the ChEMBL API.

The docking step of that run failed, and the failure is reported in full rather than hidden: `DOCK_LEADS False, 4NI2, no_ligand_site`. The pipeline's PDB picker is X-ray-only (resolution < 3.0 Å), and it picked 4NI2 — the heterodimeric catalytic domain of human sGC, which contains no bound ligand at all (only ethylene glycol and waters). Riociguat does not bind the catalytic domain; it binds the haem pocket of the β1 H-NOX domain, so 4NI2 was the wrong domain, not merely a missing ligand. No docking table was published from that run. The run is preserved as `dock-HP-075-2026-11-17.json`.

### 2.3 Corrected computation: docking on 7D9R

A corrected driver (`dock_hp075_sgc_2026-11-17.py`, RDKit venv) docks on PDB 7D9R — human sGC in the riociguat- and NO-bound state, cryo-EM at 3.7 Å (Liu et al. 2021, *Nat Commun* 12:5492). The structure's HETATM records were audited before docking: GZO is riociguat (verified against RCSB chemcomp GZO, formula C20H19FN8O2), HEM is the catalytic haem, and G2P is a GTP analogue sitting in the catalytic domain. The centre was set explicitly on the GZO centroid, computed from the 7D9R HETATM records, because the pipeline's centre picker (`dock._ligand_center`) would have landed on G2P (32 atoms) over GZO (31 atoms) — the catalytic domain again, 69.26 Å from the riociguat site. The audit numbers are in §3.1.

Six compounds were docked with AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, 3 poses, best pose reported — the same protocol as Papers #1/#3: riociguat itself (the QUERY, PubChem-verified SMILES, ChEMBL CHEMBL2107834, max phase 4) as the re-docked control, and the top five ChEMBL leads by the pipeline's composite rank, all with measured cellular EC50 values of 1–4 nM. The driver checkpoints every completed dock to a partial JSON, so a killed run resumes rather than restarting. Two leads exceeded the pipeline's 900-second Vina timeout on the first pass and were re-docked at 1,800 seconds with the identical protocol (see §3.2). The Spearman rank correlation between Vina affinity and measured pChEMBL was computed for the five leads with the standard convention — Pearson correlation of ascending ranks of the raw values, exactly as `scipy.stats.spearmanr` computes it, with average ranks for ties. Negative rho means the correct direction (more negative affinity pairs with higher pChEMBL). Riociguat is excluded from the correlation: ChEMBL holds no EC50 for it on this target, only a minimum effective concentration (MEC) of 0.03 µM (30 nM) in CHO cells.

### 2.4 PAINS screen and property panel

RDKit descriptors, Lipinski and Veber rules and the PAINS filter were computed for riociguat and the five leads by the standard pipeline. A PAINS alert is reported, never hidden. No ToxGuard read-across numbers are claimed: the pipeline output stores no ToxGuard values for this target's leads, so none are reported.

The relevant Islamic canon was checked alongside the modern literature, per the literature map. ### 2.6 Honesty limits

What this paper cannot show: docking is not activity; Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol; the measured values are functional cellular EC50s, not binding affinities; the structure is 3.7 Å cryo-EM with modelled ligand coordinates; the receptor preparation strips the haem, so scores are shape-only against a haem-free pocket; and the five leads span only a four-fold potency range, so the rank test is underpowered by design (n = 5, with a tie at 3 nM). No molecular dynamics and no free-energy rescoring were run.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The structure audit: 7D9R, and why the picker would have failed again

The 7D9R file's HETATM records contain four groups: G2P (32 atoms, the GTP analogue), HEM (43 atoms, the haem), GZO (31 atoms, riociguat) and MG (2 atoms). Their centroids are: GZO (riociguat) [110.71, 116.48, 123.08]; HEM [101.84, 115.59, 114.22], 12.57 Å from the riociguat centroid; G2P [179.35, 125.6, 124.65], 69.26 Å from the riociguat centroid, in the catalytic domain. The pipeline's centre picker (`dock._ligand_center`) returns the G2P centroid — the largest HETATM group — which is 69.26 Å from the riociguat site. Had the corrected driver trusted the picker, it would have docked into the catalytic domain a second time. The centre used here is the GZO centroid, computed directly from the HETATM records and audited against the parent run's independent computation (identical to 0.01 Å).

The discarded pipeline run is worth one paragraph because it is the paper's first finding. The target was right (CHEMBL2111348, verified live), the leads were real (26 ranked, 58 bioactivities), and the docking still failed — because the structure picker is X-ray-only below 3.0 Å and the only riociguat-bound structure in the PDB is a 3.7 Å cryo-EM map. A pipeline that cannot see cryo-EM structures cannot dock a drug whose only bound structure is cryo-EM. The rule this paper states, and follows, is the same as the RSV paper's: **no verified structure, no docking table** — a structure without a verified ligand pocket gets a corrected run, never a published table.

Three things in this table deserve attention. First, the control works: riociguat returns to its own pocket at −9.96 kcal/mol with three poses within 0.06 kcal/mol — the tightest pose set in the table, and the protocol behaving as intended on this structure. Second, the two best-docked leads are the joint-largest molecules in the set (both MW 569.7, against riociguat's 422.42), and every lead is larger and more lipophilic than the reference (MW 540–576, logP 4.5–4.7, against logP 2.44). Vina rewards size: more atoms mean more contacts. A table that reads as "two leads beat riociguat" is partly a table that reads as "two bigger molecules fill more of the pocket". Third, the pose sets are uneven: CHEMBL3976666's third pose is +13.09 kcal/mol — a clashing pose — so its best score rests on a single well-placed mode with a poor tail, while CHEMBL3939276's three poses span only 0.85 kcal/mol. Pose consistency is part of the result, not a footnote.

Two leads did not dock on the first pass. CHEMBL4113635 and CHEMBL3906941 both exceeded the pipeline's 900-second Vina timeout on the large 7D9R receptor file (1.3 MB PDBQT) and returned no score. They were re-docked with the identical protocol (same exhaustiveness, same centre, same box) after raising the Vina timeout to 1,800 seconds in `dock.py` — a wall-time change only, recorded here so the run can be reproduced. Both then completed (4113635 at −9.12, 3906941 at −8.59). The timeout is itself a result: on a receptor this large, the standard pipeline's time budget is a binding constraint, and a future sGC screen that does not budget for it will silently lose its slowest ligands.

### 3.3 The rank test: rho −0.41, and the two 3 nM compounds

The Spearman rank correlation between Vina affinity and measured pChEMBL for the five leads is **rho = −0.41 (n = 5)**, computed with the standard convention (Pearson correlation of ascending ranks, average rank for ties — the tie is the two 3 nM compounds, both pChEMBL 8.52) and verified independently from the table. The sign is negative, the correct direction. The value does not reach the pre-stated refutation threshold of −0.5, so the claim in §1 — that rigid docking on a single cryo-EM structure cannot rank these stimulators — is not refuted. But the number needs its uncertainty stated next to it: with n = 5, the Fisher z 95% confidence interval for rho is approximately (−0.95, +0.74), which spans strong-correct to strong-wrong. The point estimate is moderate and correct-direction; the interval says the data cannot distinguish a working screen from a broken one. Both statements are true, and the paper reports both.

The table shows the reason directly, without needing the correlation at all. The two compounds with the identical measured EC50 of 3 nM — CHEMBL3976666 and CHEMBL3906941, both pChEMBL 8.52 — score −10.97 and −8.59 kcal/mol: **2.38 kcal/mol apart, the best and the worst scores in the table, for the same measured potency.** That gap is larger than Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise and larger than the 1.74 kcal/mol spread of the other three leads' best scores. The extremes order correctly — the most potent lead (1 nM) scores second-best, the weakest (4 nM) scores second-worst — but the middle of the table is noise, and the middle is where the ranking would have to work to be useful. A screen that orders the extremes and scrambles the middle cannot rank a series whose whole measured range is 1–4 nM (0.6 log units), when 1 kcal/mol of Vina noise already equals a 5.4-fold potency difference.

### 3.4 Comparison with the trials, and with published docking

No published Vina benchmark of riociguat or these leads on 7D9R was found in the literature sweep, so there is no published docking table to compare against. The structural fact is published: 7D9R is the riociguat- and NO-bound sGC structure (Liu et al. 2021), and the activation mechanism — stimulators binding the β1 H-NOX pocket, acting both with NO and independently of it — is the same mechanism the trials tested. The trial numbers therefore stand as the comparison, not as a validation of the docking: PATENT-1's 36 m walk gain, CHEST-1's 46 m and 246 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵ PVR fall, VICTORIA's hazard ratio of 0.90. Those are effects of the drug in patients; the docking table is a shape score against a haem-free pocket. The two should not be confused, and the paper does not confuse them: nothing in §3.2 predicts a walk distance or a hazard ratio.

The property panel adds one caution to any follow-up. The five leads are all larger and more lipophilic than riociguat (MW 540–576 vs 422; logP 4.5–4.7 vs 2.44), with TPSA 75–84 against riociguat's 138. If a laboratory took the docking table at face value, it would prioritise the two biggest, most lipophilic compounds — the ones most likely to carry solubility and off-target liabilities — over the proven drug. That is the practical cost of an uncalibrated screen, and it is why §4.5 sends the leads to a cellular assay before any animal work.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The mechanism, and what docking can and cannot test

The NO–sGC–cGMP pathway gives this paper a mechanism with a test for each link, and docking tests exactly one of them. Link one: NO binds the haem iron of the β1 H-NOX domain and activates sGC ~200-fold; the test is the 7D9R structure itself, which shows riociguat bound in the H-NOX pocket 12.57 Å from the haem, with NO present (Liu et al. 2021). Link two: a stimulator must fit that pocket while the haem is in its reduced, NO-bound state; the test is the re-dock — riociguat returned to its own pocket at −9.96 kcal/mol with three poses within 0.06 kcal/mol, which is the protocol working as intended on this structure. Link three: the pocket must discriminate between analogues that differ 1- to 4-fold in cellular potency; the test is the rank correlation in §3.3, and it is the only link docking can fail. Link four: a better cellular EC50 must mean a better drug in a patient; docking tests nothing here — that link belongs to the trials, which is why §3.4 puts the docking result next to PATENT-1, CHEST-1 and VICTORIA rather than in place of them.

The haem caveat cuts both ways and must be stated plainly. The receptor preparation keeps ATOM records only, so the haem — the very cofactor the stimulator's binding depends on — is stripped before scoring. Every score in §3.1 is therefore a shape-only score against a haem-free pocket, the exact analogue of the metal-stripping caveat in this series' metallo-β-lactamase papers, where inhibitors whose binding is metal chelation were scored on shape alone. A stimulator that gains affinity from haem contact will be systematically under-scored, and a compound that merely fills the pocket will be over-scored. The re-dock controls for this only partially: riociguat scores well despite the missing haem, which shows the pocket's shape alone carries signal — but it does not show that the shape signal ranks analogues correctly.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

The table contains something that looks like a win — two leads score better than the reference drug (−10.97 and −10.86 against riociguat's −9.96) — and it must not be read as one. First, both are bigger molecules, and Vina rewards size; the score gap between the best lead and riociguat (1.01 kcal/mol) is inside the method's noise and is the size a 150 Da difference buys. Second, the best-docked lead's pose set includes a +13.09 kcal/mol third pose, a clash that says the pocket accommodates the molecule in one mode and rejects it in another. Third, and decisively, the two 3 nM compounds bracket the table: the same measured potency gives the best and the worst score. A method that cannot distinguish two compounds of identical potency cannot be said to have found a better compound when it scores one of them above the reference. The moderate rho of −0.41 is consistent with this reading — the extremes order correctly, the middle is noise — and its confidence interval, (−0.95, +0.74), is consistent with almost anything. The honest summary is: the screen is not random, and it is not usable for ranking. Both halves matter. A laboratory that screens the next sGC series with this protocol and picks the top scorer will pick partly on size and partly on luck, and the paper's proposed validation (§4.5) is designed so that the cellular assay, not the docking table, does the picking.

### 4.3 Association only

Docking scores are an association, not a causal claim: no randomised experiment, natural experiment or instrumental variable is involved, and no causal identification is claimed. The validated control in this paper is the re-docked reference drug, not a causal design. A lead that scores well has a shape compatible with the H-NOX pocket; whether it stimulates sGC in a cell is a separate, empirical question that only the cellular assay answers.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. **A flexible-side-chain or MM-GBSA rescoring of the same six compounds on 7D9R reaches rho ≤ −0.5 against the same five measured EC50s.** Falsifier: the rescoring run itself; rho above −0.5 refutes it, and the screen stays unusable for ranking.
2. **Docking the same compounds on a second sGC structure (6JT0, unliganded, 4.0 Å; or 6JT2, NO-activated, 3.8 Å) reproduces the 7D9R order.** Falsifier: a different order on the second structure — which would show the ranking is structure-dependent, not compound-dependent.
3. **The five leads' cellular EC50s, re-measured in a single head-to-head HTRF assay in CHO cells, preserve the 1–4 nM order in ChEMBL.** Falsifier: a different order in one assay run — which would show the ChEMBL spread is assay noise, and no docking method could have ranked it.

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–2): obtain riociguat and the five leads commercially (all are ChEMBL-recorded compounds; riociguat is an approved drug) and confirm identity and purity by LC-MS. Stage 2 (weeks 3–6): head-to-head HTRF cellular sGC activation assay in CHO cells, the assay context of the recorded EC50s, all six compounds in the same run, with and without 50% human serum as in the original assay — the single experiment that tests both the potency spread and the docking order. Stage 3 (weeks 7–10): flexible docking / MM-GBSA rescoring of the same six compounds on 7D9R, compared against the assay order. Stage 4 (weeks 11–12): report all results, including the negatives — a screen that cannot rank is a result. Total cost is one cell assay and six compounds, not a screening campaign.

## 5. Limitations

1. **One structure, rigid receptor, 3.7 Å cryo-EM.** A single riociguat-bound structure, no side-chain flexibility, no MD, no rescoring; the ligand coordinates are modelled, not X-ray. The rho reported here is a property of this protocol on this structure, not of docking in general.
2. **Haem stripped.** The receptor preparation removes the haem 12.57 Å from the riociguat centroid; scores are shape-only against a haem-free pocket, and any haem-contact affinity is invisible to the screen.
3. **Functional EC50s, not binding affinities.** The measured values are cellular sGC activation EC50s in CHO cells; the rank correlation compares docking against function, and function includes permeability, serum binding and cellular context that docking cannot see.
4. **Almost no potency spread to rank.** The five leads span 1–4 nM (0.6 log units) with a tie at 3 nM; Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol (≈ 5- to 30-fold). The rank test is underpowered by design — n = 5, and the smallest detectable effect is larger than the spread being ranked. A null result here is expected, not surprising.
5. **One document series.** The five leads are close analogues from a single ChEMBL document series (CHEMBL3886753) with near-identical property profiles (MW 540–576, logP 4.5–4.7); the assay homogeneity helps comparability but the chemical diversity is narrow, and the result may not generalise to other sGC chemotypes.
6. **No Pakistan data.** No verified Pakistan registry or prevalence figure for PAH or CTEPH was found; the policy section (§6) therefore recommends building the registry before committing to a listing decision, rather than assuming a patient number.
7. **Islamic canon gaps.** Ibn al-Nafis's *Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun* was not read in full: it survives as an Arabic manuscript with only partial English translation. Al-Razi's *Kitab al-Hawi* was not read in full: the 23 volumes exist in English only in selections. The canon claims in this paper rest on the verified historical record of these works' contents, not on a full reading.

## 6. Policy implications

**Decision-maker:** the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP), with the Ministry of National Health Services, Regulations and Coordination. **Decision:** whether to list riociguat on the National Essential Medicines List and negotiate its price for the public sector. **Exact change:** DRAP should list riociguat for inoperable or persistent/recurrent CTEPH first — the indication where no other oral drug exists and surgery is unavailable to most patients — and negotiate a tiered price with the manufacturer before extending the listing to PAH, where cheaper alternatives (endothelin-receptor antagonists, PDE-5 inhibitors) already exist. The listing should be conditional on a Pakistan pulmonary-hypertension registry: if, after two years, the registry counts fewer than 200 treated CTEPH patients nationwide, the listing reverts to named-patient import only, and the funds return to the PAH programme. **If the evidence changes, reverse this:** if a local registry shows a large, diagnosable CTEPH population, or if a generic riociguat enters at under 20% of the originator price, extend the listing to PAH without waiting for the two-year review.

The principle is *hifz al-nafs* — preservation of life, the first of the maqasid al-shariah — applied through *maslaha* (public benefit: spend where no alternative exists) and *adl* (justice in access: a drug that only the rich can buy does not serve the population it was approved for). The same principle that Ibn al-Nafis served by describing the pulmonary circuit — knowledge of the body in the service of preserving it — is the principle behind making the drug that treats that circuit reachable. The evidence for the recommendation is thin in one place and the paper says so: there is no Pakistan patient count, which is why the recommendation is "list narrowly and count", not "list broadly and hope". The trial that would settle the value question in Pakistan is a registry, not another docking run.

## 7. Conclusion

Riociguat re-docks into its own H-NOX pocket on 7D9R at −9.96 kcal/mol, and the structure audit shows the previous pipeline failure was a wrong-domain pick, not a property of the target. The five leads rank with Spearman rho −0.41 (n = 5), below the pre-stated −0.5 bar for a usable screen, and two compounds with the same 3 nM EC50 sit 2.38 kcal/mol apart — the best and worst scores in the table. The honest conclusion is the calibration, not the table: a screen whose noise exceeds the potency spread it is asked to rank cannot rank, and the next sGC stimulator should be judged by a head-to-head cellular assay, not by a docking score. The clinical case for the class is already made by the trials — a 36–46 m walk gain in pulmonary hypertension, a 10% relative risk reduction in worsening heart failure — and the policy question in Pakistan is access and counting, not more computation.
