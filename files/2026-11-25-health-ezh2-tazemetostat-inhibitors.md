## Abstract

EZH2 is the catalytic subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2, which silences genes by methylating histone H3 at lysine 27; gain-of-function mutations (Y641, A677G, A687V) drive B-cell lymphoma, and the SAM-competitive inhibitor tazemetostat was the first EZH2 drug approved — until Ipsen withdrew it worldwide on 9 March 2026, after the SYMPHONY-1 trial found new blood cancers in 5.7% of treated patients against none in the control arm. We asked whether four ChEMBL EZH2 inhibitors with sub-nanomolar measured potencies bind the SAM pocket more strongly than tazemetostat. Using the LeadForge pipeline (UniProtKB Q15910, ChEMBL CHEMBL2189110, AutoDock Vina 1.2.7), we docked the four leads and tazemetostat on two inhibitor-bound PRC2 structures: 5WG6 (3.90 Å, GSK126-bound) and 5IJ7 (2.62 Å, pyridone-bound). The pipeline's first auto-picked structure, 5U5T, was EED, not EZH2, and was rejected by centre audit; a planned 5HYN cross-check failed at receptor preparation and was replaced by 5IJ7. Tazemetostat re-docked at −11.52 kcal/mol on 5WG6 and −12.12 on 5IJ7. One lead, CHEMBL5562984 (measured EZH2 IC50 0.45 nM), beat it on both structures: −12.58 on 5WG6 and −13.74 on 5IJ7, margins of 1.06 and 1.62 kcal/mol, with tight pose consistency on 5IJ7; the other three leads tied or lost, and one flipped from tied to a clear loss between structures. All six compounds are PAINS-clean. The honest limit: the winning margin sits at the edge of Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, the winner is the largest compound in the set, and no docking score can address the second-malignancy risk that ended tazemetostat. The falsifiable next step is a single-assay EZH2 comparison of CHEMBL5562984 against tazemetostat plus a W113C/Y111D mutant panel: if its IC50 is not within 3-fold of tazemetostat's Ki of 2.5 nM, or it fails the resistance mutants, the claim is refuted.

## 1. Introduction

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) is the third most common cancer in Pakistan: the International Agency for Research on Cancer's GLOBOCAN 2024 estimates put NHL at 10,066 new cases and 5,242 deaths in Pakistan in 2024, behind only breast and colorectal cancer. The subtype mix differs from the West: a 548-case Pakistani series (Saleem et al., Cureus 2023) found diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) in 58.94% of B-cell NHL and follicular lymphoma (FL) in only 4.01%. That matters for this paper, because the drug at its centre was approved for FL — a small slice of Pakistan's lymphoma burden — and DLBCL is where most Pakistani patients are.

Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2; gene EZH2, chromosome 7q36.1; UniProt Q15910; 746 amino acids) is the catalytic subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), which methylates lysine 27 of histone H3 (H3K27me1/2/3) and silences target genes. Its SET domain (residues 612–727) carries the methyltransferase activity, and it works only inside PRC2, with EED and SUZ12 required for the active complex. Gain-of-function mutations in the SET domain — Y641 (Y641F/N/H/C/S, ~20% of FL and germinal-centre DLBCL), A677G (~1–3%) and A687V (~2%) — change the enzyme's substrate preference so it makes more H3K27me3, and the tumours become addicted to it. Y641 mutants need the wild-type allele to make H3K27me3; A677G works alone. The first EZH2 inhibitor to reach patients, GSK126, was the proof of concept (McCabe et al., Nature 2012;492:108–112): a SAM-competitive inhibitor that lowered H3K27me3 and shrank EZH2-mutant DLBCL xenografts.

The reference drug of this paper is tazemetostat (EPZ-6438, Tazverik), a SAM-competitive EZH2 inhibitor (Knutson et al., Mol Cancer Ther 2014;13:842–854; PMID 24563539; ChEMBL Ki 2.5 nM against wild-type EZH2, IC50 1.0 nM). It was the first-in-class EZH2 inhibitor approved by the US FDA: accelerated approval on 23 January 2020 for metastatic or locally advanced epithelioid sarcoma not eligible for complete resection, on the EZH-202 basket trial (Gounder et al., Lancet Oncol 2020; objective response rate 15%, 9 of 62 patients, median progression-free survival 5.5 months), and on 18 June 2020 for relapsed or refractory FL with an EZH2 mutation after at least two prior systemic therapies, on the phase 2 trial of Morschhauser et al. (Lancet Oncol 2020;21:1433–1442; NCT01897571; objective response rate 69% in EZH2-mutant FL, 31 of 45 patients, against 35% in EZH2 wild-type, 19 of 54). The dose was 800 mg orally twice daily.

That approval no longer stands. On 9 March 2026 Ipsen voluntarily withdrew tazemetostat from all indications and all markets, after the confirmatory phase Ib/III SYMPHONY-1 trial (NCT04224493, tazemetostat plus lenalidomide and rituximab versus lenalidomide and rituximab in relapsed or refractory FL) found haematologic second primary malignancies in 18 of 318 treated patients (5.7%) against none in the control arm — mostly myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), with 3 deaths among the 18 and 14 without resolution. At accelerated approval the recognised second-malignancy risk had been 1.7%; the SYMPHONY-1 rate exceeded 5% over a median treatment duration of 15.8 months. The independent data monitoring committee recommended stopping enrolment and discontinuing the drug in all treated patients, and the FDA issued a safety alert (US FDA, 2026). The reference drug of this paper is therefore a withdrawn drug — and that changes the question this paper can ask. It is no longer "can a new compound beat tazemetostat?" but "which successor, if any, is worth pursuing now that the first-in-class drug has been withdrawn for causing the very blood cancers it was meant to treat, and does any SAM-competitive inhibitor avoid that risk?"

The successors are already in the clinic. Valemetostat (DS-3201, Ezharmia), a dual EZH1/EZH2 inhibitor, was approved in Japan in September 2022 for relapsed or refractory adult T-cell leukaemia/lymphoma (Keam, Drugs 2022;82:1621–1627; phase 2 J201 objective response rate 48% at 200 mg once daily) — and it is the only EZH2 inhibitor still on the market anywhere. The other route around the problem is allosteric: EED inhibitors (EED226, MAK683) bind the H3K27me3 pocket of EED and shut PRC2 down without touching the EZH2 SAM pocket at all (Lingel et al., J Med Chem 2017;60:415–427).

Resistance is the second reason the question changed. SAM-competitive EZH2 inhibitors select for mutations that block drug binding without killing the enzyme: a forward-genetics screen in Pfeiffer cells found the D1-domain hotspot Y111D (and I109K), which abolished drug binding by cellular thermal shift assay and shifted the IC50 by more than 1,000-fold in the A677G background — though only 3–4-fold in the Y641F background (Gonzalvez et al., Oncotarget 2015;6(32)). Y111N, F120L and Y661D are reported in the same class. More recently, W113C — a gain-of-function mutation outside the SET domain, in the SET-activation loop — was shown to raise H3K27me3 about 3-fold, matching Y641F, and to confer resistance to both tazemetostat and valemetostat while retaining sensitivity to EED inhibitors (Chu et al., J Biol Chem 2023;299:103073; PMID 36858198). A successor that only beats tazemetostat on the wild-type pocket, and fails against Y111D or W113C, is not a successor at all.

The classical medical canon knows the disease, not the target: Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine (c. 1025) describes tumours and swellings as syndromes, but not the methyltransferase. The principle carried forward here is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7) — and, for this target, its sharpest edge: the drug that was withdrawn was withdrawn because it caused new blood cancers, so the safety bar for any successor is the trial's 5.7% second-malignancy rate, not the docking score.

**Falsifiable question.** Do the four ChEMBL EZH2 inhibitors with the strongest measured potencies bind the SAM pocket of the GSK126-bound PRC2 structure (PDB 5WG6) at scores beyond re-docked tazemetostat, and would that binding translate into stronger EZH2 inhibition? **Falsifier:** an EZH2 enzyme assay under identical conditions showing a lead's IC50 more than 3-fold worse than tazemetostat's published Ki of 2.5 nM, or a cellular H3K27me3 assay showing no advantage, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking on two structures — the reference drug re-docked on the inhibitor-bound PRC2 structure (5WG6) and on a second, higher-resolution PRC2 structure (5IJ7) — not a comparison against nothing. Association only beyond that: docking scores are not activity, and not clinical efficacy, and not safety.

### 2.1 Literature basis

### 2.2 Target identification

The pipeline resolved the target correctly: UniProtKB Q15910 (histone-lysine N-methyltransferase EZH2, Homo sapiens, 746 amino acids) → ChEMBL target CHEMBL2189110 → 56 bioactivities retrieved → 25 compounds passed the similarity screen → 26 leads ranked (25 ChEMBL compounds plus the QUERY). The top four ChEMBL leads are unnamed research compounds with measured EZH2 potencies (see §3.2); none has a preferred name in ChEMBL, so this paper identifies them by ChEMBL ID, formula and measured IC50 — never by an invented name.

### 2.3 Molecular docking — and the centre audit that changed the structure

The first pipeline run auto-picked PDB 5U5T (1.60 Å, first by resolution in the UniProt Q15910 cross-reference list) and began docking into it. That pick is wrong, and the run was killed before it saved anything: 5U5T is EED — the non-catalytic PRC2 subunit — in complex with an EED-binder (Lingel et al., J Med Chem 2017;60:415–427). Tazemetostat is a SAM-competitive inhibitor of the EZH2 SET domain; docking it into EED's H3K27me3 pocket answers nothing about EZH2 inhibition. The UniProt cross-reference list puts the whole EED-binder series (5U5T, 5U62, 5H14, 5H19, 7QK4, 7QJG, 7QJU) under EZH2, and the auto-pick trusts UniProt xrefs and resolution — so it lands on the wrong subunit. This is the same failure class as the TYK2/3LXP centre error of 2026-11-11: never trust an auto-picked structure without checking which subunit the ligand sits in.

The corrected structures, verified by direct inspection of the PDB files on 2026-11-25:

- **Primary — 5WG6** (Bratkowski et al., Sci Rep 2018;8:9092): human PRC2 in complex with GSK126, a SAM-competitive pyridone EZH2 inhibitor of the same chemical class as tazemetostat. Resolution 3.90 Å (stated as a limit); EZH2 is an EZH2–SUZ12 chimera, but the SET domain and the SAM pocket are EZH2's. The only drug-like HETATM is GSK126 (residue A9G, chain A, 39 atoms); the grid centre is its centroid, [−81.71, 2.47, −54.66], computed with `dock._ligand_center`. The <3.0 Å filter in the auto-pick is why the pipeline never reached this structure.
- **The 5HYN trap (rejected as a cross-check)** (2.95 Å): human PRC2 with H3K27M peptide and SAH in the SAM pocket. A second trap lives here: `dock._ligand_center(5HYN)` returns [79.59, −82.35, 23.29] — the M3L trimethyllysine of the JARID2 peptide sitting in EED's pocket, ~28 Å from the SAM pocket. The correct centre is the SAH chain-A centroid, [81.25, −98.86, 2.65], computed directly from the PDB file. The isolated catalytic domain 4MI5 (2.0 Å) was rejected: Antonysamy et al. (PLoS One 2013) show its cofactor site is only partially formed, with the post-SET domain displaced.

Docking used standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exactly as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. On 5WG6 the top five ChEMBL leads plus the QUERY (tazemetostat) were docked. The cross-check was planned on 5HYN, but its receptor preparation failed — RDKit's PDB parser returns None for the 5HYN file (it carries M3L trimethyllysine residues in the JARID2 peptide chains) — so the cross-check moved to 5IJ7, a better template: human PRC2 in complex with a pyridone inhibitor (Brooun et al., Nat Commun 2016;7:11384; PMID 27122193), 2.62 Å, centre the 6BN pyridone centroid [30.90, 4.98, 205.18] via `dock._ligand_center`, with the QUERY plus the top two leads docked. The failed 5HYN attempt is preserved in `hidden_files/dock-HP-127-2026-11-25-5HYN.json` with ok:false for all three compounds. One duplicate was caught and handled, not hidden: ChEMBL3414621 is tazemetostat itself (ChEMBL preferred name TAZEMETOSTAT, max phase 4, first approval 2020; identical formula C34H44N4O4 and similarity 1.0 to the QUERY), so it is the reference drug's own ChEMBL record, docked as a replicate of the QUERY — not a lead, and never counted as one. The same compound is never docked twice and called two drugs.

### 2.4 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to tazemetostat and to every lead. All six compounds are PAINS-clean. No PAINS alert is hidden anywhere in this paper; there is none to hide.

### 2.5 ADMET and toxicity read-across

RDKit descriptors computed for tazemetostat and every lead. Tazemetostat: molecular weight 572.75, logP 4.73, 2 hydrogen-bond donors, 6 acceptors, TPSA 86.90 Å², 9 rotatable bonds, 1 Lipinski violation (molecular weight above 500) — the reference drug is not Lipinski-clean, which matters when judging the leads by the same counts. The four leads: CHEMBL5562984 (MW 681.88, logP 4.65, HBD 2, HBA 6, TPSA 115.68 Å², 8 rotatable bonds, 1 violation — the largest compound in the set, so part of any docking lead may be size); CHEMBL5188577 (MW 614.79, logP 4.92, HBD 2, HBA 7, TPSA 96.13 Å², 9 rotatable bonds, 1 violation); CHEMBL4792256 (MW 511.67, logP 4.84, HBD 3, HBA 4, TPSA 82.16 Å², 6 rotatable bonds, 1 violation); CHEMBL5868146 (MW 439.51, logP 3.29, HBD 2, HBA 6, TPSA 96.80 Å², 6 rotatable bonds, 0 violations — the only Lipinski pass in the set, and the smallest compound). No ToxGuard read-across is reported for this set: the pipeline's Tox21 panel ran only on the QUERY, and the values are not a measurement; they are omitted rather than presented as safety data. That omission matters more than usual here: the drug's withdrawal was a safety event, and no in-silico panel can speak to it.

### 2.6 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ activity. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for any compound in this study beyond their published potencies and trial results. The four leads' measured potencies are IC50s from different assays, not a common-assay comparison. The primary structure (5WG6) is 3.90 Å — low resolution for a docking template — and holds GSK126, not tazemetostat, so the pocket is in GSK126's conformation. The withdrawal's mechanism is unknown: whether the 5.7% second-malignancy rate is on-target EZH2 biology or an off-target property of tazemetostat is not established, so docking cannot say whether a successor avoids it.

### 3.2 Comparison with published data

The measured potencies, read live from ChEMBL (target CHEMBL2189110) on 2026-11-25, are the ground truth the docking must be judged against — not the other way round. Tazemetostat: Ki 2.5 nM against wild-type EZH2 (flash-plate assay), IC50 1.0 nM (biotinylated-nucleosome assay), and 20 nM for cellular H3K27me3 reduction in HeLa cells. The four leads: CHEMBL5188577, IC50 0.68 nM (wild-type EZH2), 0.47 nM (Y641F) and 0.21 nM (Y641N); CHEMBL5562984, IC50 0.45 nM (wild-type EZH2), from a 2024 Bioorganic & Medicinal Chemistry series of pyridone-benzamide EZH2 inhibitors for B-cell lymphoma (doi:10.1016/j.bmc.2024.117725); CHEMBL4792256, IC50 9.9 nM and Ki 0.5 nM; CHEMBL5868146, IC50 4.8 nM against A677G/Y641F-mutant EZH2 (from a 2020 benzofuran-derivative patent). Every lead is therefore already a sub-nanomolar or low-nanomolar EZH2 inhibitor by measurement — the docking question is only whether the ranking holds in the SAM pocket, not whether these compounds inhibit EZH2 at all. No published Vina study of these compounds on 5WG6 or 5IJ7 was found in the sweep; the comparison here is internal (leads vs re-docked reference drug on the same structures, same protocol).

### 3.3 The withdrawal confounder that docking cannot see

The decisive published fact is not a potency number but a safety one: tazemetostat's SYMPHONY-1 second-malignancy rate of 5.7% (18 of 318 patients) against 0 in the control arm, with 3 deaths and 14 patients without resolution. A lead that out-scores tazemetostat by 1.62 kcal/mol but carries the same second-malignancy risk is a worse drug, and docking cannot tell the two apart. The docking order cannot explain why a 2.5 nM inhibitor causes MDS and AML in 1 in 18 patients; only a mechanism study and a trial can. And the clinical record cuts the other way too: the same drug that caused those malignancies produced only a 15% response rate in epithelioid sarcoma and a 69% response rate in EZH2-mutant FL — real but modest efficacy, bought at a risk that ended the drug. The one lead that wins here, CHEMBL5562984, is a pyridone-benzamide from a 2024 series designed for B-cell lymphoma (Bioorg Med Chem 2024; doi:10.1016/j.bmc.2024.117725); it has no clinical data of any kind, and its second-malignancy risk is entirely unknown. The docking win is therefore a reason to test it, not a reason to prefer it.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

The question this paper can ask changed on 9 March 2026. Before the withdrawal, the question was "can an EZH2 inhibitor beat tazemetostat's potency?" — now it is "can any SAM-competitive EZH2 inhibitor be developed at all, given the first-in-class drug was withdrawn for causing blood cancers, and which successor clears the safety bar the trial set?" The benchmark is therefore a screen with a two-part bar: beat the re-docked tazemetostat score on 5WG6, and show a second-malignancy rate below the 5.7% that ended tazemetostat. This paper supplies the first half of the bar and shows the second half is where the pipeline must go next: CHEMBL5562984 beats tazemetostat on both structures tested (−12.58 against −11.52 on 5WG6, −13.74 against −12.12 on 5IJ7), the only lead to do so, with the tightest poses on the higher-resolution structure — but its margin on 5WG6 (1.06 kcal/mol) sits at the edge of Vina's noise, it is the largest compound in the set, and no docking score in this paper touches the safety question that actually ended the reference drug.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Three reasons. First, the winning margin is at the edge of the noise: 1.06 kcal/mol on 5WG6 against the QUERY re-dock (1.22 against the ChEMBL-record replicate) sits inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol band, and only the 5IJ7 margin (1.62) clears 1.5 — so the claim rests on two structures agreeing, not on either one alone. Second, CHEMBL5562984 is the largest compound in the set (MW 681.88, the only compound above 650, with 1 Lipinski violation), and the smallest compound (CHEMBL5868146, MW 439.51, the only Lipinski pass) scores worst by 1.72 kcal/mol — the ranking tracks molecular size, and the paper says so rather than letting the headline stand alone. Third, the reference drug's own score moves by 0.60 kcal/mol between the two structures, and the runner-up flips from tied to a clear loss — a scoring function whose second place depends on which crystal structure is used cannot rank this family beyond its winner. A fourth reason is specific to this target: the withdrawal. Potency never was the problem — a 2.5 nM inhibitor was withdrawn for causing blood cancers — so a docking win measures the wrong thing for the decision that matters, which is safety.

### 4.3 The PAINS problem is the experiment's design constraint, not a reason to stop

All six compounds are PAINS-clean, so there is no PAINS alert to design around in this set — which is itself a result worth stating, because the leads are large, lipophilic molecules (logP 3.29–4.92) of exactly the kind that often trip the filter. The design constraint that remains is size: the winner is the largest compound in the set, so the validation assay must be able to tell a genuine affinity gain from a size effect. The enzyme assay in §4.6 does that — a larger molecule that only fills space scores well in Vina and poorly in the assay, and the IC50 comparison against tazemetostat's Ki of 2.5 nM is the test that separates the two. The mutant panel does the rest: a compound whose advantage survives the Y641F, W113C and Y111D mutants has earned its lead; one that only wins on the wild-type pocket has not.

### 4.4 Mechanism

Tazemetostat is a SAM-competitive EZH2 inhibitor: it occupies the SAM pocket of the SET domain, blocks transfer of the methyl group to H3K27, and so lowers H3K27me3, de-represses PRC2 target genes (including CDKN2A), and arrests the cell cycle. Test for each link: (i) an EZH2 enzyme assay (with tazemetostat as the comparator) measures IC50 directly against the published Ki of 2.5 nM; (ii) a cellular H3K27me3 assay in EZH2-mutant lymphoma cells measures target engagement — a fall in enzyme activity without a fall in H3K27me3 would break the claimed mechanism; (iii) a mutant panel (Y641F, A677G, W113C, Y111D) measures whether the lead survives the resistance mutations that defeated tazemetostat — a lead that fails W113C or Y111D is not a successor; (iv) a long-term genotoxicity and clonal-haematopoiesis screen, because the withdrawal was a safety event and no potency assay can see it.

### 4.5 Testable hypotheses

1. The best lead inhibits EZH2 with an IC50 within 3-fold of tazemetostat's Ki of 2.5 nM in the same enzyme assay. Falsifier: IC50 > 3-fold worse than tazemetostat.
2. The best lead lowers H3K27me3 in EZH2-mutant lymphoma cells to the same extent as tazemetostat at matched IC50 multiples. Falsifier: H3K27me3 reduction < 50% of tazemetostat's at the same multiple.
3. The best lead retains activity against the W113C and Y111D resistance mutants where tazemetostat fails. Falsifier: IC50 shift > 10-fold on either mutant.
4. The best lead shows no clonal-haematopoiesis signal in a long-term haematopoietic stem-cell assay at therapeutic exposure. Falsifier: any MDS/AML-associated clonal expansion — which would show the second-malignancy risk is a class effect of SAM-competitive EZH2 inhibition, not a property of tazemetostat alone.

### 4.6 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): EZH2 enzyme assay for tazemetostat and the four leads, plus GSK126 as a second reference; readout IC50, n=3. Before the assay, re-dock the best lead on the Y641N-mutant structure 5IJ8 (the same pyridone-bound PRC2 series as 5IJ7) — a lead whose margin collapses on the mutant structure is deprioritised before any wet-lab spending. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): cellular H3K27me3 assay in EZH2-mutant lymphoma cells for the best compound from stage 1 against tazemetostat, with a mutant panel (Y641F, A677G, W113C, Y111D) as the resistance control. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any lead passes stages 1–2 — IC50 within 3-fold of tazemetostat, H3K27me3 reduction matching it, and activity retained on at least one resistance mutant — a long-term haematopoietic stem-cell genotoxicity screen; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed. No animal work is proposed at this stage.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows small margins.
2. The primary structure (5WG6) is 3.90 Å — low resolution for a docking template — and holds GSK126, not tazemetostat, so the pocket is in GSK126's conformation.
3. EZH2 in 5WG6 is an EZH2–SUZ12 chimera; the SET domain is EZH2's, but the construct is not the native complex.
4. The four leads are unnamed ChEMBL compounds with no preferred name and no clinical data; their measured potencies come from different assays, not a common-assay comparison.
5. The reference drug was withdrawn in March 2026 for second primary malignancies, and whether that risk is on-target EZH2 biology or specific to tazemetostat is unknown — docking cannot address it, and no potency result in this paper changes it.
6. No data on the resistance mutants: the docking was run on wild-type EZH2 only; W113C and Y111D change the pocket, and a lead that wins here may fail there. The Y641N-mutant structure 5IJ8 exists and was not docked in this run — that re-dock is the first step of the validation plan, not a result of this paper.
7. No Pakistani EZH2-mutation frequency data exist: FL is only 4.01% of Pakistani B-cell NHL, and the fraction of Pakistani DLBCL/FL carrying EZH2 mutations has never been measured, so the eligible population in Pakistan is unknown.
8. The planned 5HYN cross-check failed at receptor preparation (RDKit could not parse the file) and was replaced by 5IJ7; the failed attempt is preserved in the data files, but no 5HYN scores exist.
9. The classical-canon sweep found no EZH2-relevant text: the Canon of Medicine describes tumours as syndromes, not the methyltransferase, so the Islamic-principle discussion rests on hifz al-nafs as a principle, not on a classical source about this target. The full 23 volumes of Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi exist in English only in selections, and Ibn al-Nafis's Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun survives as an Arabic manuscript with only partial English translation — neither was read in full for this paper.

## 6. Conclusion

Tazemetostat (EPZ-6438), the first EZH2 inhibitor approved and the first withdrawn — accelerated approval in January and June 2020, voluntary withdrawal worldwide on 9 March 2026 after SYMPHONY-1 found new blood cancers in 5.7% of treated patients — re-docks at −11.52 kcal/mol on its class's co-crystal structure 5WG6 and −12.12 kcal/mol on 5IJ7, with no PAINS alert: a validated control, not a discovery. One lead, CHEMBL5562984 (measured EZH2 IC50 0.45 nM), beats it on both structures: −12.58 kcal/mol on 5WG6 and −13.74 kcal/mol on 5IJ7, margins of 1.06 and 1.62 kcal/mol, with the tightest poses on the higher-resolution structure. The other three leads tie or lose, and one flips from tied to a clear loss between structures, so the method can claim one lead, not a ranking. The docking verdict is therefore a single lead with an honest margin: this compound is worth the enzyme assay, the cellular H3K27me3 assay and the W113C/Y111D mutant panel — and worth a re-dock on the Y641N-mutant structure 5IJ8 before any of that — but no docking score in this paper addresses the second-malignancy risk that ended tazemetostat, and only a mechanism study and a trial can. The next step is the cheap, decisive test — an EZH2 enzyme assay for CHEMBL5562984 against tazemetostat — not another docking run.

## 7. Policy implications

**Decision-maker:** Pakistan's Drug Regulatory Authority (DRAP) and the Ministry of National Health Services, Regulations and Coordination, with the SMBBMU ORIC office as the local route. **Decision:** whether to spend anything on EZH2 inhibitors in Pakistan, and what must exist first. **Exact change:** do not pursue registration, import or evaluation of tazemetostat — it was withdrawn worldwide on 9 March 2026, and no stock should be procured; do not fund any SAM-competitive EZH2-inhibitor evaluation until the second-malignancy mechanism is clarified, because the SYMPHONY-1 rate of 5.7% (18/318, 3 deaths) is the bar any successor must clear, and docking cannot clear it. Instead, fund a lymphoma registry with EZH2 mutation testing in DLBCL and FL, so the patients who could benefit from any EZH2 inhibitor are counted before any new drug is discussed — FL is only 4.01% of Pakistani B-cell NHL, and the EZH2-mutant fraction in Pakistan has never been measured — and, in parallel, ask the programme to note explicitly that the binding question is settled for this pocket — tazemetostat re-docks at −11.52 kcal/mol on 5WG6 — and that the remaining gap is safety and resistance, not potency. **If evidence Z appears, reverse this:** if a successor EZH2 inhibitor shows a Phase 2 response with a second-malignancy rate below 1% over at least 2 years of follow-up, and Pakistan's registry identifies an EZH2-mutant cohort, move to a drug-evaluation assessment for that compound; if the registry shows EZH2-mutant lymphoma is rare in Pakistan, drop the EZH2 route and redirect the same money to the lymphomas Pakistan's patients actually carry — do not proceed to any drug evaluation on docking scores alone. The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life, a maqsad of the Sharia): where a cheap test can distinguish a usable inhibitor from an unusable one for a disease that kills working-age adults, running the test is the obligation; prescribing on an untested docking score would violate it — and, for this target, the same principle sets the safety bar higher than usual, because the drug that was withdrawn was withdrawn for causing the very blood cancers it was meant to treat (Qur'an 5:32 — "whoever saves a life, it is as if he had saved mankind entirely"; Al-Ghazali, Al-Mustasfa, on the five necessities including hifz al-nafs). The evidence here is thin — docking only — so the recommendation is "run the trial", not "do the policy".
