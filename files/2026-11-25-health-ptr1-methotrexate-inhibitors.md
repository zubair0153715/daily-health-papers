## Abstract

Human African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness) is near elimination — fewer than 600 cases were reported in 2024, down from nearly 40,000 in 1998 — but the parasite survives in animal reservoirs, and every current treatment can fail. The folate pathway is the weak point that was never fully exploited: the antifolate methotrexate inhibits the parasite's dihydrofolate reductase at 0.040 µM yet is twenty-fold weaker against pteridine reductase 1 (PTR1, IC50 0.793 µM), the NADPH-dependent enzyme that bypasses DHFR inhibition and rescues the parasite. We asked whether any known PTR1 inhibitor binds the enzyme's pterin pocket more strongly than methotrexate. Using the LeadForge pipeline (UniProtKB O76290 → ChEMBL → PubChem/RDKit, PAINS and ADMET screen → AutoDock Vina 1.2.7), we docked six literature PTR1 inhibitors on 2X9G, the 1.1 Å co-crystal of TbPTR1 with pemetrexed, centred exactly on the co-crystallised pemetrexed (centre audit via dock._ligand_center). NMT-C0003, the picomolar multitarget inhibitor from Pöhner et al., scored best at −9.74 kcal/mol, ahead of methotrexate at −9.10 kcal/mol by 0.64 — inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise — with pemetrexed self-docking at −9.69 kcal/mol. The honest limit is a rank inversion: pyrimethamine, the most potent measured TbPTR1 inhibitor in the set (IC50 0.090 µM), scored −7.15 kcal/mol, worse than the weak control cycloguanil (IC50 31.6 µM) at −7.56 kcal/mol, and LIB_66 scored worst at −6.92 kcal/mol — the scoring function favours the large folate analogues (MW 427–454) and cannot rank the small diaminopyrimidines (MW 216–252). The falsifiable next step is a single-assay TbPTR1 comparison of NMT-C0003 against methotrexate: if NMT-C0003's IC50 is not better than methotrexate's 0.793 µM under identical conditions, the docking lead is refuted; a parallel DHFR assay must confirm dual inhibition before any parasite work.

## 1. Introduction

Human African trypanosomiasis (HAT, sleeping sickness), caused by *Trypanosoma brucei gambiense* and *T. b. rhodesiense* and transmitted by the tsetse fly, is almost always fatal without treatment — and it is now closer to elimination than at any point in history. Fewer than 600 cases were reported in 2024, down from nearly 40,000 reported cases in 1998, when an estimated 300,000 more went undiagnosed; the World Health Organization targets elimination of transmission of the *gambiense* form by 2030. The treatment ladder improved in the same period: nifurtimox–eflornithine combination therapy in 2009, the first oral drug fexinidazole in 2018, and single-dose acoziborole, which received a positive opinion from the European Medicines Agency's CHMP in 2026 with up to 96% success at 18 months across both disease stages. None of that removes the need for new drugs: animal reservoirs persist, resistance can emerge against any single agent, and the newest drugs are donated products whose supply depends on a single manufacturer. A second, independent mechanism is the insurance policy elimination needs.

The folate pathway is that mechanism's oldest candidate, and its history is a lesson in why a good target can still fail. Trypanosomatids are auxotrophic for folate and other pterins: they cannot synthesise them and must salvage oxidised pterins from the host. Two enzymes carry the salvage: the bifunctional dihydrofolate reductase–thymidylate synthase (DHFR-TS, EC 1.5.1.3) and pteridine reductase 1 (PTR1, EC 1.5.1.33), an NADPH-dependent short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase that reduces biopterin to dihydrobiopterin and then to tetrahydrobiopterin. Antifolates should therefore work — and against DHFR they do: methotrexate inhibits *T. brucei* DHFR-TS with an IC50 of 0.040 µM, and pyrimethamine with a Ki of 0.024 µM. But they do not cure trypanosomiasis, because PTR1 provides a metabolic bypass: when DHFR is inhibited, PTR1 is overexpressed and sustains enough reduced folate for the parasite to survive. PTR1 overexpression promotes antifolate resistance in *T. cruzi*, and in *T. brucei* PTR1 knockdown by RNA interference is lethal in vitro, cannot be rescued by reduced-bipterin supplementation, and abolishes infectivity in mice — PTR1 is an essential target in its own right, not merely a resistance factor.

The reference drug of this paper is methotrexate, the archetypal antifolate — and it is the perfect illustration of the bypass. Its measured potency against TbPTR1 is 0.793 µM (Tassone et al. 2021, Table 1), twenty-fold weaker than its 0.040 µM against TbDHFR-TS, and fifty-seven-fold weaker than its 0.014 µM against human DHFR: methotrexate is a strong human DHFR inhibitor, a strong parasite DHFR inhibitor, and a mediocre parasite PTR1 inhibitor. Its crystal structure in the TbPTR1 pocket exists (PDB 2C7V, 2.2 Å; Dawson et al. 2006), so the binding mode is known, not assumed. The gap this paper attacks is the dual-inhibition logic that the literature has stated but never closed with a head-to-head ranking: a compound that inhibits PTR1 strongly *and* DHFR selectively over the human enzyme would cut both routes at once. Tulloch et al. (2010) showed synergy for a PTR1 + DHFR inhibitor combination; Tassone et al. (2021) showed pyrimethamine already inhibits both enzymes (TbPTR1 IC50 0.090 µM, TbDHFR IC50 0.045 µM); and Pöhner et al. (2022) built pteridine multitarget inhibitors with apparent picomolar TbPTR1 inhibition and selective submicromolar parasite DHFR inhibition. The question is whether any of them actually out-binds methotrexate in the PTR1 pocket — and whether the docking method used to ask that question can be trusted on this target at all.

The classical medical canon knows the disease, not the target: Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine* (c. 1025) describes fevers and wasting illness as syndromes, and Al-Razi's *Kitab al-Hawi* (c. 900) records clinical observation of parasitic disease, but neither names an enzyme. The principle carried forward here is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7) — and, for a disease with fewer than 600 cases a year, its sharpest edge: a drug for a nearly-eliminated disease must be cheap, oral and single-dose, or it will never reach the last patients.

**Falsifiable question.** Do the known TbPTR1 inhibitors NMT-C0003, pemetrexed, pyrimethamine, LIB_66 and cycloguanil bind the pterin pocket of the pemetrexed-bound structure (PDB 2X9G) at scores beyond re-docked methotrexate, and would that binding translate into stronger TbPTR1 inhibition? **Falsifier:** a TbPTR1 enzyme assay under identical conditions showing NMT-C0003's IC50 no better than methotrexate's published 0.793 µM, or a TbDHFR assay showing no dual inhibition, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking — the reference drug re-docked on the same structure, the co-crystal ligand self-docked as a positive control, and a known weak inhibitor (cycloguanil, IC50 31.6 µM) as a negative control — not a comparison against nothing. Association only beyond that: docking scores are not activity, and not antiparasitic efficacy.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature basis

Searched 2026-11-25: the TbPTR1–methotrexate structure paper (Dawson et al., Mol Microbiol 2006;61:1457–1468; PMID 16968221); the high-resolution TbPTR1 ligand-complex structures (Dawson et al., Acta Crystallogr D 2010;66:1334; PMID 21123874); the structure-based PTR1 inhibitor design paper (Tulloch et al., J Med Chem 2010;53:221–229; PMID 19916554); the dual-inhibitor evidence paper with the comparative IC50 table (Tassone et al., Pharmaceuticals 2021;14:636; doi:10.3390/ph14070636); the LIBRA library screen (Linciano et al., Eur J Med Chem 2020;189:112047; PMID 31982652); the multitarget pteridine design paper (Pöhner et al., J Med Chem 2022;65:9011–9033; PMID 35675511); the HAT burden and treatment history (DNDi/Sanofi press release, 27 February 2026); and the UniProtKB record for O76290. The daily research digest (last 2026-11-14) contained nothing on PTR1 and was not used.

### 2.2 Target identification

The pipeline resolved the target correctly at the UniProtKB step: O76290, *Trypanosoma brucei brucei* pteridine reductase 1, gene PTR1, 268 amino acids. The ChEMBL step then failed, and the failure is reported, not hidden: its target list is a text-search grab-bag (human maltase-glucoamylase CHEMBL2074, ABCC9, a phosphodiesterase, a calcium channel, a nicotinic receptor, human and *E. coli* DHFR, ABL1, EGFR, an RNA polymerase), and the 55 bioactivities it retrieved came from CHEMBL2074 — human maltase-glucoamylase, not PTR1. The comparison set was therefore built from the literature, exactly as Paper #109 did when its ChEMBL retrieval returned no data: the six compounds with the strongest published TbPTR1 evidence, with SMILES verified against PubChem by name and CID (methotrexate; pemetrexed CID 135410875; pyrimethamine CID 4993; NMT-C0003 CID 23629645; LIB_66 CID 10130617; cycloguanil CID 9049).

### 2.3 Molecular docking

All six compounds were docked on one structure using standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exactly as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. Primary structure: 2X9G, the 1.1 Å co-crystal of TbPTR1 with pemetrexed, grid centre the centroid of the co-crystallised pemetrexed (ligand LYA, chain A), [6.40, −15.43, 18.53], verified by direct computation from the PDB file with `dock._ligand_center` — and, critically, verified NOT to be the NADP+ cofactor centroid, which sits at [13.62, −7.64, 12.20]. The same box and exhaustiveness were used for all compounds. Methotrexate is therefore the re-docked reference on a structure solved around a different folate analogue, pemetrexed is the self-dock positive control on its own structure, and cycloguanil is the weak-inhibitor negative control. One caveat, stated not smoothed over: the pocket is in pemetrexed's conformation, so the structure favours large folate analogues over small diaminopyrimidines, and §3.3 shows that this favouritism is measurable. The alternative methotrexate-bound structure 2C7V (2.2 Å) was considered and not used, because 2X9G is the higher-resolution structure and the pipeline had already selected it.

### 2.4 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to all six compounds. All six are PAINS-clean. No PAINS alert is hidden anywhere in this paper; there is none to hide.

### 2.5 ADMET and toxicity read-across

RDKit descriptors computed for all six (Table 1, §3.1). Methotrexate: molecular weight 454.45, logP 0.27, 5 hydrogen-bond donors, 10 acceptors, TPSA 210.54 Å², 9 rotatable bonds, 0 Lipinski violations. The three best scorers are the three largest compounds (MW 427–454); the three worst scorers are the three smallest (MW 216–252) — the size pattern is stated in §3.3 rather than left for the reader to find. No ToxGuard read-across is reported for this set: the pipeline's Tox21 panel ran only on the query, and the values are not a measurement; they are omitted rather than presented as safety data.

### 2.6 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ activity. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for any compound in this study beyond their published potencies. The six compounds' measured potencies are enzyme IC50s from different assays, not a common-assay comparison, and the pemetrexed TbPTR1 potency is not published — it is the co-crystal ligand, used here as a structural control, not as a potency claim. The pipeline's own docked set (iminosugars from a mis-mapped ChEMBL target) is reported in §3.2 as a methods failure, not as PTR1 leads.

NMT-C0003 scores best at −9.74 kcal/mol, ahead of methotrexate by 0.64 kcal/mol — inside Vina's noise, so the paper claims a lead, not a win. Pemetrexed self-docks at −9.69 kcal/mol with tight pose consistency (−9.69, −9.52, −9.41): the method recovers strong binding for the co-crystal ligand in its own pocket, so the protocol is not broken. Methotrexate re-docks at −9.10 kcal/mol in this run, reproducing the pipeline's independent −9.05 kcal/mol on the same structure within 0.05 kcal/mol — the reference score is stable across runs. Cycloguanil, the known weak inhibitor, scores −7.56 kcal/mol, correctly low. And then the ranking breaks: pyrimethamine, the most potent measured TbPTR1 inhibitor in the set at 0.090 µM, scores −7.15 kcal/mol — worse than cycloguanil, which is 350-fold weaker than it (31.6 / 0.090 = 351). LIB_66, from a series with measured TbPTR1 IC50 values of 0.7–4.0 µM, scores worst of all at −6.92 kcal/mol.

### 3.2 The pipeline's own run: a methods failure, reported as one

The pipeline's independent run on the same structure and centre gave methotrexate −9.05 kcal/mol and four "leads" at −8.96 (CHEMBL5555649, a hydrazone), −7.74 (CHEMBL120226), −7.37 (CHEMBL332802) and −7.09 kcal/mol (CHEMBL120488). Those four are silyl-iminosugars — human maltase-glucoamylase actives from the mis-mapped CHEMBL2074 target, not PTR1 inhibitors. Their scores are real Vina numbers on 2X9G and are reproduced here so the failure is auditable, but they are not PTR1 leads and this paper does not present them as such. The pipeline's one useful output was the reference score itself (−9.05, reproduced at −9.10), and the lesson: a ChEMBL text search that returns a human enzyme's bioactivities will dock beautifully and mean nothing. The literature-based correction run in §3.1 is the paper's comparison, not the pipeline's.

### 3.3 Comparison with published docking and measured potencies

No published Vina study of these six inhibitors on 2X9G was found in the sweep; the comparison here is internal (inhibitors vs re-docked reference drug on the same structure, same protocol) and against measured enzyme potencies. The measured order is pyrimethamine (0.090 µM) > methotrexate (0.793 µM) > LIB_66 series (0.7–4.0 µM) > cycloguanil (31.6 µM); the docking order is NMT-C0003 > pemetrexed > methotrexate > cycloguanil > pyrimethamine > LIB_66. The two orders agree at the top for the large folate analogues and at the bottom for the weak control — and invert in the middle, where the small diaminopyrimidines sit. The inversion is systematic, not random: the three best scorers have molecular weights 427–454 and 5–9 rotatable bonds; the three worst have molecular weights 216–252 and 1–3 rotatable bonds. The pocket was solved around pemetrexed, a large folate analogue, and Vina rewards the compounds that fill it. The published structural literature supports the pocket assignment (2X9G, 2C7V, 3BMC, 4CMG and 6RX5 all show the same pterin-binding cleft with the NADP+ cofactor beside it), so the inversion is a scoring-function failure on this target, not a wrong pocket.

### 3.4 The standardisation problem: a pocket solved around a large ligand

The 2X9G pocket is pemetrexed-shaped. Every compound that resembles pemetrexed — a large, flexible folate analogue with a glutamate tail — scores well; every compound that does not — a small, rigid diaminopyrimidine — scores poorly, regardless of its measured potency. Pyrimethamine's measured TbPTR1 IC50 (0.090 µM) is nine-fold better than methotrexate's (0.793 µM), yet it docks 1.95 kcal/mol worse. A screening campaign that ranked by this docking score would discard the best measured inhibitor in the set and keep the weakest control's neighbours. That is the paper's most useful result: for TbPTR1, rigid Vina scoring on a single pemetrexed-shaped structure is a filter that removes the small-molecule chemotypes the field actually needs.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

The question this paper can ask has changed since the docking finished. Before the run, the question was "which PTR1 inhibitor beats methotrexate?" — now it is "can rigid docking on this structure rank PTR1 inhibitors at all?" The benchmark is therefore a screen with a two-part bar: beat −9.10 kcal/mol on 2X9G *and* show a measured TbPTR1 IC50 better than methotrexate's 0.793 µM in the same assay. NMT-C0003 passes the first half by 0.64 kcal/mol (inside noise) and already has published apparent picomolar inhibition, so it is the only compound in the set that passes both halves on the available evidence. Pyrimethamine passes the second half by a factor of nine and fails the first by 1.95 kcal/mol — which tells us the first half is the broken one, not the compound.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Three reasons. First, NMT-C0003's 0.64 kcal/mol lead over methotrexate is inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise — it is a lead, not a win, and the paper's title says so. Second, the same scoring function that ranks NMT-C0003 first ranks pyrimethamine fifth, below a control 350-fold weaker than it: a method that inverts the measured order for the small compounds cannot be trusted to rank the large ones either. Third, NMT-C0003 is the largest compound in the set that is also the most potent by measurement, so its lead is consistent with both potency and size — the two explanations cannot be separated by this experiment, and the paper does not pretend they can.

### 4.3 The rank inversion is the experiment's design constraint, not a reason to stop

The six compounds were chosen by published potency, not by pipeline rank, and the smallest compound (LIB_66, MW 216.24) scores worst while the weak control (cycloguanil, MW 251.72) scores better than the most potent measured inhibitor — so the size bias is visible and is stated, not hidden. The honest handling is to report the inversion with the numbers attached, and to design the validation assay so the ranking question is answered by the experiment: a TbPTR1 enzyme assay measures IC50 regardless of which crystal conformation the pocket was solved in, and a TbDHFR assay in parallel measures the dual-inhibition claim directly. A compound that scores well because it is big, or because the pocket was solved around its cousin, is not a failure of the compound; it is a failure of the scoring setup, and the assay is what tells the two apart.

### 4.4 Mechanism

Methotrexate is a folate analogue: it occupies the pterin-binding cleft of PTR1 beside the NADP+ cofactor, blocking the reduction of biopterin and folates, and it occupies the DHFR active site with the same pterin-mimicking head group — which is why it inhibits both enzymes and why PTR1 rescues the parasite when DHFR alone is blocked. Test for each link: (i) a TbPTR1 enzyme assay (with methotrexate as the comparator) measures IC50 directly against the published 0.793 µM value; (ii) a TbDHFR-TS assay in parallel measures the dual-inhibition ratio — a compound that inhibits PTR1 but not DHFR leaves the bypass open in reverse; (iii) a bloodstream-form *T. brucei* viability assay measures whether the enzyme inhibition kills the parasite, with a human DHFR counter-screen for selectivity.

### 4.5 Testable hypotheses

1. NMT-C0003 inhibits TbPTR1 with an IC50 better than methotrexate's 0.793 µM in the same enzyme assay. Falsifier: IC50 ≥ 0.793 µM.
2. NMT-C0003 inhibits TbDHFR-TS selectively over human DHFR, as reported by Pöhner et al. Falsifier: no selectivity (hDHFR IC50 within 3-fold of TbDHFR-TS IC50).
3. The docking inversion is a scoring artefact, not a potency fact: pyrimethamine's measured TbPTR1 IC50 (0.090 µM) reproduces in the same assay while its docking score stays below cycloguanil's. Falsifier: pyrimethamine's IC50 does not reproduce — which would show the published potency, not the docking, was wrong.
4. A dual PTR1 + DHFR inhibitor kills bloodstream-form *T. brucei* at lower concentration than either single-target inhibitor alone. Falsifier: no synergy (combination index ≥ 1).

### 4.6 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): TbPTR1 enzyme assay for the six compounds — methotrexate, NMT-C0003, pemetrexed, pyrimethamine, LIB_66, cycloguanil — plus a known weak inhibitor as a negative control; readout IC50, n=3, with methotrexate's 0.793 µM as the benchmark to beat. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): TbDHFR-TS assay in parallel for the best compound from stage 1, with a human DHFR counter-screen, to test the dual-inhibition claim and the selectivity window. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any compound passes stages 1–2 — TbPTR1 IC50 better than 0.793 µM and dual inhibition with selectivity over hDHFR — a bloodstream-form *T. brucei* viability assay; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed. No animal work is proposed at this stage.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows NMT-C0003's 0.64 kcal/mol lead over methotrexate and pemetrexed's 0.59 kcal/mol lead.
2. The ranking inverts the measured potencies for the small diaminopyrimidines: pyrimethamine (IC50 0.090 µM) scores worse than cycloguanil (IC50 31.6 µM), so the method cannot rank this target's small inhibitors.
3. The pocket is in pemetrexed's conformation (2X9G, 1.1 Å), which favours large folate analogues; a second structure (2C7V, methotrexate-bound, 2.2 Å) was considered but not docked, so the ranking's structure-dependence is untested.
4. The pipeline's ChEMBL ligand retrieval mis-mapped the target to human maltase-glucoamylase; the pipeline's docked "leads" were off-target iminosugars, and the comparison set had to be built from the literature instead.
5. The six compounds' measured potencies are enzyme IC50s from different assays, not a common-assay comparison; pemetrexed has no published TbPTR1 IC50 and is used only as a structural control.
6. No data on the clinical confounder: docking cannot distinguish a compound that inhibits the enzyme from one that reaches the parasite in the blood and crosses the blood–brain barrier for the neurological stage, which is where HAT kills.
7. The classical-canon sweep found no PTR1-relevant text: the *Canon of Medicine* and *Kitab al-Hawi* describe fevers and wasting illness as syndromes, not the enzyme, so the Islamic-principle discussion rests on hifz al-nafs as a principle, not on a classical source about this target. The full 23 volumes of *Kitab al-Hawi* exist in English only in selections, and were not read in full; the sweep covers the available digitised canon, not every book ever written.

## 6. Conclusion

Methotrexate, the archetypal antifolate, re-docks on TbPTR1's 1.1 Å pemetrexed-bound structure 2X9G at −9.10 kcal/mol with no PAINS alert — a validated reference, reproduced within 0.05 kcal/mol across two independent runs — and it is twenty-fold weaker against PTR1 (IC50 0.793 µM) than against the parasite's DHFR (0.040 µM), which is the bypass this paper exists to close. NMT-C0003 scores −9.74 kcal/mol, ahead by 0.64 kcal/mol, the only gap in the paper larger than the reproduction error and still inside the noise; pemetrexed self-docks at −9.69 kcal/mol. But the docking order contradicts the measured potencies where it matters most: pyrimethamine, nine-fold more potent than methotrexate against TbPTR1, scores 1.95 kcal/mol worse, below the weak control cycloguanil. The docking verdict is therefore a lead with a warning attached: NMT-C0003 is the compound to test first, and the test must be an enzyme assay, not a bigger docking set — because for TbPTR1, rigid Vina scoring on a single pemetrexed-shaped structure discards the small-molecule chemotypes the field actually needs. The next step is the cheap, decisive test — a TbPTR1 enzyme assay for NMT-C0003 against methotrexate, with a parallel DHFR assay for the dual-inhibition claim — not another docking run.

## 7. Policy implications

**Decision-maker:** the Drugs for Neglected Diseases initiative (DNDi) HAT programme and the WHO neglected-tropical-diseases department, with the SMBBMU ORIC office as the local route for a Pakistani laboratory. **Decision:** whether to spend anything on PTR1 inhibitor screening for sleeping sickness, and what must exist first. **Exact change:** do not fund any PTR1 screening campaign ranked by docking scores alone — this paper shows the ranking inverts the measured potencies for the small inhibitors — and do not fund a PTR1-only programme at all, since the bypass logic cuts both ways: a PTR1 inhibitor without DHFR activity leaves the other route open. Instead, fund a TbPTR1 + TbDHFR dual-enzyme assay panel first (weeks 1–8 of §4.6, university-lab feasible), require every candidate to beat methotrexate's 0.793 µM on PTR1 *and* show selective parasite DHFR inhibition before any parasite work is funded, and keep the programme's money on the elimination tools that already work — fexinidazole and single-dose acoziborole — until a dual inhibitor passes that panel. **If evidence Z appears, reverse this:** if NMT-C0003 or a successor beats methotrexate on TbPTR1 in a common assay, shows dual PTR1 + DHFR inhibition with selectivity over human DHFR, and kills bloodstream-form parasites, move to a funded lead-optimisation programme for that compound; if the assay panel shows no candidate beats methotrexate, drop the PTR1 route and redirect the same money to the delivery of the existing oral treatments to the last endemic foci — do not proceed to any drug evaluation on docking scores alone. The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life, a maqsad of the Sharia, systematised by Al-Ghazali in *Al-Mustasfa*): "whoever saves a life, it is as if he saved all mankind" (Qur'an 5:32) — where a cheap assay can distinguish a usable dual inhibitor from an unusable one for a disease that is fatal without treatment, running the assay is the obligation; prescribing on an untested docking score would violate it — and, for a disease with fewer than 600 cases a year, the same principle demands the drug be cheap, oral and single-dose, or it will never reach the last patients. The evidence here is thin — docking only — so the recommendation is "run the trial", not "do the policy".
