## Pinning the Cosmic Shoreline with JWST: LTT 1445 A b and c Are Airless Above the Line, LHS 1140 b Keeps Its Helium Below It

- **File:** `2026-11-14-space-cosmic-shoreline-ltt1445-lhs1140.md` (+ PDF)
- **Abstract:** 1614 characters (limit 2,500)
- **Abstract text:** Two results published in November 2026 test the cosmic shoreline — the I ∝ v_esc^4 boundary that separates rocky planets with atmospheres from airless worlds — from opposite sides. The JWST Rocky Worlds programme reports MIRI/F1500W secondary eclipses of LTT 1445 A b and c (Ih et al., arXiv:2611.17300): eclipse depths of 157(+18/−17) and 154(+54/−37) ppm give 15-µm brightness temperatures of 528(+39/−29) K and 625(+109/−79) K, both consistent with dark bare rock; for b, CO2 partial pressure is <0.6 mbar and surface pressure ≲0.1 bar. LTT 1445 A b is the closest planet to the shoreline yet observed in eclipse. In the same week, ground-based spectroscopy reported metastable helium escaping from the habitable-zone super-Earth LHS 1140 b in 2024 but not in 2025 (Cherubim et al., Science 2026), while four JWST NIRISS transits rule out helium at the reported level (Radica 2026; Bennett et al. 2026). We place all three planets on the classical Mars-anchored XUV shoreline, I_XUV = (a⊕/a)^2 (L*/L⊙)^0.4, and find LTT 1445 A b 2.3× above the line, LTT 1445 A c 11.9× above it, and LHS 1140 b 8.5× below it — every planet on its predicted side. The two results bracket the shoreline zero point to log10 I_XUV = 4 log10 v_esc + D with D in [−4.10, −2.81], against the canonical −3.17. We state this as a falsifiable claim: any substantial atmosphere found on a rocky planet more than 0.36 dex above the classical line, or any bare rock more than 0.93 dex below it, falsifies the classical zero point; simultaneous ground- and space-based helium transits of LHS 1140 b will decide whether its lower bound stands.
