## Abstract

Receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) drives necroptosis and inflammatory cytokine production, and its first-in-class inhibitor GSK2982772 reached Phase 2 — then failed to show meaningful clinical improvement in rheumatoid arthritis. We asked whether ChEMBL-recorded RIPK1 ligands bind the allosteric pocket more strongly than GSK2982772 itself. Using the LeadForge pipeline (UniProtKB Q13546 → ChEMBL target CHEMBL5464, 46 bioactivities → PubChem/RDKit similarity, PAINS and ADMET screen → AutoDock Vina 1.2.7), we re-docked the top four leads and GSK2982772 on the 2.0 Å human RIPK1 kinase-domain structure 7FD0, centred on the co-crystallised benzoxazepinone inhibitor (verified centroid). All four leads scored −8.57 to −9.90 kcal/mol against −9.80 kcal/mol for re-docked GSK2982772, with tight pose consistency; their measured IC50 values are 0.4–0.79 nM, and none of the 26 pipeline leads carries a PAINS alert. The honest limit: the best lead (CHEMBL4071864, −9.90 kcal/mol) is GSK2982772 itself, the other three are close analogues of the same benzoxazepinone-triazole chemotype, every margin over the reference is inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, and the reference drug already failed its efficacy test in patients despite on-target exposure. The claim that any of these analogues is a better RIPK1 drug would be refuted if a kinase assay shows no potency advantage over GSK2982772, or if a cellular necroptosis assay shows no advantage. The falsifiable next step is an 8–12-week kinase-plus-necroptosis test of the three analogues against GSK2982772, with a brain-penetration screen — the indication where RIPK1 inhibition still has an open question.

## 1. Introduction

RIPK1 is a serine/threonine kinase that sits at the junction of cell death and inflammation: downstream of TNF receptors, Toll-like receptors 3/4 and interferons, it controls apoptosis, necroptosis (via RIPK3 and MLKL) and the production of TNF-α and IL-6. Necroptosis is highly inflammatory — the dying cell releases its contents and activates innate immunity — and RIPK1-mediated necroptosis has been implicated in rheumatoid arthritis, ulcerative colitis, psoriasis, neurodegeneration and sepsis. Blocking the kinase should therefore quiet both cell death and cytokine production, and the first drug to test that idea in humans was GSK2982772 (GSK772), a potent, selective, oral, ATP-competitive inhibitor discovered from a DNA-encoded library: IC50 16 nM against recombinant RIPK1, ~98% inhibition at 10 nM, EC50 0.8 µM against TNF-α-induced necroptosis in L929 fibroblasts, with >1,000-fold selectivity reported over other kinases. It binds an allosteric pocket of the RIPK1 kinase domain. In the clinic it was safe — 60 mg twice or three times daily for 84 days in 52 patients with moderate-to-severe rheumatoid arthritis produced no safety signal — but it did not work: a randomised, placebo-controlled experimental-medicine study (NCT02858492) found no significant difference from placebo in disease activity, radiological progression or inflammatory markers, and the authors concluded that RIPK1 inhibition at the exposures achieved "do not translate into meaningful clinical improvement of RA". The target is therefore in an awkward position: validated as a kinase, unproven as a medicine, with the open question being whether the failure was the indication (RA), the exposure, or the target itself.

The classical medical canon has no concept of a kinase, and this paper does not pretend otherwise: Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine (c. 1025) discusses inflammatory swellings and fevers, and Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi records clinical observation of joint disease. These texts are hypothesis sources, not evidence — they knew the syndrome, not the target — but they establish that the disease burden this paper addresses is centuries old in the region, and the Islamic principle behind acting on it is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7).

**Falsifiable question.** Do ChEMBL-recorded RIPK1 ligands bind the allosteric pocket (PDB 7FD0) more strongly than GSK2982772, and is there a scaffold beyond the benzoxazepinone chemotype worth testing? **Falsifier:** a kinase assay showing no IC50 advantage over GSK2982772's 16 nM for any analogue, or a cellular necroptosis assay showing no EC50 advantage, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking — the reference drug re-docked on a kinase-domain structure bound to its own chemotype — not a comparison against nothing. Association only beyond that: docking scores are not activity.

### 2.2 Molecular docking

Standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 protocol, exactly as Papers #1/#3. Target: human RIPK1, UniProtKB Q13546 (receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase 1); ChEMBL target CHEMBL5464 (46 bioactivities retrieved live 2026-11-19). Structure: PDB 7FD0, the human RIPK1 kinase domain in complex with a benzoxazepinone-triazole inhibitor, 2.0 Å. Grid centre: centroid of the co-crystallised ligand, [−7.67, −14.94, 24.29], computed by the pipeline's `_ligand_center` from the PDB file; box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. The same centre, box and exhaustiveness were used for GSK2982772 and all four leads.

**A wrong-domain audit, stated plainly.** The pipeline's first run for this topic auto-picked PDB 6AC5 — the RIPK1 DEATH DOMAIN (C-terminal domain, GlcNAcylated by the EPEC effector NleB), 1.45 Å — and docked all five kinase inhibitors onto its N-acetylglucosamine sugar, scoring −4.11 to −4.72 kcal/mol. That run is DISCARDED, not reported as a result: the death domain has no kinase pocket, and GSK2982772 binds the kinase domain's allosteric pocket, not a sugar on the death domain. The failure mode is the same class as the TYK2 JH1/JH2 domain audit (2026-11-11), the gyrase 3M4I audit (Paper #75) and the MDM2 PLK1 audit (Paper #80): the auto-picker ranks by resolution and accepts any drug-like HETATM, with no domain awareness, so the structure must be checked against the drug's binding site before any score is believed. The corrected run used 7FD0, a kinase-domain structure bound to the same chemotype as GSK2982772. All scores in §3 are from the corrected run (`hidden_files/dock-HP-101b-2026-11-19.json`, `hidden_files/redock_hp101.py`); the discarded death-domain run is preserved in `hidden_files/dock-HP-101-2026-11-19.json` for audit.

### 2.3 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to all 26 pipeline leads. None of the 26 carries a PAINS alert, including all four docked leads and GSK2982772.

### 2.4 ADMET and toxicity read-across

RDKit descriptors (Lipinski/Veber) computed for every lead; ToxGuard Tox21 kNN read-across (12 assays) run for every lead. GSK2982772 itself (PubChem CID 77108121, C20H19N5O3, MW 377.40) passes Lipinski with 0 violations (logP 1.55, TPSA 100.21) and scores at most 0.40 on any Tox21 assay (SR-ARE).

### 2.5 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ activity. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for these four compounds against RIPK1 in this study beyond the ChEMBL records cited. Measured IC50 values below are ChEMBL records against RIPK1, not new measurements.

All four leads dock within 1.33 kcal/mol of each other, and the best lead beats re-docked GSK2982772 by only 0.10 kcal/mol. The reason is visible in the table: CHEMBL4071864 is GSK2982772 itself (identical formula, descriptors and similarity 1.0 to the query), and the other three are close analogues of the same benzoxazepinone-triazole chemotype (two share the formula C20H18N4O4). There is no scaffold diversity in this set — it is one chemotype, four times.

### 3.2 The docking–activity discordance that must not be hidden

The measured IC50s do not track the docking order. CHEMBL3785482, tied for the most potent measured inhibitor (0.4 nM), docks worst at −8.57 kcal/mol — 1.33 kcal/mol behind GSK2982772 itself — while CHEMBL4088857, the weakest measured inhibitor (0.79 nM), docks second-best at −9.70 kcal/mol. Rigid Vina scoring on this pocket cannot resolve a two-fold potency difference, and it mis-ranks the most potent compound. This is the paper's most important negative result: **the docking ranking is not a potency ranking**, and any claim built on a 0.10 kcal/mol "win" would be over-reading noise. The deeper problem is clinical, not computational: the reference drug was already tested in 52 patients and failed to improve rheumatoid arthritis, so a 0.10 kcal/mol docking advantage for the same molecule is not a drug-discovery result.

### 3.3 Toxicity read-across

Tox21 kNN read-across predicted no assay above 0.5 probability for any of the four leads (highest: SR-ARE 0.46 for CHEMBL4099004; all others ≤0.40). This is a weak screen — read-across, not measurement — and does not clear any compound for development.

### 3.4 Comparison with published docking

No published Vina study of these four ChEMBL compounds on 7FD0 was found in the sweep; the comparison here is internal (leads vs re-docked reference on the same structure, same protocol). The re-docked GSK2982772 score of −9.80 kcal/mol is the validated control that anchors the comparison: a method that could not recover strong binding for a 16 nM binder on a structure bound to its own chemotype would be broken. It did. The 2018 J Med Chem paper that deposited the related structure 6C3E overlaid its HTS hit with GSK2982772 in the RIPK1 kinase domain — the same pocket this paper docks into.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

The question this paper can actually answer is narrower than the one it started with. It cannot say "these analogues beat GSK2982772" — the margins are inside noise, the docking order contradicts the measured potency order, and there is no new scaffold here. What it can say is: (a) the benzoxazepinone-triazole chemotype binds the RIPK1 allosteric pocket tightly and reproducibly, with four sub-nanomolar measured inhibitors scoring −8.57 to −9.90 kcal/mol; (b) the pipeline's first, wrong-domain run was caught and corrected before publication, and the correction is documented; (c) the decisive question is no longer potency — it is indication: the RA trial failed, so the next test must be in a disease where RIPK1 inhibition still has an open question (neurodegeneration, where brain penetration matters, or sepsis), and it is cheap and feasible in a university lab.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Three reasons. First, the 0.10 kcal/mol margin is far inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise. Second, the docking order contradicts the measured potency order. Third, and decisively, the reference drug already failed in patients: 52 randomised patients, 84 days, no difference from placebo in disease activity or inflammatory markers. A docking score cannot rescue a target whose clinical translation failed in its first indication; it can only motivate a better-chosen second indication, and only an experiment can tell whether that works.

### 4.3 Mechanism

RIPK1's kinase activity drives necroptosis through RIPK3–MLKL and cytokine production through NF-κB; allosteric inhibitors like GSK2982772 occupy a pocket adjacent to the ATP site and lock the kinase in an inactive conformation. Test for each link: (i) a recombinant RIPK1 kinase assay measures whether the analogue inhibits with an IC50 at or below GSK2982772's 16 nM; (ii) a TNF-α-induced necroptosis assay in L929 or HT-29 cells measures whether kinase inhibition translates into cell-death blockade; (iii) a brain-penetration measurement (PAMPA-BBB or MDCK-MDR1) measures whether the analogue reaches the CNS, which is the property a neurodegeneration indication would need.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. At least one of the three analogues inhibits recombinant RIPK1 with an IC50 ≤16 nM (GSK2982772's value). Falsifier: all three IC50s >50 nM.
2. At least one analogue blocks TNF-α-induced necroptosis with an EC50 ≤0.8 µM (GSK2982772's value). Falsifier: all three EC50s >5 µM.
3. At least one analogue shows better brain penetration than GSK2982772 in a PAMPA-BBB assay. Falsifier: all three are worse.

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): recombinant RIPK1 kinase assay for the three analogues + GSK2982772 control; readout IC50, n=3. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): TNF-α-induced necroptosis assay (L929 fibroblasts, zVAD-fmk) for the same compounds, with phospho-RIPK1 (Ser166) and phospho-MLKL as readouts. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any analogue passes stages 1–2, PAMPA-BBB and MDCK-MDR1 permeability plus HepG2 cytotoxicity; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows every reported margin.
2. Docking order contradicts measured IC50 order; rigid docking cannot rank these compounds.
3. No scaffold diversity: all four leads are the same benzoxazepinone-triazole chemotype, and the best is the reference drug itself.
4. A single structure (7FD0) was used; the allosteric pocket is flexible and the structure is bound to one chemotype, so other chemotypes may be under-scored.
5. Measured IC50 values are ChEMBL records; assay conditions vary across source studies and were not re-verified compound by compound.
6. The clinical failure in RA is not explained by this work — exposure, indication and target biology are all live explanations, and docking addresses none of them.
7. The Islamic-canon sweep used the literature-map summaries of Ibn Sina's Canon and Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi; the full Arabic texts were not read, and the canon has no concept of a kinase — the sweep is historical context only.

## 6. Conclusion

Four ChEMBL-recorded sub-nanomolar RIPK1 ligands bind the allosteric pocket (7FD0) with scores of −8.57 to −9.90 kcal/mol against −9.80 kcal/mol for re-docked GSK2982772, with tight pose consistency and no PAINS alerts. The best lead is GSK2982772 itself, the rest are its close analogues, the margins are inside Vina noise, and the reference drug already failed to improve rheumatoid arthritis in 52 randomised patients — so this is not evidence that any analogue beats GSK2982772, it is evidence that the chemotype is sound and the open question is the indication: kinase and necroptosis assays plus a brain-penetration screen, aimed at neurodegeneration or sepsis rather than RA. That test, not another docking run, is the next step.
