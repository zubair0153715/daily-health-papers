## Abstract

Malaria still killed an estimated 597,000 people in 2023, from 263 million cases (WHO World Malaria Report 2024), and artemisinin partial resistance makes a new mechanism a priority. Plasmodium falciparum phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase (PfPI4K) is the target of MMV390048, the first PI4K inhibitor to reach clinical development: it clears blood-stage parasites at 28 nM in vitro, blocks liver stages and gametocytes, and cured 8 of 8 P. vivax patients at day 14 on a single 120 mg dose, before its Phase 2a trial was stopped over emerging teratogenicity data. We asked whether the standard computational benchmark used in this series, ChEMBL potency retrieval plus AutoDock Vina docking, can evaluate this target at all. It cannot, and the failure is structural, not technical: ChEMBL holds no target carrying the PfPI4K accession Q8I406, and no experimental structure of PfPI4K exists in the PDB, so docking was skipped rather than faked. A first pipeline run that ignored this returned 26 human maltase inhibitors as PfPI4K leads and was discarded. What could be computed honestly was computed: MMV390048 is drug-like (MW 393.39, logP 3.82, zero Lipinski violations, PAINS-clean) with a weak ToxGuard read-across (max similarity 0.382); four close 2D analogues (Tanimoto 0.72–0.79) are likewise clean but have no published PfPI4K activity. Falsifiable claim: the 2-aminopyridine scaffold's future depends on an enzyme assay, not more docking. If none of the four analogues inhibits PfPI4K below 1 micromolar, the similarity neighbourhood is a dead end; if one does, it is the backup series that MMV390048's termination left behind. The falsifier is a single PfPI4K enzyme assay, which no Pakistani laboratory currently runs.

## 1. Introduction

Malaria killed an estimated 597,000 people in 2023, from 263 million cases, with about 11 million more cases than in 2022 and deaths nearly unchanged (WHO World Malaria Report 2024). Artemisinin partial resistance, now confirmed in Africa as well as Southeast Asia, means the field cannot rely on the artemisinin combination therapies that carry almost all treatment. A drug with a new mechanism, active against more than one life-cycle stage, is the stated priority of the Medicines for Malaria Venture (MMV) target product profiles: a single-dose cure, or a chemoprotective agent, that also blocks transmission.

Phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase (PI4K) is that kind of target. The parasite enzyme (PfPI4K, UniProt Q8I406, gene PF3D7_0509800) synthesises phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate, the lipid that organises membrane trafficking and protein secretion; the parasite needs it in the blood stage, the liver stage and the gametocyte, so one inhibitor can in principle treat, prevent and block transmission. MMV390048, a 2-aminopyridine discovered from a phenotypic screen at the University of Cape Town's H3D centre, was the first PI4K inhibitor to reach clinical development (Paquet et al. 2017). Its published profile is unusually complete: P. falciparum NF54 IC50 28 nM and IC90 40 nM, with a steep inhibition curve and only a 1.5-fold IC50 range across multidrug-resistant clinical isolates, suggesting low cross-resistance risk; a log parasite reduction ratio of 2.7 per 48-hour cycle; late-stage gametocyte IC50 285 nM; SCID-mouse ED90 0.57 mg/kg at day 7; full chemoprotection in monkeys; and delayed relapse (though not elimination of late hypnozoites) in the P. cynomolgi monkey model. Target validation was genetic and chemoproteomic: parasites selected for resistance carry PfPI4K mutations H1484Y (IC50 62.8 nM) and S1320L (57.7 nM) against a Dd2 parent of 22.7 nM, and chemoproteomics identified PfPI4K as the molecular target, with no binding to the human orthologues PI4KA (P42356) or PI4KB (Q9UBF8).

The clinical story then stopped. Phase 1 (Barnes et al. 2020) found single oral doses up to 120 mg generally well tolerated, rapidly absorbed, with a long half-life, though the volunteer infection study saw rapid recrudescence at the subtherapeutic 20 mg dose. The Phase 2a study in Ethiopia (2023) gave a single 120 mg dose to eight adult men with P. vivax malaria: adequate clinical and parasitological response at day 14 was 100% (8/8), asexual parasites cleared by 66 hours and gametocytes by 78 hours — and then the study was not completed, for operational reasons and emerging teratogenicity data. A 2022 rat study reported diaphragmatic hernias after a malarial PI4K inhibitor, with a potential link to mammalian kinase inhibition. MMV390048's trial is listed as terminated. The best-validated new antimalarial mechanism of the decade has no drug and no backup series in the clinic.

**Falsifiable question (stated before the results):** can the standard computational pipeline of this series — ChEMBL target resolution, potency retrieval, and Vina docking — evaluate PfPI4K at all, and if not, what honest computation is possible on the scaffold that remains? The claim is that the pipeline cannot evaluate the target, because the target is absent from both ChEMBL and the PDB, and that the only defensible computation is a property and similarity analysis of MMV390048 itself. The claim is refuted if a ChEMBL target carrying Q8I406, or an experimental PfPI4K structure, is produced — either would let the standard benchmark run.

### 2.2 Target verification, and the discarded wrong-target run

PfPI4K was resolved by accession, not by text search: UniProt Q8I406, 1-phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase (EC 2.7.1.67), Plasmodium falciparum (isolate 3D7), zero PDB cross-references. A ChEMBL target query by component accession (`target_components__accession=Q8I406`) returned total_count 0 on 17 November 2026, and a pref_name search returned seven PI4K targets, all human or bovine. An RCSB search by reference-sequence accession Q8I406 returned no entry. The standard pipeline run (`run_docking.py --target Q8I406 --ref MMV390048`) therefore had no ChEMBL target to retrieve from; its fallback text search silently substituted CHEMBL2074 — human maltase-glucoamylase — and returned 55 bioactivities and 26 "leads" that are maltase inhibitors. That run is discarded in full and preserved as `dock-HP-067-2026-11-17-WRONG-TARGET.json`. It is reported here because it is the paper's first finding: a text-search fallback in a drug-discovery pipeline can manufacture a complete, plausible-looking, entirely wrong result for a target that does not exist in the database, and the only defence is the accession check.

### 2.4 Honesty limits

No docking was run, so there is no docking score in this paper and no ranking. The four analogues have no published PfPI4K activity; similarity to MMV390048 is not evidence of activity. ToxGuard's read-across for MMV390048 rests on neighbours at maximum similarity 0.382, which is weak — its scores are a flag, not a safety assessment. Human PI4K bioactivity counts describe the human enzymes, not the parasite enzyme, and say nothing about MMV390048's selectivity beyond the published chemoproteomic result.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The target is absent from both databases the pipeline trusts

ChEMBL holds no target with component accession Q8I406 (0 targets, verified live). The seven PI4K targets it does hold are human PI4K alpha (CHEMBL3667), beta (CHEMBL3268), type 2-alpha (CHEMBL2251) and type 2-beta (CHEMBL3220), bovine PI4K alpha (CHEMBL3185), a human PI4K protein family (CHEMBL2096619) and a putative human PI4K alpha-like protein (CHEMBL4105789). The PDB holds no structure for Q8I406 (0 UniProt cross-references; RCSB accession search empty). PfPI4K is therefore unevaluable by the standard benchmark: there is no potency table to retrieve and no structure to dock on. This is not a data-quality footnote. The most clinically advanced PI4K inhibitor in history has a target that the two databases a computational screen trusts most simply do not contain, because the target's evidence lives in papers and patents, not in ChEMBL's target table.

### 3.2 The wrong-target run, and what it would have published

Had the accession check been skipped, the pipeline would have published a benchmark of 26 human maltase-glucoamylase inhibitors as PfPI4K leads, with docking scores on a maltase structure, under an MMV390048 heading. The run completed with "PIPELINE OK: True". Only the target line — CHEMBL2074 where a PfPI4K target should have been — betrayed it. This is the same trap that produced discarded runs earlier in this series (maltase substituted for GyrB, for DHFR, for PTP1B): the ChEMBL text search returns maltase-glucoamylase first for short queries, and a pipeline that falls back to text search without an accession check will silently evaluate the wrong enzyme. The rule this paper states, and follows, is: **no accession match, no benchmark** — a target without a verified ChEMBL target and a verified structure gets a literature-and-property paper, never a docking table.

### 3.3 MMV390048: drug-like, PAINS-clean, weakly covered by ToxGuard

MMV390048 (PubChem CID 53311393) computes as a clean drug-like molecule: MW 393.39, logP 3.82, 1 hydrogen-bond donor, 5 acceptors, TPSA 85.94 Å², 3 rotatable bonds, zero Lipinski violations, Veber pass, no PAINS alerts. ToxGuard's kNN read-across (k = 15) found no close neighbour — maximum similarity 0.382 — and returned low scores on all twelve Tox21 assays (highest: NR-ER 0.172, NR-ER-LBD 0.094, SR-ARE 0.093, all others 0.0). Read honestly, that is not a clean bill of health; it is an admission that the model has almost nothing to read across from, at a similarity where read-across is unreliable. The compound's real safety signal is not computational at all: it is the rat diaphragmatic-hernia finding and the teratogenicity data that stopped the Phase 2a trial. No Tox21 score in this paper should be quoted against that animal evidence.

### 3.4 Four close analogues, none with a published PfPI4K measurement

PubChem 2D similarity (threshold 85) returned 20 compounds; the top four distinct analogues by RDKit Tanimoto, after excluding the self-match, are all 2-aminopyridines that keep MMV390048's methylsulfonylphenyl half and vary the trifluoromethyl aryl ring:

All four pass Lipinski and Veber and carry no PAINS alerts; their ToxGuard read-across is as weak as MMV390048's (maximum similarity 0.373–0.468). The structural lesson is narrow but real: the series tolerates moving the trifluoromethyl group around the phenyl ring (para, meta) and replacing the pyridyl ring with a phenyl, and it tolerates a second methylsulfonyl group at the cost of polarity (TPSA 107.19, logP 2.80 for CID 53312146). What is **not** known about any of them is the only thing that matters: none has a published PfPI4K measurement, and none is presented here as a lead. They are a prioritised assay list, not a result.

### 3.5 The human PI4K context, and why it does not settle selectivity

Human PI4KA (CHEMBL3667) holds 48 bioactivities at pChEMBL ≥ 5, the most potent at IC50 0.32 nM (CHEMBL3121323, pChEMBL 9.50); human PI4KB (CHEMBL3268) holds 45, the most potent at 0.98 nM (CHEMBL3923414, pChEMBL 9.01). Sub-nanomolar human PI4K inhibitors exist in number. That is the selectivity problem in one line: the human enzymes are eminently druggable, and a parasite inhibitor must thread the gap between PfPI4K and two human enzymes that bind the same lipid substrate. Paquet et al.'s chemoproteomic result — no binding to human PI4KA/PI4KB, with human PIP4K2C as the main human target at similar affinity to PfPI4K — is the only selectivity evidence for MMV390048, and it is assay evidence, not a database count. The ChEMBL counts cannot substitute for it, and the rat hernia signal is a reminder that the mammalian kinase liability is real even when the intended human orthologues are spared.

### 3.6 The field has not stopped

Two recent papers keep the target alive: 2,8-disubstituted-1,5-naphthyridines as dual PfPI4K inhibitors (J Med Chem 2024, PMID 38918002), a new chemotype beyond the 2-aminopyridines; and the ATM kinase inhibitor AZD0156 reported as a potent PfPI4Kβ inhibitor (Angew Chem Int Ed 2025, PMID 40317875), a repurposing lead from an oncology drug. A 2023 review (Trends Parasitol, PMID 37385921) frames the open question as opportunity or liability: kinase inhibitors for malaria carry mammalian-kinase risk by definition, and the field has not decided whether the PI4K class can be made safe enough. MMV390048's termination is a data point in that debate, not its conclusion.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe: the bottleneck is the database, not the chemistry

This paper's finding is not about MMV390048's potency, which was established in 2017, but about the infrastructure around it. A target can be genetically validated, chemoproteomically confirmed, clinically tested to Phase 2a — and still be invisible to the standard computational pipeline, because ChEMBL has no target row for it and the PDB has no structure. The pipeline's failure mode is worse than an error message: it silently substitutes a different enzyme and returns a clean-looking result. Any group screening antimalarial targets by text search should assume this has happened to them at least once and check by accession.

### 4.2 Why the analogues must not be over-read

Four clean, drug-like, similar molecules are not four leads. Similarity to an active compound is the weakest form of evidence in medicinal chemistry, and here it is unaccompanied by any enzyme data, any whole-cell data, and any structural model to explain why the changes should preserve binding. The correct reading of §3.4 is as an assay shopping list ordered by similarity: test CID 53311566 first (closest, Tanimoto 0.791), then 53312146 (the polarity variant, the most informative single change), then the two 0.723 analogues. The test that matters is a PfPI4K enzyme assay at 1 µM and 10 µM, with MMV390048 as the control and a human PI4KB counter-screen in the same run.

### 4.3 Association only

This is a retrospective database audit and property analysis, not a causal experiment. It shows that PfPI4K is absent from ChEMBL and the PDB, that MMV390048 is drug-like and PAINS-clean, and that four similar molecules exist without published activity. It does not show that any analogue inhibits PfPI4K, that MMV390048 is safe, or that PI4K inhibition is clinically viable. The falsifier names the experiment that could change each of those statements.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. **CID 53311566 inhibits PfPI4K below 1 µM.** Falsifier: recombinant PfPI4K enzyme assay; IC50 ≥ 10 µM refutes it, and refutes the similarity neighbourhood with it.
2. **CID 53312146, the bis-sulfonyl analogue, is the most selective of the four against human PI4KB.** Falsifier: paired PfPI4K / human PI4KB enzyme assays; a selectivity ratio below 10 refutes it.
3. **A PfPI4K structure or ChEMBL target will appear within two years.** Falsifier: none needed — this is a dated prediction, checkable in November 2028; if neither exists, the infrastructure gap is permanent for practical purposes.
4. **The rat diaphragmatic-hernia signal tracks mammalian kinase inhibition, not PI4K inhibition itself.** Falsifier: a PI4K inhibitor with no mammalian kinase binding at 10 µM that still produces hernias in rat would refute it; one that does not would support the mammalian-kinase link and clear the class.

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–2): obtain the four analogues commercially (all are in PubChem with suppliers listed) and confirm identity and purity by LC-MS. Stage 2 (weeks 3–6): recombinant PfPI4K enzyme assay (ADP-Glo or equivalent) at 10 µM, 1 µM and 0.1 µM, MMV390048 as positive control, with a human PI4KB counter-screen in parallel. Stage 3 (weeks 7–10): whole-cell P. falciparum 3D7 assay for any analogue active below 1 µM, plus the H1484Y and S1320L mutant lines as a resistance check. Stage 4 (weeks 11–12): report all four results, including the negatives — a dead end is a result. Total cost is one enzyme assay and four compounds, not a screening campaign.

## 5. Limitations

1. **No docking, by design.** No PfPI4K structure exists; no docking score is reported and no structural claim is made.
2. **No ChEMBL potency data for the parasite target.** Every PfPI4K number in this paper is a literature value, not a database retrieval.
3. **No lab data.** No assay was run; the four analogues are untested.
4. **Weak ToxGuard coverage.** Maximum neighbour similarity 0.382 for MMV390048 means the Tox21 scores are unreliable and must not be quoted as safety evidence.
5. **The Phase 2a record is incomplete.** The Ethiopia trial was not completed; its published report covers eight P. vivax patients, and the teratogenicity data that contributed to stopping it are described, not fully published.
6. **Islamic canon gaps (named, per the programme's rule).** The *Canon* and *Kitab al-Hawi* were checked through named works, not read in full; the full 23-volume *Hawi* exists in English only in selections, and most classical Arabic medical manuscripts remain undigitised.

## 6. Policy implications

**Decision-maker: the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP), the National Institute of Health, and the Directorate of Malaria Control — and, for the method rule, every public funder of computational drug discovery in Pakistan.** Three exact changes, each with its falsifier:

1. **Fund one PfPI4K enzyme assay in Pakistan, at a single reference laboratory, and publish the protocol.** The four analogues in §3.4 cost one assay to test, and the country has no domestic route from a computational antimalarial claim to a measured number. If the assay shows all four analogues inactive above 10 µM, the scaffold is closed and the money is still well spent — a dead end found in weeks beats a decade of untested docking. Reverse this only if a cheaper regional assay (e.g. through MMV's network) becomes available to Pakistani groups.
2. **Require accession-verified targets in every publicly funded computational screen.** Any screening proposal must state the UniProt accession and the ChEMBL target ID it resolved to, and any run that falls back to text search must declare it. This paper's discarded maltase run is the evidence: without that rule, public money can fund a complete benchmark of the wrong enzyme. Reverse this only if ChEMBL's text search is shown to return the correct target first for a blinded set of parasite accessions.
3. **Do not restart MMV390048, but do not abandon the PI4K class either.** The teratogenicity signal and the terminated trial argue against reviving this compound; the 2024 naphthyridine series and the 2025 AZD0156 result argue the target is still live. The correct public position is to fund the assay in (1) and let the analogues decide. **Islamic principle behind the recommendations:** preservation of life (*hifz al-nafs*, Al-Ghazali, *Al-Mustasfa*; Al-Shatibi, *Al-Muwafaqat*) — malaria kills nearly 600,000 people a year, so a screening method that mis-ranks antimalarials is a failure to protect life, not a technical footnote; and stewardship (*khilafah*, Qur'an 2:30) — public research funds are a trust, and a steward does not spend a trust on a benchmark of the wrong enzyme. Where the Islamic sources and the modern evidence point the same way, this paper says so; they do here.

## 7. Conclusion

PfPI4K is the target of the most clinically advanced new-mechanism antimalarial of the decade, and it is absent from ChEMBL and the PDB: no target row, no structure, no docking possible. The standard pipeline's answer to that absence was to silently benchmark human maltase instead, a run discarded here in full. What remains is honest and small: MMV390048 is drug-like and PAINS-clean (MW 393.39, logP 3.82); four close analogues exist, all clean, none tested; and a single PfPI4K enzyme assay — which no Pakistani laboratory currently runs — would settle whether the scaffold has a future. The memorable number is **zero**: zero ChEMBL targets, zero PDB structures, for a target that reached Phase 2a.
