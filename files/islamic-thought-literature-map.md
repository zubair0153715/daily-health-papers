## 2. Field map — which Islamic works matter for which paper field

- **Health / medicine / pharmacy:** Ibn Sina, *Canon* (esp. Book II materia medica, Book V compound drugs); Al-Razi, *Kitab al-Hawi* (23 vols of clinical observation) and *On Smallpox and Measles* (first clinical distinction); Al-Zahrawi, *Kitab al-Tasrif* (surgery, instruments); Ibn al-Nafis, *Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun* (pulmonary circulation, 1242). Use: historical controls and drug-discovery leads — e.g. test whether a Canon-listed plant's claimed indication matches a modern target (docking + literature), and report the match or mismatch honestly.
- **Brain / neuroscience / psychology:** Al-Ghazali, *Ihya* (qalb, ruh, nafs, aql — the four terms of the self); Ibn Sina, *Kitab al-Shifa* (psychology of the soul, the "Floating Man" argument); Al-Razi on mental illness and the doctor–patient relationship. Use: falsifiable models of attention, emotion, habit — never as proof, always as a hypothesis source.
- **Space / astronomy / physics:** Al-Biruni, *Kitab al-Hind* and *Al-Qanun al-Mas'udi* (Earth's circumference, planetary motion); Al-Battani, *Kitab al-Zij*; Ibn al-Haytham, *Kitab al-Manazir* (the experimental method itself — light, optics, camera obscura); Al-Tusi, *Tadhkira* (the Tusi couple, later used by Copernicus). Use: the Islamic experimental method as the paper's method ancestor, and historical measurements as benchmarks for modern data.
- **Natural Science (climate, energy, water, environment):** Nasr, *Man and Nature* (the spiritual crisis behind the ecological crisis); Guessoum, *Islam's Quantum Question* (science–religion method); Al-Biruni (geodesy, earth science). Use: khilafah/stewardship as the policy frame — nature as a trust, not a resource to exhaust.
- **Social Science (economics, sociology, politics, education):** Ibn Khaldun, *Muqaddimah* (asabiyyah, dynastic cycles, taxation and the Laffer-type curve, education); Al-Mawardi, *Al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyya* (constitutional theory); Chapra, *Islam and the Economic Challenge*; Siddiqi, *Banking Without Interest*; Usmani, *Introduction to Islamic Finance*; Al-Shatibi, *Al-Muwafaqat* (maqasid); Ibn Ashur, *Maqasid al-Shari'ah al-Islamiyyah*; Iqbal, *Reconstruction* (ijtihad as movement); Al-Ghazali, *Ihya* (education of the self); Maududi, *Tafhim al-Qur'an* (Islam as a social system).

## 3. Where the books are available (verified 14 Nov 2026)

**Freely readable online (verified):**
- **archive.org (Internet Archive):** the single best source. Verified holdings: the Muqaddimah (Rosenthal translation, Princeton 1958/1969 — borrow), Iqbal's *Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam* (full text, opensource collection), Nasr's *Islam and the Plight of Modern Man* (full PDF, 2nd ed. 2001), and hundreds of classical and modern Islamic texts in English, Urdu and Arabic. First place to check for any title.
- **al-islam.org:** large free library of Islamic texts in English/Urdu/Arabic (Qur'an, hadith, tafsir, history, philosophy) — strong on Shia and Sunni classics; check per title.
- **Open Library (openlibrary.org):** catalogue of 54 editions of the Muqaddimah and many classics — but note: the Princeton Muqaddimah was "currently unavailable" for borrow at the last check; use archive.org first.
- **Google Books:** previews and full view for many public-domain works; useful for verifying a quote's page.
- **ghazali.org:** free English translations of Al-Ghazali (Ihya selections, *Al-Munqidh*, *Mishkat al-Anwar*).

**Honest gap list — do NOT claim access to what is not available:**
- Most of Al-Razi's *Kitab al-Hawi* exists in English only in selections; the full 23 volumes are Arabic (and Latin *Continens*, 1282).
- Al-Zahrawi's *Kitab al-Tasrif* has no complete modern English translation; only partial/surgical selections.
- Ibn al-Nafis's *Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun* survives as an Arabic manuscript (rediscovered Berlin, 1924); English exists only in partial translation.
- Many classical Arabic manuscripts are undigitised — held in libraries (Süleymaniye, Istanbul; Dar al-Kutub, Cairo; the British Library) with no online copy. A paper must name this gap in its Limitations, never pretend the text was read.
- Project Gutenberg holds almost no classical Islamic canon — do not cite it as a source for these works.

## 4. Islamic policy principles for papers

Each principle below is a test a policy paper must pass. Cite the Islamic source as a primary source with a real reference (Qur'an verse, hadith collection, or a named book from §1) — never paraphrase from memory.

1. **Maqasid al-shariah — the five objectives** (Al-Ghazali, *Al-Mustasfa*; systematised by Al-Shatibi, *Al-Muwafaqat*): preservation of religion (din), life (nafs), intellect ('aql), lineage (nasl), wealth (mal). *Paper use:* every policy proposal is scored against all five — a policy that saves wealth but destroys intellect (e.g. addictive design) fails.
2. **Maslaha — public interest** (Al-Shatibi): the law exists to secure benefit and prevent harm. *Paper use:* the paper's cost–benefit analysis must count benefits to the poor and the unborn, not only GDP.
3. **'Adl — justice** (Qur'an 4:135, 16:90): justice as the purpose of the state. *Paper use:* any policy is tested for who it harms — if the burden falls on the poorest, the design is wrong.
4. **Shura — consultation** (Qur'an 42:38): decisions by consultation, not decree. *Paper use:* the paper names who was consulted and who was excluded; a policy designed without its subjects is flagged.
5. **Khilafah — stewardship of nature** (Qur'an 2:30; Nasr, *Man and Nature*): humans are trustees of the earth. *Paper use:* environmental papers price depletion as a debt to future generations, not as free income.
6. **Prohibition of riba (interest) and gharar (excessive uncertainty)** (Qur'an 2:275–279; Chapra 1992; Usmani 2002): money must be tied to real activity and shared risk. *Paper use:* finance papers must offer a risk-sharing alternative (mudarabah/musharakah), not just criticise interest.
7. **Zakat and waqf as redistribution instruments** (Qur'an 9:60; Chapra): zakat is a right of the poor, waqf is perpetual public capital. *Paper use:* poverty papers test zakat/waqf mechanisms against cash transfers with real numbers.
8. **Ijtihad — independent reasoning** (Iqbal, *Reconstruction*, 1930): the principle of movement in Islam. *Paper use:* the paper itself is an act of ijtihad — it must reason from sources to a new answer, not repeat a fatwa.

**Citation rule:** every Islamic claim in a paper carries a verifiable reference (book + chapter/section, or Qur'an surah:ayah, or hadith collection + number). A claim sourced only from "Islamic scholars say" is removed.

## 6. Sweep rule for paper runs (standing order, 14 Nov 2026)

Every paper's literature sweep must check three legs, in this order:

1. **(a) The modern scientific literature** — the daily digest + targeted PubMed / bioRxiv / arXiv / economics and policy sources (per the method brief's Harvard/MIT/Oxford standard).
2. **(b) The relevant Islamic canon from this map** — NAMED works, not vague "Islamic scholars": e.g. "Ibn Khaldun, Muqaddimah, ch. 3" or "Chapra, Islam and the Economic Challenge (1992), ch. 5". If the canon work was not actually read (no digitised copy — see §3 gap list), the paper says so in its Limitations.
3. **(c) The policy-shaping professors' method** — `files/method-brief-policy-shaping-professors.md`: one falsifiable question + falsifier, causal identification named, mechanism, effect size + uncertainty, policy implications naming the decision-maker.

**The honest limit — stated plainly.** "Every book ever written" is literally impossible: the Islamic tradition contains thousands of manuscripts, many untranslated and undigitised (§3 gap list), and no single person or agent can read them all. The rule is therefore: **the full available digitised canon + every canonical work relevant to the question, with gaps named in the paper's Limitations.** A paper that read three relevant classical works and names the two it could not access is honest; a paper that claims "all Islamic thought" without naming works is not. The canon grows: whenever a run finds a relevant work missing from this map, add it here with its verified date.
