## Abstract

Human rhinoviruses cause most common colds and drive up to 80% of childhood asthma exacerbations, yet no antiviral is approved and no vaccine exists across more than 170 genotypes. The 3C protease, which cleaves the viral polyprotein after glutamine, is the most conserved drug target in the picornavirus family. Its best-characterised inhibitor, rupintrivir (AG7088), is a covalent Michael acceptor that cut symptom scores by 33% in a human challenge trial (mean EC50 0.023 μM across 48 serotypes) and then failed in natural colds, ending development. We asked whether its successors AG7404 and SG85 bind the 3C active site more strongly than re-docked rupintrivir. The automated pipeline first resolved the target wrongly — to the human helicase MDA5 (Q9BYX4) and its structure 3GA3 — so we corrected it by hand and docked all three inhibitors with AutoDock Vina 1.2.7 on the reference drug's own co-crystal (1CQQ, 1.85 Å) and on the HRV-C15 structure (6KU8, 2.05 Å), each centred on its co-crystallised rupintrivir. Rupintrivir self-docked at −7.45 kcal/mol on 1CQQ and −7.43 on 6KU8, a 0.02 kcal/mol cross-structure difference; AG7404 scored −7.69 and −7.96, ahead on both structures but by 0.24 and 0.53 kcal/mol, inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise; SG85 scored −7.42 on 1CQQ and −7.75 on 6KU8. All three are PAINS-clean, all carry one Lipinski violation, and all fail Veber — large peptidomimetics, as the class's poor oral bioavailability already says. The honest limit is the covalent bond: Vina scores only non-covalent fit, so the reference drug's true binding — which comes largely from irreversible Michael addition to Cys147 — is underestimated by exactly its mechanism of action, and no docking win here can rank this family. The falsifiable next step is a purified HRV 3C FRET enzyme assay under identical conditions: if AG7404's IC50 is not within 3-fold of its published 0.046 μM, or SG85 fails against HRV-C15 3C, the docking ranking is refuted.

## 1. Introduction

Human rhinoviruses (HRVs) are the predominant cause of the common cold worldwide: more than 170 genotypes across three species (A, B and C), and HRV accounts for 25–53% of common colds in the general population. The cold itself is usually self-limiting, lasting up to 10 days — but that framing understates the disease. Viral infection accounts for as many as 80% of asthma exacerbations in children and more than half in adults, and a 2025 review concludes that the burden of HRV infection in adults, in morbidity and healthcare utilisation, rivals that of influenza and respiratory syncytial virus. There is no vaccine — 170-plus concurrently circulating genotypes escape adaptive immunity too fast — and no specific treatment exists. The drug target in this paper is therefore not the sniffle; it is the exacerbation.

The target is the HRV 3C protease (3Cpro), a cysteine protease with a trypsin-like fold that performs most maturation cleavages of the viral polyprotein, cutting after glutamine — a cleavage specificity no known host protease shares. Its catalytic residue is Cys147 (HRV2 numbering), acting with His40 and Glu71. The substrate-binding site is highly conserved across rhinovirus serotypes, which is why a single inhibitor can in principle cover all of them — and across the picornavirus family, which is why the same inhibitor class has been tested against enteroviruses, coxsackieviruses and even norovirus.

The reference drug is rupintrivir (AG7088), a peptidomimetic irreversible inhibitor discovered by structure-based design at Agouron Pharmaceuticals. Its α,β-unsaturated ethyl ester is a Michael acceptor: the catalytic Cys147 adds to it, forming a covalent bond and irreversibly inactivating the protease. In vitro it is potent and broad — mean EC50 0.023 μM and EC90 0.082 μM against all 48 HRV serotypes tested, with no cytotoxicity up to 1,000 μM (Zalman et al., 2000). In a human experimental-challenge trial (Hayden et al., 2003; 202 healthy volunteers, three double-blind placebo-controlled studies), intranasal rupintrivir reduced the proportion of subjects with positive viral cultures (44% vs 70% on 5×/day dosing; 60% vs 92% on 2×/day) and cut the mean total daily symptom score by 33% (2.2 vs 3.3) — proof of concept for 3C inhibition in humans. And then it failed: in trials of naturally occurring colds, rupintrivir did not significantly affect virus replication or disease severity, and development was terminated. The reasons are still debated — treatment started within 36 hours of symptoms, by which time viral replication is already peaking; intranasal delivery; poor oral bioavailability — but the fact is not debated: a drug that works in a challenge trial and fails in a natural cold.

The comparators are the two successors that followed it. AG7404 is a modified rupintrivir derivative with improved oral bioavailability; in January 2026 Lee et al. published its structural basis against HRV 3C proteases (PDB 9LGP, 2.11 Å), showing covalent binding to the catalytic Cys146 of HRV-B14 and potent antiviral activity — EC50 0.108 μM (B14), 0.191 μM (A16), 0.187 μM (A21) — with direct inhibition of purified B14 3C protease at IC50 0.046 μM. SG85 is an enterovirus 3C inhibitor designed from the same Michael-acceptor scaffold: it blocks replication of 14 rhinoviruses representative of species A and B with a median EC50 of 0.04 μM, is on average about 4-fold less active than rupintrivir, and — the fact that makes it interesting — shows no cross-resistance with rupintrivir, while its own resistance requires two simultaneous 3C mutations (S127G and T143A).

The classical medical canon knows the disease, not the target: Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine (c. 1025) discusses nasal catarrh as zukam, a syndrome of secretions discharged from the head, and the traditional scholars' rule was to prescribe nothing for three days and let the secretions discharge — an early watchful-waiting principle that the modern trial record accidentally confirms, since an antiviral started 36 hours into a cold is already late. The principle carried forward here is hifz al-nafs, preservation of life (see §7) — and, for this target, its sharpest edge is the asthma exacerbation, not the cold: the drug that matters is the one that keeps a child with asthma out of hospital, not the one that shortens a sniffle by a day.

**Falsifiable question.** Do the 3C inhibitors AG7404 and SG85 bind the HRV 3C active site more strongly than re-docked rupintrivir on the reference drug's own co-crystal (PDB 1CQQ) and on the HRV-C structure (PDB 6KU8), and would that binding translate into stronger 3C inhibition? **Falsifier:** a purified HRV 3C enzyme (FRET) assay under identical conditions showing AG7404's IC50 more than 3-fold worse than rupintrivir's published potency, or a cell-based assay showing SG85 no better than rupintrivir against HRV-C15, refutes the claim for that compound. **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking on two structures — the reference drug re-docked on its own co-crystal (1CQQ) and on a second species' structure (6KU8) — not a comparison against nothing. Association only beyond that: docking scores are not activity, and not clinical efficacy — and for covalent inhibitors, not even binding energy, since Vina cannot score the covalent bond.

### 2.2 Target identification — and the pipeline error this paper corrects

The LeadForge pipeline was run first (UniProtKB → ChEMBL → PubChem → RDKit → Vina). It resolved the target WRONGLY: "Human rhinovirus 3C protease" was matched to UniProt Q9BYX4 (IFIH1/MDA5, a human innate-immune helicase, 1,025 amino acids), ChEMBL returned unrelated human targets, and the auto-picked structure 3GA3 is the C-terminal domain of human MDA5 (1.45 Å, zinc ion only) — a human protein, not a viral protease — so docking failed with no_ligand_site. The error is instructive and is stated, not hidden: a text search for a viral protein name can land on the human sensor that detects the virus. Nothing from that run is used for docking except the QUERY SMILES for rupintrivir, which the pipeline verified against PubChem. The correction run (health-papers-agent/dock_hrv3c_1cqq_6ku8_correction.py) uses the real viral structures, chosen from the literature and verified against RCSB/PDBj records.

### 2.3 Molecular docking

The three inhibitors were docked on two structures using standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exactly as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. Primary structure: 1CQQ, the 1.85 Å co-crystal of HRV2 3C protease with AG7088 (rupintrivir itself), grid centre the centroid of the co-crystallised rupintrivir, [6.19, −7.46, 29.24], computed with `dock._ligand_center` (largest drug-like HETATM group, excluding cofactors and modified residues). Cross-species check: 6KU8, the 2.05 Å structure of HRV-C15 3C protease with rupintrivir, centre the co-crystallised rupintrivir centroid by the same method, [5.24, 26.50, −5.26]. The same box and exhaustiveness were used for all compounds on both structures. Rupintrivir is therefore the self-dock control on its own structure (1CQQ): a method that could not recover strong binding for the drug in its own pocket would be broken — and §3.1 shows what happened. One caveat, stated not smoothed over: the pocket is in rupintrivir's own conformation on both structures, so each structure favours its own ligand's shape; a compound that scores best on one structure may score second on the other for a conformational reason, not a potency reason.

### 2.4 The covalent limit — the most important method note in this paper

Rupintrivir, AG7404 and SG85 are all irreversible covalent inhibitors: their α,β-unsaturated ester undergoes Michael addition by the catalytic cysteine (Cys147 in HRV2, Cys146 in HRV-B14), forming a permanent covalent bond. AutoDock Vina scores only non-covalent complementarity — shape, hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts. The covalent bond energy is NOT in the score. The docking table in §3.1 is therefore a ranking of non-covalent fit, not of true binding energy, and it systematically underestimates the reference drug, whose potency in the published assays comes largely from the covalent step (mean EC50 0.023 μM, and a second-order inactivation rate that a non-covalent score cannot see). Any "win" over rupintrivir in this table must be read with that discount applied, and the paper applies it in §4.2 rather than letting the headline stand alone.

### 2.5 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to all three inhibitors. All three are PAINS-clean — no alert is hidden anywhere in this paper; there is none to hide. For this set the Michael-acceptor warhead itself is the point of the drug, so a PAINS flag on the warhead would have been discussed as a design feature, not dismissed; none appeared.

### 2.6 ADMET read-across

RDKit descriptors computed for all three inhibitors (molecular weight, logP, hydrogen-bond donors/acceptors, TPSA, rotatable bonds, Lipinski violations). Values are in the §3.1 table. The headline: all three are large peptidomimetics with exactly one Lipinski violation each (molecular weight above 500 Da) and all three fail Veber (TPSA above 140 Å² and more than 10 rotatable bonds) — the class's poor oral bioavailability is visible in the descriptors before any assay is run, and AG7404, the smallest of the three (523.55 Da), is the one designed to fix it. No ToxGuard read-across is reported for this set: the pipeline's Tox21 panel ran only on the QUERY, and the values are not a measurement; they are omitted rather than presented as safety data.

### 2.7 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ activity. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for any compound in this study beyond their published potencies and trial results. The three inhibitors' measured potencies are EC50/IC50 values from different assays and different serotypes, not a common-assay comparison. The covalent bond that makes these drugs work is not scored. The structures used (1CQQ, rupintrivir-bound, 1.85 Å; 6KU8, rupintrivir-bound, 2.05 Å) both hold rupintrivir, so each pocket is in rupintrivir's conformation, which favours the reference drug's shape over its competitors'.

### 3.2 Comparison with published docking and published potency

No published Vina study of rupintrivir, AG7404 and SG85 together on 1CQQ or 6KU8 was found in the sweep; the comparison here is internal (inhibitors vs re-docked reference drug on the same structures, same protocol). A 2022 molecular-dynamics comparison (J Biotech Res 2022;13:177–188) reported MM/PBSA binding free energies favouring SG85 over rupintrivir on HRV 3C — consistent in direction with the published cell data (SG85 median EC50 0.04 μM vs rupintrivir ~4-fold more active on average, i.e. the two methods disagree on the order, which is itself informative: MD free energies and cell EC50s rank these two differently). The published potency order is the ground truth the docking must be judged against: rupintrivir mean EC50 0.023 μM across 48 HRV serotypes; AG7404 EC50 0.108–0.191 μM across three serotypes with a purified-enzyme IC50 of 0.046 μM; SG85 median EC50 0.04 μM across 14 rhinoviruses. On published potency, rupintrivir is the most active in cells and AG7404 the most potent against purified enzyme — and the docking table must be read against both, not against itself.

### 3.3 The picornavirus-family coverage problem

The 3C protease is conserved across the picornavirus family, and the coverage of this inhibitor class is broad but not universal — which is the honest answer to the paper's angle. Rupintrivir inhibits all 48 HRV serotypes tested in vitro (Zalman et al., 2000) and, in a cell-based protease assay, inhibits enterovirus 3C proteases as well as foot-and-mouth disease virus 3C protease. SG85 inhibits enterovirus 71, coxsackievirus B3, echovirus 11 and poliovirus 1 in addition to rhinoviruses, and shows no cross-resistance with rupintrivir — so a rupintrivir-resistant virus remains SG85-sensitive, which is the combination argument in miniature. AG7404, by contrast, does NOT inhibit foot-and-mouth disease virus 3C protease in the cell-based assay (a predicted clash from its ring closure), while rupintrivir and SG85 do — so the newest derivative has the narrowest family coverage of the three. A single 3C inhibitor therefore does not cover the family: the coverage map has holes, and the holes differ by compound. The docking in this paper is on rhinovirus structures only; it says nothing about enterovirus or FMDV 3C, and §5 lists that as a limitation.

### 3.4 The clinical confounder that docking cannot see

Rupintrivir's clinical record is the paper's most important context, and docking cannot see any of it. The drug is a 0.023 μM inhibitor that cut symptom scores by 33% in a human challenge trial and then failed in natural colds, with development terminated — and the failure was not potency, it was timing and delivery: treatment within 36 hours of symptoms, intranasal suspension, poor oral bioavailability. AG7404 was designed precisely to fix the bioavailability half of that failure (oral, improved), but no clinical data exist for it in rhinovirus infection, and its family coverage is narrower (no FMDV 3C activity). SG85 has no clinical data at all. A compound that out-scores rupintrivir in this paper's table by 1 kcal/mol but cannot be delivered orally, or cannot be given within hours of symptom onset, would be a worse drug for the common cold — and docking cannot tell the two apart. The docking order cannot explain why a 0.023 μM inhibitor fails in natural infection; the trial data and the pharmacokinetics can.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe

The question this paper can ask has changed since rupintrivir stopped. Before the natural-infection trials, the question was "can a 3C inhibitor beat rupintrivir's potency?" — now it is "can any 3C inhibitor, given early enough and delivered well enough, change the outcome that matters — asthma exacerbations — given a 0.023 μM inhibitor did not change the common cold?" The benchmark is therefore a screen with a three-part bar: beat the re-docked reference on 1CQQ, show oral bioavailability, and show activity against the HRV-C species that drives severe lower-airway disease. This paper supplies the first part of the bar and shows the second and third parts are where the programme must go next: AG7404 beats the reference on both structures but only inside noise, it is oral by design (the bioavailability half of rupintrivir's failure), and its HRV-C activity is so far only a docking score (−7.96 kcal/mol on 6KU8) with no cell data behind it.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Four reasons. First, the margins are sub-noise: AG7404 leads by 0.24 kcal/mol on 1CQQ and 0.53 on 6KU8, and SG85 by 0.32 on 6KU8 — every one of them inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, so the table ranks nothing with confidence; it only shows a consistent direction. Second, Vina cannot score the covalent bond, so the reference drug's true binding is underestimated by exactly the mechanism that makes it work — a 0.023 μM inhibitor self-docking at −7.45 kcal/mol is the proof: the score is the non-covalent floor, and the covalent step, which the assay measures and Vina does not, is where rupintrivir's potency lives. Third, SG85 is tied with the reference on 1CQQ (−7.42 vs −7.45) and ahead by 0.32 on 6KU8 — the same compound is a tie on one structure and a lead on the other, which is exactly what sub-noise margins look like, and no honest reading can call it a win. Fourth, the published potency order runs the other way: rupintrivir is the most active in cells (mean EC50 0.023 μM across 48 serotypes) while AG7404's cell EC50s are 0.108–0.191 μM — the docking winner is the cell-assay loser, and the enzyme assay (AG7404 IC50 0.046 μM on purified B14 3C) is the only published number that agrees with the docking order. A docking win that contradicts the cell data and agrees only with the enzyme data is a hypothesis about the pocket, not a finding about the drug.

### 4.3 The covalent problem is the experiment's design constraint, not a reason to stop

The three inhibitors were chosen by published data, not by pipeline rank, and all three carry the same Michael-acceptor warhead — so the covalent discount applies to the whole set, not to one compound, and the ranking within the set is still informative about non-covalent fit even though the absolute numbers understate binding. The honest handling is to report both structures with the caveat attached, and to design the validation assay so the ranking question is answered by the experiment: a purified-enzyme FRET assay measures IC50 regardless of which crystal conformation the pocket was solved in, and it captures the covalent inactivation rate (k_inact/K_I) that Vina cannot see. A compound that scores well because it is big, or because the pocket was solved around its competitor, is not a failure of the compound; it is a failure of the scoring setup, and the assay is what tells the two apart.

### 4.4 Mechanism

Rupintrivir, AG7404 and SG85 are all peptidomimetic Michael acceptors: the P1 glutamine-mimicking lactam sits in the S1 pocket, the P2/P3 groups fill their subsites, and the α,β-unsaturated ester is attacked by the catalytic cysteine (Cys147 HRV2 / Cys146 HRV-B14), forming a covalent bond and irreversibly inactivating the protease, which blocks polyprotein processing and so blocks viral replication. Test for each link: (i) a purified HRV 3C FRET enzyme assay (with rupintrivir as the comparator) measures IC50 and the covalent inactivation rate k_inact/K_I directly against the published values; (ii) a cell-based cytopathic-effect assay across HRV-A, -B and -C serotypes measures antiviral EC50 — a fall in enzyme activity without a fall in viral replication would break the claimed mechanism and point to delivery or permeability; (iii) a resistance-selection passage measures whether the compound selects the known rupintrivir mutations or new ones, and whether SG85 retains activity against the rupintrivir-resistant virus (the no-cross-resistance claim, tested rather than cited).

### 4.5 Testable hypotheses

1. AG7404 inhibits purified HRV-B14 3C protease with an IC50 within 3-fold of the published 0.046 μM in the same FRET assay. Falsifier: IC50 > 3-fold worse than 0.046 μM.
2. SG85 inhibits HRV-C15 3C protease in the enzyme assay and HRV-C15 replication in cells at an EC50 within 3-fold of its published median 0.04 μM. Falsifier: EC50 > 3-fold worse, which would show the HRV-C pocket (with its half-closed S2 subsite) rejects it.
3. The docking ranking predicts the enzyme ranking: the compound that scores best on 1CQQ has the lowest IC50. Falsifier: the docking winner's IC50 is worse than the reference drug's, which would show the non-covalent score does not rank this covalent family.
4. SG85 retains activity against a rupintrivir-resistant HRV (selected by serial passage), confirming no cross-resistance in this system. Falsifier: the resistant virus is equally resistant to SG85.

### 4.6 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): purified HRV 3C FRET enzyme assay for the three inhibitors — rupintrivir, AG7404, SG85 — plus a known weak inhibitor as a negative control; readout IC50 and k_inact/K_I, n=3, on HRV-B14 3C and HRV-C15 3C. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): cell-based cytopathic-effect assay for the best compound from stage 1 against rupintrivir across HRV-A, -B and -C serotypes, with a cytotoxicity counter-screen. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any inhibitor passes stages 1–2 — IC50 within 3-fold of the published value and EC50 matching rupintrivir on at least two species — a resistance-selection passage to test the cross-resistance claim; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed. No animal work is proposed at this stage.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows small margins.
2. The covalent bond that makes these drugs work is not scored by Vina — the reference drug's true binding is underestimated by exactly its mechanism of action.
3. SG85's first 1CQQ dock hit Vina's 1800 s wall-time limit under shared-CPU contention; the retry (same centre, box and exhaustiveness) completed at −7.42 kcal/mol, so the table is complete — but the timeout is a warning that this VM cannot reliably dock the largest ligands when several papers run at once.
3. Both structures hold rupintrivir, so each pocket is in rupintrivir's conformation, which favours the reference drug's shape over its competitors'.
4. The pipeline's target resolution was wrong (human IFIH1/MDA5, structure 3GA3) and had to be corrected by hand; the correction is documented in §2.2, but it means the automated pipeline cannot be trusted for viral targets without a manual structure audit.
5. The three inhibitors' measured potencies are EC50/IC50 values from different assays and different serotypes, not a common-assay comparison.
6. No data on the clinical confounder: docking cannot distinguish the 0.023 μM potency the class achieves from the natural-infection failure that stopped it, nor the delivery and timing problem that a better score does not fix.
7. Docking was on rhinovirus structures only; the picornavirus-family coverage claims (enterovirus, FMDV, norovirus) rest on published assays, not on this paper's computation — and AG7404's FMDV gap is published, not computed here.
8. No Pakistan-specific HRV burden data were found; the paper reports the global numbers and does not invent local ones.
9. The classical-canon sweep found the disease (catarrh, zukam) but not the target: the Canon of Medicine describes coryza as a syndrome, not a protease, so the Islamic-principle discussion rests on hifz al-nafs as a principle, not on a classical source about this target. The full 23 volumes of Al-Razi's Kitab al-Hawi exist in English only in selections, and Ibn al-Nafis's Sharh Tashrih al-Qanun survives as an Arabic manuscript with only partial English translation — neither was fully read, and the sweep covers the available digitised canon only.

## 6. Conclusion

Rupintrivir (AG7088), the covalent 3C inhibitor with a 0.023 μM mean EC50 that cut symptom scores by 33% in a human challenge trial and then failed in natural colds, self-docks at −7.45 kcal/mol on its own 1.85 Å co-crystal 1CQQ and −7.43 kcal/mol on the HRV-C15 structure 6KU8, with no PAINS alert — a validated control, not a discovery, and a modest score that shows Vina cannot see the covalent bond that does the drug's work. AG7404 scores −7.69 and −7.96 kcal/mol, ahead on both structures by 0.24 and 0.53 kcal/mol, inside noise; SG85 scores −7.42 on 1CQQ — tied with the reference — and −7.75 on 6KU8. The docking verdict is a consistent but sub-noise ranking that contradicts the cell-assay potency order and agrees only with the purified-enzyme data — and it is useful precisely as one: this covalent family cannot be ranked by rigid non-covalent scoring, the reference drug's win is absent even on its own co-crystal, and the next screen must be a purified 3C enzyme assay measuring the covalent inactivation rate, not a bigger docking set. What the docking cannot do is resolve the clinical split: the same pocket that binds rupintrivir at −7.45 kcal/mol is the pocket whose blockade failed in natural infection, and only an enzyme assay plus a cell assay can tell whether a successor keeps the potency without the delivery failure. The next step is the cheap, decisive test — a 3C FRET enzyme assay for the three inhibitors against rupintrivir — not another docking run.

## 7. Policy implications

**Decision-maker:** Pakistan's Drug Regulatory Authority (DRAP) and the National Institute of Health (NIH) Islamabad, with the SMBBMU ORIC office as the local route. **Decision:** whether to spend anything on a rhinovirus 3C-inhibitor programme in Pakistan, and what must exist first. **Exact change:** do not fund any rupintrivir-repurposing or 3C-inhibitor evaluation for the common cold — no 3C inhibitor is approved, the only clinical data show a challenge-trial win and a natural-infection failure, and Pakistan has no published HRV burden data at all. Instead, fund two cheap things first: (a) a one-season HRV surveillance study in Larkana and Karachi clinics, typing HRV-A/B/C in cold and asthma-exacerbation cases, so the patients who could benefit from any 3C inhibitor are counted before any drug is discussed; and (b) a purified 3C enzyme assay capability at one university lab, so the docking rankings in papers like this one can be tested for the price of reagents rather than debated. **If evidence Z appears, reverse this:** if the surveillance shows HRV-C drives a defined share of asthma exacerbations in Pakistan, and an inhibitor shows an IC50 within 3-fold of rupintrivir's published potency against HRV-C15 3C with oral bioavailability, move to a drug-evaluation assessment for that compound; if the surveillance shows HRV is a minor contributor to exacerbations, drop the 3C route and redirect the same money to the respiratory diseases Pakistan's patients actually carry — do not proceed to any drug evaluation on docking scores alone. The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life, a maqsad of the Sharia; Qur'an 5:32 — "whoever saves a life, it is as if he had saved mankind entirely"): where a cheap test can distinguish a usable inhibitor from an unusable one for a virus that drives asthma exacerbations, running the test is the obligation; prescribing on an untested docking score would violate it — and the classical rule, that nothing be prescribed for catarrh for three days while secretions discharge, is the same principle in older language: do not intervene until you know what you are treating. The evidence here is thin — docking only — so the recommendation is "run the trial", not "do the policy".
