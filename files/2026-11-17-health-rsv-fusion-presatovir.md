## Abstract

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) caused an estimated 33.0 million lower-respiratory episodes and 101,400 deaths in children under five in 2019, with more than 97 percent of deaths in low- and middle-income countries, and no small-molecule antiviral is approved. Presatovir, an RSV fusion inhibitor, cut viral load in a human challenge study but failed its Phase 2b trial in haematopoietic-cell transplant recipients, with no viral-load or clinical benefit and treatment-emergent fusion-protein substitutions in 21 percent of treated patients with lower-respiratory infection. We asked whether standard rigid docking can rank RSV fusion inhibitors at all. A first automated run docked onto the wrong structure — a 51-residue fragment (29QJ) — and was discarded in full. The corrected benchmark docks presatovir, the co-crystallised inhibitor rilematovir, and five ChEMBL leads with measured potency onto the prefusion F structure 5KWW (2.5 angstrom), at the verified rilematovir-pocket centre. Presatovir scores −6.15 kcal/mol and rilematovir −5.36, but the ranking against measured potency is essentially random (Spearman rho +0.1, n = 5): the most potent measured compound (EC50 0.47 nM) scores −4.94, while presatovir's own ChEMBL record scores best at −6.53, and the same molecule docked twice from two SMILES encodings differs by 0.38 kcal/mol. Rigid Vina docking on a single prefusion structure cannot rank RSV fusion inhibitors, and no lead can be claimed better than presatovir from this screen. The falsifier is a rescoring method, or a second prefusion structure, that reaches rho of −0.5 or below against the same five measured potencies, or a cell assay that reproduces the docking order.

## 1. Introduction

RSV is the leading viral cause of severe lower-respiratory infection in infants, and a major cause in immunocompromised adults and the elderly. Li et al. estimated that globally in 2019 there were 33.0 million RSV-associated acute lower respiratory infection episodes (uncertainty range 25.4–44.6 million), 3.6 million hospital admissions, and 101,400 RSV-attributable deaths in children aged 0–60 months, with more than 95 percent of episodes and more than 97 percent of deaths in low-income and middle-income countries (Li et al. 2022, PMID 35598608). In adults aged 60 years and older in high-income countries, the 2019 burden was approximately 5.2 million cases, 470,000 hospitalisations and 33,000 in-hospital deaths (Savic et al. 2023, PMID 36369772). In Pakistan, RSV circulates year-round and is tracked in national sentinel surveillance of respiratory viruses (2022–2025; PMID 41600791). There is no approved small-molecule antiviral for RSV.

The fusion (F) protein is the obvious target. RSV F is a class I fusion protein that drives viral entry by refolding from a metastable prefusion conformation to a stable postfusion six-helix bundle; locking the prefusion state blocks entry. The prefusion structure was solved by McLellan et al. in 2013 (PMID 23618766), and the same pocket now has a co-crystallised inhibitor: JNJ-53718678 (rilematovir) bound to prefusion F (5KWW, 2.5 angstrom; Roymans et al. 2017, PMID 28761099). Presatovir (GS-5806) is the most clinically tested fusion inhibitor in the class. It reduced viral load and symptoms in a human challenge study (DeVincenzo et al. 2014, PMID 25140957), then failed in the patients who needed it most: in haematopoietic-cell transplant (HCT) recipients with RSV lower-respiratory infection, presatovir did not reduce viral load or improve any clinical outcome (Marty et al. 2020, PMID 31915807), and in HCT recipients with upper-respiratory infection it did not meet its prespecified endpoint either (Chemaly et al. 2020, PMID 31793991). Resistance emerged during treatment in a fifth of the lower-respiratory patients (Porter et al. 2020, PMID 32071058). Meanwhile the class itself is not dead: ziresovir met its primary endpoint in a Phase 3 trial in hospitalised infants (NEJM 2024, PMID 39321361), and prevention has moved to long-acting monoclonals — nirsevimab (74.5 percent efficacy in MELODY; Hammitt et al. 2022, PMID 35235726) and clesrovimab, licensed by the FDA in June 2025 (MMWR 2025).

**Falsifiable question (stated before the results):** can the standard rigid-docking benchmark of this series — ChEMBL potency retrieval plus AutoDock Vina docking on the best available structure, with a validated control — rank RSV fusion inhibitors by their measured potency? The claim is that it cannot, on a single prefusion structure, and the paper's own calibration (Spearman rho against measured pChEMBL, with a re-docked co-crystallised control) is the test. The claim is refuted if a rescoring method, or a second prefusion structure, reaches rho of −0.5 or below against the same five measured potencies, or if a cell assay reproduces the docking order.

### 2.2 Target verification, and the discarded wrong-structure run

RSV F was resolved by accession, not by text search: UniProt P03420, Fusion glycoprotein F0, Human respiratory syncytial virus A (strain A2), 574 amino acids, 92 PDB cross-references. ChEMBL holds exactly one target with component accession P03420: CHEMBL3856166, "Fusion glycoprotein F0", SINGLE PROTEIN, with 67 bioactivities (verified live 17 November 2026). The text-search trap is named, not fallen into: a ChEMBL pref_name search for "respiratory syncytial virus fusion" returns CHEMBL2074 — human maltase-glucoamylase — first, the same trap that produced discarded runs earlier in this series (maltase substituted for PfPI4K, GyrB, DHFR and PTP1B). This run's target resolution was correct; its structure pick was not.

The standard pipeline run (`run_docking.py --target "Respiratory syncytial virus fusion protein" --ref presatovir`) resolved the target correctly (P03420 → CHEMBL3856166, 52 bioactivities retrieved, 26 leads ranked) and then docked onto the wrong structure. Its PDB auto-pick takes the best-resolution X-ray structure under 3.0 angstrom with a drug-like HETATM centre, and chose 29QJ (1.32 angstrom): "RSV fusion protein N-terminal heptad repeat domain in complex with double-stapled peptide 4/4G" — a 51-residue fragment (residues 159–209) bound to a stapled peptide (MK8, 76 atoms). Its "ligand centre" (6.04, 12.10, 85.20) is the centroid of that stapled peptide on the fragment, not the presatovir pocket: presatovir binds a pocket in the prefusion trimer, at the interface of the protomers, which a 51-residue fragment does not contain. The run's docking scores (presatovir −6.55, CHEMBL3410450 −6.04, and three more) are discarded in full and preserved as `dock-HP-072-2026-11-17-WRONG-STRUCTURE.json`. Its leads list — real RSV F actives from CHEMBL3856166 — was verified correct by accession and reused. The rule this paper states, and follows, is: **no verified structure, no docking table** — a structure without a verified ligand pocket gets a corrected run, never a published table.

### 2.3 Corrected computation (5KWW, verified centre)

A corrected driver (`dock_hp072_rsv_f_2026-11-17.py`, RDKit venv) docked on PDB 5KWW, "Crystal Structure of Inhibitor JNJ-53718678 In Complex with Prefusion RSV F Glycoprotein", 2.50 angstrom, prefusion trimer (Roymans et al. 2017). The co-crystallised ligand is 6YA — JNJ-53718678 (rilematovir), formula C21H20ClF3N4O3S, MW 500.9, an exact match to PubChem CID 118892432. Centre audit: `dock._ligand_center` on the 5KWW PDB file returns (20.60, 18.96, 19.21), the centroid of 6YA (33 atoms); the other HETATM groups are NAG (glycan, excluded by the picker), NHE (buffer, 13 atoms, smaller than 6YA) and SO4/HOH (junk). The centre was verified against a hand-computed 6YA centroid (20.60, 18.96, 19.21) and matches to within 0.01 angstrom — the centre is audited, not hand-rolled. Docking used the same Vina 1.2.7 protocol as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 angstrom, exhaustiveness 8, 3 poses, best pose reported. Seven compounds were docked: presatovir (reference, PubChem CID 58029842, SMILES verified live), rilematovir (positive control — the co-crystallised ligand, re-docked into its own pocket), and the top five ChEMBL leads from the discarded run, all with measured EC50 values. A Spearman rank correlation between Vina affinity and measured pChEMBL was computed for the five leads (hand-rolled ranks; no scipy assumed); negative rho is the correct direction (better dock, more potent). All outputs are in `dock-HP-072-2026-11-17.json`.

### 2.4 Honesty limits

No RMSD was computed for the re-docked rilematovir, so pose recovery against the crystal pose is not claimed — only the score and the tight pose spread are reported. The five pChEMBL values mix two assay contexts (RSV A2 infection of HepG2 cells, and the D486N mutant F protein in Hep2 cells), which weakens the calibration; the assay context is stated per compound in Table 3.1. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol; the whole docked set spans only 2.28 kcal/mol, so most gaps in the table are inside the noise. No molecular dynamics or free-energy rescoring was run. No lab data were generated.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The wrong-structure run, and what it would have published

Had the structure audit been skipped, the pipeline would have published a benchmark of presatovir at −6.55 kcal/mol and four ChEMBL leads at −6.04 to −3.96, all docked onto a 51-residue fragment at the centroid of a stapled peptide. The run completed with "PIPELINE OK: True". Only the structure line — 29QJ where a prefusion trimer should have been — betrayed it. This is the same failure family as the maltase runs, one step later in the pipeline: the target was right, the structure was wrong, and the output looked complete. The defence is the same in both cases: verify by accession, and verify the structure against the bound ligand.

Three things in this table deserve attention. First, the duplicate: presatovir docked twice — once as the PubChem query, once as its ChEMBL record CHEMBL3410450 (pref_name PRESATOVIR, synonyms GS-5806; verified live) — scores −6.15 and −6.53, a 0.38 kcal/mol gap between identical molecules, because the two SMILES encodings differ in stereochemistry specification and RDKit generates different starting conformers. That gap is the run's noise floor, and every gap between distinct compounds in the table is of the same order or smaller. Second, the control: rilematovir re-docked into its own pocket scores only −5.36 — weaker than presatovir, weaker than its own ChEMBL twin — with three poses within 0.05 kcal/mol of each other. A proven nanomolar inhibitor, in its own crystal pocket, does not top this table. Third, the ranking is inverted against measurement: the most potent measured compound (CHEMBL4747540, 0.47 nM) scores −4.94, fifth of seven, while the weakest measured (presatovir's own record, 1,193 nM against the D486N mutant) scores best. Spearman rho between Vina affinity and measured pChEMBL is **+0.1 (n = 5)** — no correlation.

### 3.3 Comparison with published docking

No published Vina benchmark of presatovir on 5KWW was found in the literature sweep, so there is no published docking table to compare against. The structural fact is published: 5KWW is the JNJ-53718678 complex (Roymans et al. 2017), and 6EAD is the F140I inhibitor-escape-variant structure (2.8 angstrom), which confirms that the F140 region is part of the inhibitor pocket. Absolute Vina scores are protocol- and structure-dependent and cannot be compared across studies; only the within-protocol ranking in Table 3.1 is interpretable, and that ranking is the paper's negative result.

### 3.4 The clinical record, and why it matters more than the table

Presatovir's clinical history is the reason the docking result is a negative finding rather than a lead. In the human challenge study it worked: viral load 250.7 vs 757.7 log10 PFUe·h/mL (P < .001), mucus 6.9 vs 15.1 g (P = .03). In HCT recipients it did not. The Phase 2b lower-respiratory trial (NCT02254421, 60 patients, 31 presatovir / 29 placebo) found a viral-load difference of −0.02 log10 copies/mL (95% CI −0.62 to 0.57; P = .94), median supplemental-oxygen-free days 26 vs 28 (P = .84), incident respiratory failure 10.3 vs 10.7 percent (P = .98), and all-cause mortality 0 vs 7.1 percent (P = .19). The Phase 2 upper-respiratory trial (NCT02254408, 189 patients) found a viral-load difference of −0.33 log10 (95% CI −0.64 to −0.02; P = .040) against a prespecified alpha of 0.01 — not significant — and progression to lower-respiratory complications 11.2 vs 19.5 percent (odds ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.22–1.18; P = .11); a post-hoc lymphopenia subgroup did show fewer complications (13.3 vs 64.3 percent, P = .008), which is hypothesis-generating, not evidence. Resistance-associated substitutions emerged in 6 of 29 presatovir-treated lower-respiratory patients (20.7 percent) and 10 of 89 upper-respiratory patients (11.2 percent), against none on placebo; V127A was already present at baseline in 8 subjects. The drug was well tolerated in both trials — the failure was efficacy, not safety. The class, meanwhile, has a positive Phase 3: ziresovir in hospitalised infants (n = 244) improved the Wang bronchiolitis score by 0.8 points (95% CI −1.3 to −0.3; P = .002) and cut viral load by 0.6 log10 at day 5, though resistance-associated mutations appeared in 15 participants (9 percent). The target is druggable; presatovir, in HCT recipients, was not the drug.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe: the screen's failure is the finding

This paper set out to rank RSV fusion inhibitors and found that its own screen cannot rank them. The value of the run is the calibration, not the table. A docking protocol that gives its co-crystallised control −5.36, its reference drug −6.15, and the most potent measured compound −4.94 — with a duplicate-molecule noise floor of 0.38 kcal/mol and a Spearman rho of +0.1 — has no ranking power on this target. The honest conclusion is that the five ChEMBL leads, however potent in cells, cannot be ordered by this screen, and none can be claimed better than presatovir from these scores. The sub-nanomolar leads (CHEMBL4747540 at 0.47 nM, CHEMBL4640616 at 2.0 nM, CHEMBL4779491 at 2.1 nM) remain what they were before docking: measured cell potencies with no structural ranking attached.

### 4.2 Why the "win" must not be over-read

Presatovir's −6.15 is not a win. It is 0.38 kcal/mol behind its own duplicate record, and only 0.79 kcal/mol ahead of the control — barely twice the duplicate noise floor — and it sits in a table whose control — a proven nanomolar inhibitor in its own pocket — scores worse. The three poses of presatovir span 0.35 kcal/mol and the three poses of rilematovir span 0.05, so the poses are converged; the problem is the scoring function on this pocket, not the sampling. The pocket was crystallised around rilematovir, so any pocket-shape effect should favour the control, not presatovir — and still the control scores lower. The most likely reading is that rigid docking on a single prefusion structure, with no side-chain flexibility and no rescoring, cannot separate compounds whose measured potencies span 0.47 nM to 1,193 nM when the assay contexts differ (wild-type A2 infection vs the D486N mutant) and the binding site is a shallow, hydrophobic, inter-protomer groove.

### 4.3 Association only

This is a retrospective docking benchmark, not a causal experiment. It shows that Vina scores on 5KWW do not track measured RSV F potency for these five compounds, that presatovir's clinical failure is consistent with a weak and resistance-prone inhibitor, and that the pipeline's structure picker can silently dock onto a fragment. It does not show that any lead is inactive, that presatovir is inactive in any other population, or that another docking method would fail the same way. The causal identification is validated-control re-docking (rilematovir into its own pocket); beyond that, the paper is association only.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. **A flexible-side-chain or MM-GBSA rescoring of the same seven compounds on 5KWW reaches rho ≤ −0.5 against the same five measured potencies.** Falsifier: the rescoring run itself; rho above −0.5 refutes it, and the screen stays unusable for ranking.
2. **Docking the same compounds on a second prefusion structure (5EA3, JNJ-2408068 complex, 2.75 angstrom; or 6VKC, JNJ-36811054 complex, 2.6 angstrom) reproduces the 5KWW order.** Falsifier: a different order on the second structure — which would show the ranking is structure-dependent, not compound-dependent.
3. **CHEMBL4747540 (0.47 nM) inhibits RSV A2 in a cell assay at sub-nanomolar EC50, while presatovir does not beat it.** Falsifier: a head-to-head A2 HepG2 assay; if presatovir matches or beats it, the ChEMBL potency gap is an assay artefact.
4. **Presatovir's D486N weakness is the resistance mechanism that matters clinically.** Falsifier: a D486N virus that presatovir still inhibits at wild-type potency in the same assay — which would point the resistance story at the other emergent substitutions instead.

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–2): obtain presatovir, rilematovir and the three sub-nanomolar leads commercially and confirm identity and purity by LC-MS. Stage 2 (weeks 3–6): head-to-head RSV A2 infection assay in HepG2 cells (the assay context of the three sub-nanomolar measurements), EC50 for all five compounds in the same run, with a D486N-mutant counter-screen in parallel — the single experiment that tests both the potency gap and the resistance hypothesis. Stage 3 (weeks 7–10): flexible docking / MM-GBSA rescoring of the same five compounds on 5KWW and 5EA3, compared against the assay order. Stage 4 (weeks 11–12): report all results, including the negatives — a screen that cannot rank is a result. Total cost is one cell assay and five compounds, not a screening campaign.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Docking only, and a negative calibration.** No compound in this paper was tested in a cell; the headline is that the screen cannot rank, not that any lead works.
2. **Single structure, rigid receptor.** One prefusion structure (5KWW), no side-chain flexibility, no MD, no rescoring; the rho of +0.1 is a property of this protocol on this structure, not of docking in general.
3. **Mixed assay contexts.** The five pChEMBL values mix RSV A2 HepG2 infection with the D486N mutant in Hep2 cells; the calibration is weaker for it, and the presatovir record (1,193 nM) is a mutant measurement, not a wild-type one.
4. **No RMSD for the control.** Rilematovir was re-docked into its own pocket but pose recovery was not measured, so the control validates the score scale, not the pose.
5. **No presatovir EC50 quoted.** No PMID-verified in-vitro EC50 for presatovir was retrieved; the paper uses the challenge-study and trial numbers instead, and does not invent the missing value.
6. **No lab data, no clinical recommendation.** Nothing here changes treatment; the prevention standard remains the monoclonals, and the treatment gap remains open.
7. **Islamic canon gaps (named, per the programme's rule).** The *Canon* and *Kitab al-Hawi* were checked through named works, not read in full; the full 23-volume *Hawi* exists in English only in selections, and most classical Arabic medical manuscripts remain undigitised.

## 6. Policy implications

**Decision-maker: the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP), the National Institute of Health Islamabad, and the Federal Directorate of Immunization — and, for the method rule, every public funder of computational drug discovery in Pakistan.** Three exact changes, each with its falsifier:

1. **Do not fund a presatovir-analogue programme on docking scores alone; require the head-to-head cell assay first.** The five leads in Table 3.1 cost one assay to order, and this paper shows the docking order is random (rho +0.1). A programme that picks its lead from this table would pick presatovir's own record — the weakest measured compound — and call it the winner. Reverse this only if a rescoring method reaches rho ≤ −0.5 on this same set, in which case the docking order becomes usable evidence again.
2. **Prioritise prevention procurement over a new small-molecule programme.** With 101,400 under-five deaths a year, more than 97 percent in countries like Pakistan, and two long-acting monoclonals now licensed (nirsevimab 74.5 percent efficacy; clesrovimab licensed June 2025), the fastest mortality reduction is a prevention programme, not a fusion-inhibitor discovery programme — especially when the only positive Phase 3 in the class (ziresovir, infants) still showed resistance in 9 percent. Reverse this if a fusion inhibitor posts a positive Phase 3 in infants with a resistance rate below that 9 percent, or if monoclonal supply or price makes prevention unreachable in Pakistan.
3. **Require accession-verified targets and structure audits in every publicly funded computational screen.** Any screening proposal must state the UniProt accession, the ChEMBL target ID it resolved to, and the PDB ID with the bound ligand its centre was verified against; any run that falls back to text search or to a resolution-sorted structure pick must declare it. This paper's discarded fragment run is the evidence: without that rule, public money can fund a complete benchmark docked onto a 51-residue fragment. Reverse this only if the auto-picker is shown to return the correct structure first for a blinded set of viral accessions. **Islamic principle behind the recommendations:** preservation of life (*hifz al-nafs*, Al-Ghazali, *Al-Mustasfa*; Al-Shatibi, *Al-Muwafaqat*) — RSV kills more than a hundred thousand young children a year, so a screening method that mis-ranks antivirals is a failure to protect life, not a technical footnote; and stewardship (*khilafah*, Qur'an 2:30) — public research funds are a trust, and a steward does not spend a trust on a benchmark of the wrong structure. Where the Islamic sources and the modern evidence point the same way, this paper says so; they do here.

## 7. Conclusion

Presatovir scores −6.15 kcal/mol on the prefusion RSV F structure 5KWW, at a centre verified against the co-crystallised inhibitor rilematovir — and the number means almost nothing, because the same screen gives the co-crystallised control −5.36 and the most potent measured compound −4.94, with a rank correlation of 0.1 against measured potency. The clinical record agrees with the negative reading: presatovir failed its Phase 2b trials in transplant recipients, with resistance emerging in a fifth of the lower-respiratory patients, while the class's one positive Phase 3 (ziresovir) and the licensed monoclonals carry the field forward. The memorable number is **0.1**: the correlation between docking and measurement for RSV fusion inhibitors on this structure. The next step is one cell assay, not more docking.
