### Submission abstract (1,967 chars)

Question. Pakistan's Prime Minister's Youth Business & Agriculture Loan Scheme (PMYBALS) has disbursed about Rs 289 billion since January 2023 to over half a million borrowers, at a taxpayer subsidy the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) prices near 15 paisa per rupee lent. Its stated purpose is youth enterprise creation. Does the money reach young entrepreneurs and create enterprises, or does subsidised credit concentrate in a few large borrowers while youth unemployment rises? Claim. On the published aggregates, the scheme fails its enterprise test twice. First, concentration: in the SBP tier tables to December 2025, Tier-III loans (Rs 1.5-7.5 million, 7% interest) took 67.0% of all money while reaching only 11.0% of borrowers; the average Tier-III loan (Rs 2.92 million) was 22 times the average interest-free Tier-I loan (Rs 132,339) received by 85.6% of borrowers. Second, no enterprise signal: youth unemployment (15-24) rose from 11.1% (2020-21) to 12.6% (2024-25) while Rs 175 billion had been disbursed, and the 27.4% rise in SECP company registrations is attributed by SECP to digitisation, not the scheme. A data-quality finding: the reported Rs 102 billion to 3,957 agriculture borrowers is arithmetically impossible under the Rs 7.5 million loan cap (maximum Rs 29.7 billion). Falsifier. The claim is refuted if district-level SBP data show per-youth disbursement predicting per-youth enterprise formation after controlling for the pre-2023 trend and SECP digitisation, or if a random sample of Tier-I borrowers shows at least 50% still trading 24 months after disbursement. Identification. Association only: a descriptive audit of published national aggregates with no control group. Policy. Publish district-level outcomes, correct the statistical record, and pilot a randomised musharakah (profit-sharing) youth window against the interest-bearing tiers.
