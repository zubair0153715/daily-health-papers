## One variant, two proteinopathies: APOE Christchurch (R136S) as a heparan-sulfate-gated brake on tau and α-synuclein seeding — a structural hypothesis and a testable therapeutic programme

- **File:** `2026-11-13-brain-apoe-christchurch-dual-proteinopathy.md` (+ PDF)
- **Abstract:** 2022 characters (limit 2,500)
- **Abstract text:** Background. APOE is the strongest genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer's disease, and a rare variant — APOE3 Christchurch (APOE3ch, R136S) — protected a PSEN1 E280A carrier from dementia for roughly 28 years despite extensive amyloid deposition. Until November 2026 the protection was understood as a tau story: R136S weakens apolipoprotein E (ApoE) binding to heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs), limiting tau spread. New evidence. Soto-Faguás and colleagues (Cell Reports, 11 November 2026) now report that the Christchurch mutation engineered into mouse ApoE also prevents the accumulation of α-synuclein — the protein of Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia — in predisposed mice, with the senior author describing it as acting "like a super-APOE-2". Hypothesis. We propose a single shared mechanism: tau and α-synuclein seeds both enter neurons through the same HSPG gateway, and R136S closes that gateway for both. We test the structural premise on the real human sequence (UniProt P02649): residues 136–150 form a cationic cluster (Arg136, Arg142, Lys143, Arg145, Lys146, Arg147, Arg150) carrying a net charge of +7; R136S removes one positive charge (+7 → +6) and shifts the N-terminal domain net charge to −4 — the same value as protective APOE2, versus −2 for risk-conferring APOE4. Falsifiable claim. If the shared-gateway model is correct, recombinant ApoE3ch will reduce α-synuclein preformed-fibril uptake into primary neurons by a comparable fraction to its reduction of tau uptake, and any Christchurch-mimetic that occupies the ApoE HSPG-binding pocket will inhibit α-synuclein seeding as well as tau seeding. We specify the experiments, effect-size expectations, and the observations that would falsify the model. Conclusion. One rare variant may define one druggable gateway for two diseases — and, because women with Parkinson's disease dementia carry a substantially higher amyloid co-pathology burden, the benefit of closing that gateway should be tested with sex-stratified analysis.
