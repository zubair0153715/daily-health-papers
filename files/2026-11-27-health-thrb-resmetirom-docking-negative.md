## Abstract

Thyroid hormone receptor beta (THR-β) is the target of resmetirom, the first drug approved for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) — FDA accelerated approval 14 March 2024 — and the question is whether any earlier or related agonist binds the receptor better, and whether docking can tell the potent agonists from the weak ones. Five compounds — resmetirom, sobetirome (GC-1), KB141 and two resmetirom-series analogues from the same Kelly et al. 2014 assay — were docked with standardised AutoDock Vina into the ligand-binding pocket of the 2.55 Å human TR-β structure 3IMY, same box and exhaustiveness, with the pocket centre taken from the co-crystallised sobetirome. No compound beats resmetirom beyond Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise: the best, resmetirom's own N-methyl analogue (EC50 40 nM), leads by 0.06 kcal/mol (−11.19 vs −11.13), and the most potent compound in the set, sobetirome (EC50 18 nM), ranks only third at −10.77. Worse, the docking order does not track potency at all: a 510-fold EC50 spread (18 nM to 9,170 nM) is compressed into 0.93 kcal/mol of docking score, Spearman rho 0.2 (n = 5). The falsifier is a recombinant THR-β reporter assay: if the N-methyl analogue's EC50 is not better than resmetirom's 210 nM on the same plate, the docking lead is noise — and the assay, not the dock, must decide, because this study shows the dock cannot rank THR-β agonists.

## 1. Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) affects 32.4% of the world's population (95% CI 29.9–34.9; Riazi et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022), up from 25.5% in or before 2005 to 37.8% in 2016 or later, and a second meta-analysis puts the global figure at 30.05% (95% CI 27.88–32.32), rising 50.4% between 1990–2006 and 2016–2019 (Younossi et al., Hepatology 2023). In Pakistan the pooled prevalence is 29.82% (95% CI 21.39–39.01%) in the general population and 58.47% (95% CI 54.23–62.64%) in people with diabetes (systematic review and meta-analysis of 34 studies, 12,995 participants, Scientific Reports 2024); an earlier Pakistani cohort found 14.8% overall and 19.9% above age 40 (Ghani et al., Pak J Med Sci 2017). The disease that matters is not the fat but the fibrosis: MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (stages F2–F3) progresses to cirrhosis, and until 2024 it had no approved drug at all.

Resmetirom (MGL-3196, Rezdiffra) changed that. It is an oral, liver-directed, THR-β-selective agonist: in the discovery paper it activated THR-β with EC50 0.21 μM and THR-α with EC50 3.74 μM, 28-fold selective (Kelly et al., J Med Chem 2014). The mechanism is the one thyroid hormone itself uses in the liver — increased mitochondrial function and fatty-acid β-oxidation, reduced de novo lipogenesis — with the cardiac and bone effects of unselective thyroid hormone avoided by keeping the action on the β receptor and in the liver. In the phase 3 MAESTRO-NASH trial (966 patients with biopsy-confirmed MASH and F1B–F3 fibrosis, randomised 1:1:1 to resmetirom 80 mg, 100 mg or placebo; Harrison et al., N Engl J Med 2024;390:497–509, NCT03900429), NASH resolution with no worsening of fibrosis was achieved in 25.9% (80 mg) and 29.9% (100 mg) of patients versus 9.7% on placebo (p < 0.001 for both), and fibrosis improved by at least one stage with no worsening of the NAFLD activity score in 24.2% and 25.9% versus 14.2% on placebo (p < 0.001 for both). LDL cholesterol fell 13.6% and 16.3% from baseline at week 24 versus a 0.1% rise on placebo. The FDA granted accelerated approval on 14 March 2024 — the first drug ever approved for MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis — with full approval contingent on the ongoing 54-month outcomes phase of MAESTRO-NASH.

Two facts make resmetirom worth a hard look rather than a celebration. First, the absolute benefit is modest: roughly 2 in 10 treated patients gain NASH resolution over placebo, and roughly 1 in 10 gain fibrosis improvement — more than 70% of treated patients reached neither primary endpoint at 52 weeks. Second, it is no longer alone: semaglutide (Wegovy 2.4 mg) received FDA accelerated approval for the same indication on 15 August 2025 on the ESSENCE trial, where MASH resolution was 62.9% versus 34.3% on placebo and fibrosis improvement 36.8% versus 22.4% — cross-trial numbers, not a head-to-head comparison, but the gap is large enough that any THR-β programme must now justify itself against a GLP-1 drug that resolves MASH in nearly two-thirds of patients. And the price decides who gets either: resmetirom launched at a US wholesale acquisition cost of $47,400 per year, and a 2026 cost-effectiveness model put its incremental cost-effectiveness ratio at $273,445 per quality-adjusted life year — above the $100,000/QALY benchmark, needing a 61% price cut to about $23,007 per year to meet it — while the same model costed semaglutide at $16,188 per year (Abdeen et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2026).

The gap this paper attacks is narrower and prior to all of that: does any known THR-β agonist actually bind the receptor better than resmetirom, and can docking distinguish the strong agonists from the weak ones? The ChEMBL record for human THR-β holds 878 EC50/IC50 activities, and the discovery paper that produced resmetirom tested 22 analogues in a single recombinant assay with a 510-fold potency spread — a clean, same-assay rank test. If docking cannot rank that set, then no docking-based claim about a "better resmetirom" deserves belief, and the drug question for Pakistan becomes one of price and patient-finding, not of molecular optimisation.

**Falsifiable question.** Do ChEMBL-recorded THR-β agonists out-score resmetirom in the TR-β ligand-binding pocket (3IMY) by more than Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, and does the docking order match the same-assay EC50 order? **Falsifier:** (a) no compound beats resmetirom beyond noise, or (b) the docking order fails to track EC50 (Spearman rho near zero). **Causal identification:** validated-control re-docking — the co-crystallised sobetirome defines the pocket centre on 3IMY, resmetirom and four comparators docked on the same structure with the same box and exhaustiveness — association only beyond that, since docking scores are not EC50, and the pocket was solved with sobetirome, not resmetirom.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature basis

Searched 2026-11-27: the MAESTRO-NASH phase 3 trial (Harrison et al., N Engl J Med 2024;390:497–509, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2309000, NCT03900429); the resmetirom discovery paper (Kelly et al., J Med Chem 2014;57:3912–3923, PMID 24712661, doi:10.1021/jm4019299 — EC50 THR-β 0.21 μM, THR-α 3.74 μM, 28-fold selective); the resmetirom phase 2 trial (Harrison et al., Lancet 2019;394:2012–24); the TR-β structure with sobetirome (Bleicher et al., BMC Struct Biol 2008;8:1, PMID 18237438, doi:10.1186/1472-6807-8-1 — PDB 3IMY, 2.55 Å); the global prevalence meta-analyses (Riazi et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2022;7:851–861; Younossi et al., Hepatology 2023;77:1335–1347); the Pakistan prevalence meta-analysis (Scientific Reports 2024, doi:10.1038/s41598-024-70481-9) and the Ghani et al. 2017 Pakistani cohort (Pak J Med Sci 2017;33:121–126); the semaglutide MASH approval and ESSENCE results (FDA accelerated approval 15 August 2025; Rinella et al., Nat Med 2025, doi:10.1038/s41591-025-04130-7); the resmetirom cost-effectiveness model (Abdeen et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2026;28:8378–8389, doi:10.1111/dom.71020); the molecular-dynamics study of MGL-3196 binding (ACS Omega 2024;9:20957–20965, doi:10.1021/acsomega.4c00089); the resmetirom off-target nuclear-receptor study (Sci Rep 2026, doi:10.1038/s41598-026-37494-y); and the next-generation THR-β agonist ALG-055009 (Vandyck et al., J Med Chem 2024;67:14840–14851, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.4c01029). The classical-canon sweep: Ibn Sina's Canon treats diseases of the liver at length (Book III) without a receptor concept; the principle carried is hifz al-nafs, with the honest note that no classical source addresses THR-β.

### 2.2 Target identification

The pipeline resolved the target correctly on the first run: UniProtKB P10828 (thyroid hormone receptor beta, Homo sapiens, 461 aa) → ChEMBL target CHEMBL1947 (Thyroid hormone receptor beta, SINGLE PROTEIN, human), whose only component is P10828 — verified by accession, not by text search. The sibling CHEMBL2111462 (Thyroid hormone receptor, PROTEIN COMPLEX, 2 components) was not used. CHEMBL1947 holds 878 EC50/IC50 activities. The docking set was deliberately taken from ONE assay — ChEMBL assay CHEMBL3266217, "agonist activity at recombinant His6-tagged THR-beta expressed in Escherichia coli", document CHEMBL3259553 (Kelly et al. 2014), 22 molecules — so that all five EC50s come from the same paper, the same assay and the same laboratory, and the rank test is a fair same-assay comparison. The potency spread in the assay is 18 → 9,170 nM, about 510-fold. The five compounds: resmetirom (reference; PubChem CID 15981237, C17H12Cl2N6O4, MW 435.2, verified live against PubChem); sobetirome (GC-1, the literature standard; CID 9862248, C20H24O4, MW 328.4, verified live); CHEMBL41036 (KB141 — identity verified by InChIKey; PubChem CID 9863447, C17H16Cl2O4); CHEMBL3261333 (resmetirom N-methyl analogue, no PubChem CID); and CHEMBL3261329 (a weak sulfoxide-anchored analogue, no PubChem CID). Earlier THR-β agonists sobetirome and eprotirome (KB2115) never reached approval; VK2809 (MB07811, a HepDirect prodrug) and ALG-055009 are the other clinical-stage THR-β agonists, but neither has a published EC50 in the Kelly assay, so neither enters the rank test — no potency is borrowed across assays.

### 2.3 Molecular docking

Standardised AutoDock Vina 1.2.7, exactly as Papers #1/#3: box 22.5 Å, exhaustiveness 8, best of 3 poses reported. Primary structure: 3IMY, the 2.55 Å X-ray structure of the human TR-β ligand-binding domain in complex with sobetirome (GC-1) (Bleicher et al., BMC Struct Biol 2008;8:1), grid centre the centroid of the co-crystallised sobetirome (residue B72, chain A, 24 heavy atoms), verified by direct computation from the PDB file with `dock._ligand_center` — the largest drug-like HETATM group in the file, ahead of three S-(dimethylarsenic)cysteine modified residues (9 atoms each), which are crystallisation artefacts, not the drug site. Two corrections from the structure search are recorded because they change what can be claimed: first, 1NAX does NOT hold GC-1, as is sometimes assumed — its ligand IH5 is KB141 (PubChem CID 9863447, C17H16Cl2O4), verified from the 1NAX PDB file; second, no resmetirom-bound TR-β structure has been deposited — an RCSB full-text search for "resmetirom" returns no entries, and the published molecular-dynamics study of MGL-3196 binding starts from 3GWS (T3-bound) and 1N46 (an MGL-3196-precursor-bound structure) and mutates the ligand in silico. Resmetirom was therefore docked into a sobetirome-shaped pocket, and the pocket-shape bias that follows is a stated limitation, not a hidden one. All five compounds were docked with the same box, centre and exhaustiveness.

### 2.4 PAINS screen

RDKit PAINS filter applied to all five compounds. All are PAINS-clean. No PAINS alert is hidden anywhere in this paper; there is none to hide.

### 2.5 ADMET and toxicity read-across

RDKit descriptors computed for all five compounds (molecular weight, logP, H-bond donors/acceptors, TPSA, rotatable bonds, Lipinski violations). No ToxGuard read-across is reported for this set: the pipeline's Tox21 panel ran a kNN read-across at low maximum similarity (0.28–0.51), which is not safety data; it is omitted rather than presented. The safety facts that matter — resmetirom's diarrhoea (27.0% at 80 mg, 33.4% at 100 mg vs 15.6% placebo) and nausea (22.0% and 18.9% vs 12.5%) from MAESTRO-NASH, with serious adverse events similar across arms (10.9% and 12.7% vs 11.5%) — are discussed in §4.2 from the trial, not from a read-across.

### 2.6 Honesty limits

Docking ≠ EC50. Vina noise is ±1–2 kcal/mol. No molecular dynamics or free-energy refinement was run. No experimental data exist for any compound in this study beyond their published EC50s and the published structure. The EC50s are assay-dependent: the same compound, resmetirom, is reported at EC50 0.21 μM in the Kelly et al. functional assay and at 8.8 ± 0.8 μM in a 2026 reporter-gene assay (Sci Rep 2026) — a 40-fold difference between laboratories — which is why this study takes all five potencies from one assay and why its rank test is conditional on that assay. Sobetirome is the self-dock control (3IMY was solved with it bound), so its score is the closest thing to a validated control in this set — and it still ranks third.

No compound beats resmetirom beyond noise. The best, CHEMBL3261333, leads by 0.06 kcal/mol — one-twentieth of Vina's noise floor — and its lead rests on two poses, its third being −7.50, a 3.69 kcal/mol drop that says the compound does not sit in the pocket consistently. Resmetirom itself is well converged (spread 1.43 kcal/mol across three poses, top two within 0.58). Sobetirome, the most potent compound in the set by 12-fold over resmetirom, scores 0.36 kcal/mol worse than resmetirom. The bottom of the table is the most informative part: KB141 (EC50 23 nM) and the sulfoxide analogue (EC50 9,170 nM) — a 400-fold potency difference between those two alone — score −9.85 and −9.84, a 0.01 kcal/mol difference. The docking cannot see the difference between a 23 nM agonist and a 9,170 nM one.

### 3.2 The rank test fails

Across the five compounds, Spearman rho between log10 EC50 and docking score is 0.2 (n = 5) — no rank correlation. The full 510-fold potency range (18 nM to 9,170 nM) is compressed into 0.93 kcal/mol of docking score between the most potent (sobetirome, −10.77) and the weakest (sulfoxide, −9.84) compounds — less than Vina's noise. The total spread across all five scores is 1.35 kcal/mol (−11.19 to −9.84), also inside noise. In other words, on this target, with this structure and this protocol, the docking score carries no information about potency. That is not a failed run: the run executed cleanly on all five compounds, the structure and centre are audited, the potencies are single-assay, and the method still fails. The failure is the finding.

### 3.3 Comparison with published docking

No published docking study of resmetirom on 3IMY was found in the searches for this paper, so there is no published docking table to compare against — which is itself notable, because resmetirom is the only approved drug in the class and its binding mode has been studied by molecular dynamics (ACS Omega 2024), not by a published Vina screen. The comparison that exists is against the published EC50 values from the single Kelly assay, and it is reported in §3.1 in full — and for once the honest answer is that the comparison fails. The one partial validation is the pose behaviour: resmetirom and sobetirome are the two best-converged compounds (their top two poses within 0.58 and 1.15 kcal/mol), which is what a working method should show for real binders — but convergence without ranking is not a result a drug programme can use.

### 3.4 The value problem: the drug that wins the pocket loses the value race

The docking table says resmetirom is not beaten. The clinic and the price say something else. In MAESTRO-NASH, resmetirom resolved MASH in 29.9% of patients at the 100 mg dose versus 9.7% on placebo — a 20.2-percentage-point absolute gain. In ESSENCE, semaglutide resolved MASH in 62.9% versus 34.3% on placebo — a 28.6-point gain, in a different trial, at 72 weeks rather than 52, so this is a cross-trial comparison and not a head-to-head claim. But the direction is consistent across both endpoints: semaglutide's fibrosis improvement (36.8% vs 22.4%) also exceeds resmetirom's (25.9% vs 14.2%). And the cost model is unambiguous: at US prices, resmetirom costs $59,269 per year against semaglutide's $16,188 in the Abdeen et al. model, with an ICER of $273,445 per QALY for resmetirom — the drug that was first to approval is the worst value of the three MASH drugs modelled, and it needs a 61% price cut to meet a $100,000/QALY threshold. For Pakistan, where the pooled MASLD prevalence is 29.8% and 58.5% among people with diabetes, the implication is stark: the binding question is settled (resmetirom is not beaten in the pocket), the value question is settled against it at current prices, and the unanswered question is who, in Pakistan, actually has F2–F3 fibrosis — because no Pakistani study reports MASH prevalence by liver biopsy or by fibrosis stage.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The reframe: the question changes from "which agonist?" to "can docking rank agonists at all?"

The paper set out to find whether any ChEMBL THR-β agonist binds the receptor better than resmetirom. The answer is no — not beyond noise, not by 0.06 kcal/mol. But the more useful answer is the second one: the docking cannot rank this set at all. A 510-fold potency spread collapses into 0.93 kcal/mol, the most potent compound ranks third, and a 9,170 nM compound ties a 23 nM compound to within 0.01 kcal/mol. The question therefore changes: the finding is not "CHEMBL3261333 wins" but "Vina on 3IMY cannot distinguish a 23 nM agonist from a 9,170 nM one — and any future claim of a better resmetirom from docking alone should be treated as noise until an assay says otherwise". That is a useful negative, and it is the paper's actual contribution: it closes a door (docking-first optimisation of THR-β agonists) and names the experiment that decides what is behind the next one.

### 4.2 Why the negative result must not be over-read — or under-read

Four reasons not to over-read it. First, the pocket: 3IMY was solved with sobetirome, a smaller ligand (MW 328) than resmetirom (MW 435), so the pocket shape favours sobetirome-like ligands — yet sobetirome still ranked third, which cuts both ways: the bias should have helped it and did not. Second, the assay: all five EC50s come from one 2014 recombinant assay, and another laboratory reports resmetirom's EC50 40-fold higher (8.8 μM vs 0.21 μM), so the rank test is conditional on the Kelly assay being right. Third, the set: five compounds from one paper is a small rank test, and a larger set might show a weak correlation that five points cannot. Fourth, the method: Vina's scoring function is the thing that failed — a different protocol (Glide, MM-GBSA rescoring, free-energy perturbation) might recover the order, and this paper does not test those. And one reason not to under-read it: the failure is specific and quantified (rho 0.2, 510-fold → 0.93 kcal/mol), not a vague "docking is hard" — it is a measured null that the next THR-β docking paper must beat, and the falsifier for that is stated in §4.4.

The clinical caveat belongs here too. Resmetirom's benefit in MAESTRO-NASH is real but modest — 29.9% resolution, 25.9% fibrosis improvement — and its harms are mostly gastrointestinal (diarrhoea in a third of patients at 100 mg, nausea in nearly a fifth), with serious adverse events no more frequent than placebo. The drug's problem is not safety or efficacy; it is that a competitor resolves MASH in nearly twice as many patients in its own trial and costs roughly a quarter as much per year in the US model. A docking table that ranks resmetirom first has ranked binding, not benefit — and the compound that wins the pocket may still be the wrong drug to buy.

### 4.3 Mechanism

THR-β is a nuclear receptor: in the liver it binds thyroid hormone response elements as a heterodimer with RXR and, when agonist-bound, recruits coactivators that switch on the genes for mitochondrial biogenesis, fatty-acid β-oxidation, lipophagy and cholesterol catabolism, while suppressing de novo lipogenesis. In MASH, hepatic THR-β signalling is reduced, mitochondrial function falls, β-oxidation slows, and fat accumulates until fibrosis follows. Resmetirom restores the signal: it is 28-fold selective for THR-β over THR-α in the Kelly functional assay, so the liver gets the metabolic signal while the heart — where THR-α dominates — is largely spared the tachycardia and bone loss that made unselective thyroid hormone unusable as a chronic therapy; and it concentrates in the liver at roughly four times the serum level, adding tissue selectivity to receptor selectivity. The 3IMY structure (Bleicher et al. 2008) shows how the selectivity is built: sobetirome's binding mode in the TR-β pocket, and the pocket residues that differ between the α and β isoforms. The mechanistic question is therefore not "which compound binds tightest" but "which binding mode gives liver THR-β activation without THR-α activation" — and a docking score, which does not know which isoform it is scoring against, cannot answer that. Test for each link: (i) a recombinant THR-β/THR-α reporter assay measures EC50 and selectivity directly against the published values; (ii) a hepatocyte assay measures fatty-acid oxidation and lipogenesis genes, separating receptor activation from functional effect; (iii) a cardiomyocyte counterscreen measures the THR-α activity that decides whether an agonist is liver-safe at all.

### 4.4 Testable hypotheses

1. The best-scoring compound on 3IMY, CHEMBL3261333, has an EC50 within 3-fold of its published 40 nM in the same recombinant assay. Falsifier: EC50 more than 3-fold worse than published. (Status after this study: untested — and the docking gives no reason to prefer it over resmetirom, since its 0.06 kcal/mol lead is noise; the assay would be testing the published potency, not a docking prediction.)
2. Sobetirome out-scores resmetirom on a resmetirom-shaped pocket, if one existed. Falsifier: it does not, which would show pocket bias — not the ligand — decides the ranking. (Status after this study: untestable today, because no resmetirom-bound TR-β structure has been deposited; the structure is the missing experiment.)
3. No docking protocol (Vina, Glide, or MM-GBSA rescoring) recovers the EC50 order (rho > 0.7) on this five-compound set. Falsifier: a rescoring protocol does recover it, which would show the failure is Vina's scoring function, not the pocket. (Status after this study: the hypothesis stands for Vina — rho 0.2 — and the rescoring experiment is the cheapest way to kill or confirm it.)

### 4.5 Proposed validation (university-lab feasible, 8–12 weeks)

Stage 1 (weeks 1–3): recombinant THR-β and THR-α reporter assay for the five compounds, same plate, n = 3, with resmetirom's published EC50 (210 nM in the Kelly assay) as the control; readout EC50 and the β/α selectivity ratio. Stage 2 (weeks 4–8): hepatocyte assay for the best two compounds from stage 1, measuring fatty-acid oxidation rate and lipogenesis-gene expression, with resmetirom as the control. Stage 3 (weeks 9–12): if any compound passes stages 1–2 — EC50 better than resmetirom's and a hepatocyte effect — a cardiomyocyte counterscreen for that compound to measure the THR-α risk; if none passes, publish the negative result with the docking data as the reason the hypothesis failed. No animal work is proposed at this stage.

## 5. Limitations

1. Docking only; no experimental data in this study. Vina noise ±1–2 kcal/mol swallows every margin in the table — the largest gap (resmetirom vs the sulfoxide analogue, 1.29 kcal/mol) is inside noise — and the only interpretable number is the compression itself: 510-fold of potency into 0.93 kcal/mol.
2. No resmetirom-bound TR-β structure exists, so resmetirom was docked into a sobetirome-shaped pocket (3IMY, 2.55 Å, sobetirome MW 328 vs resmetirom MW 435); pocket bias favours sobetirome-like ligands, yet sobetirome still ranked third.
3. All five EC50s come from one 2014 assay, and another laboratory reports resmetirom's EC50 40-fold higher (8.8 μM vs 0.21 μM), so the rank test is conditional on the Kelly assay; the docking is the only like-for-like comparison in this study, and it fails.
4. Five compounds is a small rank test; a larger set might show a weak correlation that five points cannot, and other scoring protocols (Glide, MM-GBSA, free-energy perturbation) were not tested.
5. The Tox21 read-across was run and is omitted (maximum similarity 0.28–0.51 — not safety data); the safety discussion rests on the MAESTRO-NASH trial, not on this computation.
6. No published Pakistani study reports MASH prevalence by liver biopsy or fibrosis stage, so the clinical picture is global; the Pakistani numbers in this paper are burden numbers, not response numbers.
7. The semaglutide comparison is cross-trial, not head-to-head (ESSENCE 72 weeks vs MAESTRO-NASH 52 weeks, different populations), and the cost figures are US prices — no Pakistan pricing or cost-effectiveness assessment exists.
8. The classical-canon sweep found no THR-β-relevant text: the Canon describes liver disease without a receptor concept, so the Islamic-principle discussion rests on hifz al-nafs, maslaha and 'adl as principles, not on a classical source about this target.

## 6. Conclusion

No ChEMBL THR-β agonist out-scores resmetirom in the TR-β pocket beyond Vina's noise — the best, its own N-methyl analogue, leads by 0.06 kcal/mol — and the docking cannot rank the set at all: a 510-fold EC50 spread (18 nM to 9,170 nM) collapses into 0.93 kcal/mol, Spearman rho 0.2. The most potent compound, sobetirome, ranks third on the very structure solved with it bound. The next step is a recombinant THR-β reporter assay on the same five compounds, with a cardiomyocyte counterscreen for any compound that passes — the experiment that decides whether the best pocket binder is a drug, and whether docking deserves any role in the next THR-β programme. Until that assay exists, no docking-based claim of a better resmetirom should be believed.

## 7. Policy implications

**Decision-makers:** Pakistan's Drug Regulatory Authority (DRAP) and the Ministry of National Health Services, Regulations & Coordination, with the provincial health departments and the hepatology units that run liver clinics. **Decision:** whether to spend anything on a THR-β agonist programme or on resmetirom procurement, and what must exist first. **Exact change:** (1) do not fund a THR-β agonist discovery programme on docking evidence — this study shows docking cannot rank THR-β agonists, and resmetirom is not beaten in the pocket anyway; (2) fund instead a MASH fibrosis-staging survey using non-invasive scores (FIB-4, transient elastography) in the diabetic population, so Pakistan's own F2–F3 population is counted rather than assumed from the global 30% and the Pakistani 29.8% prevalence figures; (3) commission a Pakistan cost-effectiveness model comparing resmetirom against semaglutide at negotiated prices before any formulary decision — at US prices resmetirom costs $47,400 a year at launch with an ICER of $273,445 per QALY against semaglutide's $16,188 a year in the same model, and no Pakistan pricing exists at all. **If evidence Z appears, reverse this:** if the survey shows advanced fibrosis above 10% in diabetics and a local model shows an ICER below the local threshold at an achievable price, move to a procurement assessment; if the survey shows the burden is mostly simple steatosis, keep the money on diabetes control and weight management — do not proceed to any THR-β drug programme on docking scores alone. The Islamic principle is hifz al-nafs (preservation of life) joined to 'adl (justice, Qur'an 4:135) and maslaha (public interest): where the drug costs $47,400 a year, resolves MASH in 3 of 10 patients, and the patients who need it have not been identified, spending on the drug before spending on the test would put the benefit where the poor cannot reach it and violate all three. The evidence here is thin — docking only, and a negative at that — so the recommendation is "run the survey", not "do the policy".
