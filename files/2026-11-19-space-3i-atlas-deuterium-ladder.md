## Abstract

Three interstellar objects have now passed through the Solar System — 1I/'Oumuamua (2017), 2I/Borisov (2019) and 3I/ATLAS (2025) — and the third has finally given us isotopes. JWST/NIRSpec spectra of 3I/ATLAS (22–23 December 2025, r_H ≈ 2.4 au) measured a methane D/H ratio of (3.18 ± 0.30)% — the highest methane D/H ever measured anywhere, 13 ± 2 times the Rosetta value at comet 67P — and a water D/H of (0.98 ± 0.06)%, more than an order of magnitude above any known comet, with ¹²C/¹³C ratios of 141–191 (CO₂) and 123–172 (CO). We compile the complete isotopic record of all three interstellar objects against the Solar System comet baseline and derive four new numbers: 3I/ATLAS water is 63× Vienna Standard Mean Ocean Water (VSMOW) and 18.5× the water of 67P; its methane is 204× VSMOW; its CH₄/H₂O D/H ratio (3.24) matches 67P's (4.6) within a factor of 1.4, implying the same deuteration mechanism operating ~13–18× harder; and on every measured axis — D/H, ¹²C/¹³C, CO/H₂O — both active interstellar comets sit outside the entire Solar System comet range. **Falsifiable claim:** interstellar comets fall into two formation populations — a Cold-Core population (water D/H ≥ 0.3%, ¹²C/¹³C > 120) and a Warm-Disk population (water D/H ≤ 0.05%, ¹²C/¹³C < 100) — with almost no objects in between. **Falsifier:** a single future measurement of an interstellar comet's water D/H inside the 0.05–0.3% gap band, or a cold-band D/H paired with ¹²C/¹³C < 100, kills the two-population model. **Causal identification: association only** — this is a comparative synthesis of published observations, not an experiment; the mechanism is physico-chemical (H₂D⁺ ion–molecule deuteration at T ≲ 30 K; ¹³C as a secondary nucleosynthesis product whose Galactic abundance grows with time). At the Rubin Observatory's expected discovery rate (0.9–1.9 interstellar objects per year), the test is decidable by the very next cometary interstellar object with a measured water D/H. **The memorable number: 3.18%** — one hydrogen atom in every thirty-one in 3I/ATLAS's methane is deuterium.

**Keywords:** interstellar comets; 3I/ATLAS; deuterium; isotopic ratios; JWST; Rubin Observatory; planet formation; Galactic chemical evolution

## 1. Introduction

For most of human history, the only planetary building blocks anyone could touch were the ones that formed around our own Sun. That changed in 2017. Since then, exactly three macroscopic objects from other planetary systems have been caught passing through ours: 1I/'Oumuamua (2017), 2I/Borisov (2019) and 3I/ATLAS (2025). The first was dry and enigmatic — no coma was ever detected, though its trajectory showed a non-gravitational acceleration at 30σ significance consistent with outgassing (Micheli et al. 2018). The second was a recognisable comet, but a chemically strange one: its coma held at least 1.7 times as much carbon monoxide as water (Bodewits et al. 2020), more than three times the CO/H₂O ratio of any comet previously measured inside 2.5 au, where Solar System comets show 0.2–23% (Bockelée-Morvan & Biver 2017). The third is the first for which isotopes — the fingerprints that record where and when ice formed — have been measured at all.

3I/ATLAS (C/2025 N1) was discovered on 1 July 2025 by the ATLAS survey telescope at Río Hurtado, Chile, and designated by the Minor Planet Center in MPEC 2025-N12. It is the fastest interstellar object yet seen: eccentricity e = 6.14, hyperbolic excess velocity v∞ ≈ 58 km s⁻¹, perihelion 1.356 au on 29 October 2025. Its Galactic velocity components place it in the Milky Way's thick disk, and kinematic modelling gives an age of 7.6–14 Gyr at 68% confidence (Hopkins et al. 2025) — older than the Solar System itself. It is also the most spectroscopically productive of the three: high gas production rates around perihelion enabled JWST, Hubble, ALMA and ground-based facilities to measure its coma in detail, including — for the first time for any interstellar object — the isotopic composition of its ices.

Two papers published in 2026 report those isotope measurements, both from JWST/NIRSpec spectra taken on 22–23 December 2025, when the comet was 2.4 au from the Sun and receding. Cordiner et al. (2026, *Nature* 655, 870–874) measured water D/H = (0.98 ± 0.06)% and ¹²C/¹³C = 141–191 in CO₂ and 123–172 in CO, and inferred formation at T ≲ 30 K in a metal-poor environment, with Galactic chemical-evolution models implying accretion as long ago as ~12 Gyr. Roth et al. (2026, *Nature Astronomy*) measured methane D/H = (3.18 ± 0.30)% — the highest methane D/H measured anywhere in the Solar System or beyond, 13 ± 2 times the Rosetta value at 67P — and concluded the ices are consistent with a cold, low-metallicity interstellar or protostellar environment under high cosmic-ray irradiation, while noting it is uncertain whether current star-formation or disk-chemistry models can reproduce the number at all. A third study, Protopapa et al. (2026, *ApJL*), found the comet's water ice is carried in sub-micron grains thickly mixed with refractory dust — unlike the cleaner ice grains of Solar System comets — in spectra spanning 3.31 au inbound to 5.65 au outbound.

Each of those papers is, rightly, about one object. This paper asks the question none of them could ask alone: **what do the three objects, taken together against the Solar System baseline, say about the population they come from — and what would prove that picture wrong?** Our contribution is a synthesis with new arithmetic: enrichment factors computed from the published ratios, a cross-species comparison (CH₄ vs H₂O deuteration in the same comet), and a quantitative, falsifiable two-population model with a named test for the Rubin Observatory era.

The method ancestor of this work is older than the telescope. Ibn al-Haytham's *Kitab al-Manazir* (Book of Optics, c. 1011–1021) established the experimental method itself — that claims about light, and by extension about nature, must be settled by controlled observation rather than authority. Al-Biruni's *Al-Qanun al-Mas'udi* (c. 1030s) measured the Earth's circumference to within ~1% by combining geometry with a single careful observation, the same move this paper makes: one precise ratio, correctly interpreted, constrains a whole history. Al-Battani's *Kitab al-Zij* (c. 900) fixed the solar year to within ~2 minutes. The Qur'an's invitation to observe the heavens (51:47; 67:3–5) and its question "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (39:9) frame, in the Islamic tradition, the same duty that drives this measurement: knowledge is sought because it is a trust, not a trophy. The policy implications in §7 are drawn under that principle — *hifz al-'aql*, preservation of the intellect — and are stated as falsifiable recommendations, not as certainties.

## 2. Methods

**Design.** Comparative synthesis of published observations. **Causal identification: association only.** No experiment was performed, no new observation was taken, and no causal effect is estimated. The paper's claims are of the form "objects with property X also show property Y", with a mechanism proposed (Section 5) and a falsifier stated (Section 1 and Section 6). This is stated in bold because the method brief requires it wherever causality cannot be supported.

**Analysis plan (stated before results).** Before computing anything, the plan was: (1) tabulate every published isotope ratio for the three interstellar objects and the Solar System comet baseline, with uncertainties, from peer-reviewed sources only; (2) compute enrichment factors of each 3I/ATLAS ratio relative to VSMOW (D/H = 1.5576 × 10⁻⁴) and relative to comet 67P, the best-measured Solar System comet; (3) compute the within-comet CH₄/H₂O D/H ratio for 3I/ATLAS and for 67P and compare them; (4) test whether the interstellar objects fall inside or outside the Solar System range on each axis; (5) define the two-population bands from the observed gap and state the falsifier. No post-hoc redefinition of the bands was performed after seeing the numbers: the gap band (0.05–0.3% water D/H) is the interval between the top of the measured Solar System comet range (0.053%) and one-third of the 3I/ATLAS value (0.33%), rounded to one significant figure.

### 3.2 Four derived numbers

**1. The deuterium ladder.** 3I/ATLAS water D/H (0.98%) is **62.9× VSMOW** and **18.5× the water of comet 67P** (5.3 × 10⁻⁴). Its methane D/H (3.18%) is **204× VSMOW**. The entire measured Solar System comet water D/H range spans only 0.016–0.053% (103P/Hartley 2 to 67P); 3I/ATLAS sits 18.5× above the top of that range. There is no measured comet anywhere between 0.053% and 0.98% — a gap of a factor of 18.5 with zero occupants.

**2. The same mechanism, running harder.** Within 3I/ATLAS itself, methane is 3.24× more deuterated than water (3.18% / 0.98%). At 67P the same ratio is 4.62 (derived: 67P CH₄ D/H ≈ 0.24% from Roth et al.'s factor of 13 ± 2, divided by 67P water D/H of 0.053%). The two ratios agree within a factor of 1.4. This matters: whatever enriched 3I/ATLAS's ices enriched methane and water together, in the same proportion as in an ordinary Solar System comet — the process is the same, the magnitude is ~13–18× larger. Any model of 3I/ATLAS must explain both the absolute enrichment and the preserved CH₄/H₂O ratio; a model that produces the D/H values but the wrong ratio is falsified.

**3. The carbon clock agrees with the deuterium thermometer.** The ¹²C/¹³C ratios in 3I/ATLAS (123–191) exceed the solar value (~89) by 1.4–2.1×. Because ¹³C is a secondary nucleosynthesis product — it is made from pre-existing ¹²C in stars, so its Galactic abundance grows with time — a high ¹²C/¹³C ratio means formation when the Galaxy was younger and less enriched. This is an independent clock from the D/H thermometer, and it points the same way: Cordiner et al.'s Galactic chemical-evolution interpretation gives accretion as long ago as ~12 Gyr, and the independent kinematic estimate (thick-disk membership) gives 7.6–14 Gyr at 68% confidence (Hopkins et al. 2025). Two independent methods, one answer: this ice is roughly twice the age of the Solar System. The age is a model-dependent inference, not a measurement — see Limitations.

**4. Two out of two active interstellar comets are CO-super-rich.** 3I/ATLAS's December 2025 CO/H₂O ratio (2.40 ± 0.07) and Borisov's (≥1.73) both exceed the entire Solar System comet range (0.002–0.23) by an order of magnitude. The coma of 3I/ATLAS also evolved dramatically during its passage: CO₂/H₂O fell by a factor of ~7 between August and December 2025, while CO/CO₂ rose by a factor of ~10 — a CO₂-dominated coma becoming a CO-dominated one, with a likely H₂O-dominated phase around perihelion. The pattern is consistent with ices that formed beyond the CO snowline and were never thermally processed — the same cold story the isotopes tell.

### 3.3 The two-population model and its test

The observations define two bands in water D/H:

- **Cold-Core population:** water D/H ≥ 0.3%, ¹²C/¹³C > 120. Ices formed at T ≲ 30 K in a prestellar core or the cold outer reaches of a metal-poor disk, then ejected with their fractionation intact. 3I/ATLAS is the sole member so far.
- **Warm-Disk population:** water D/H ≤ 0.05%, ¹²C/¹³C < 100. Ices formed in a warmer disk where deuteration was modest — the Solar System comets, and, by hypothesis, interstellar comets ejected from similar disks. No interstellar member has yet been measured in this band.

Between them lies the **gap band, 0.05–0.3% water D/H**, which contains no measured comet of any origin. The model predicts it will stay nearly empty: the physics of deuteration is steep in temperature (H₂D⁺ chemistry switches on below ~30 K and the enrichment grows by orders of magnitude across a narrow temperature range), so a comet's ice either formed cold enough to be strongly enriched or it did not; intermediate values require a finely tuned formation temperature that disks rarely provide.

**The test.** The Vera Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time is expected to discover 0.9–1.9 interstellar objects per year (Hoover et al. 2022) — roughly 5–10 by 2031 — with a September 2026 study estimating 0–8 cometary 3I-like objects in ten years. The next cometary interstellar object with a measured water D/H will either land in one of the two bands (model survives) or inside the 0.05–0.3% gap (model dies). Because the falsifier is a single measurement, the test is decidable by the very next object — it does not need a large sample. A secondary, joint test: a cold-band D/H (≥0.3%) must be accompanied by ¹²C/¹³C > 120; a cold-band D/H with ¹²C/¹³C < 100 would also kill the model, because it would break the link between the deuterium thermometer and the carbon clock.

## 4. Discussion

**Comparison against the strongest incumbent.** The incumbent explanation is the null: 3I/ATLAS is a single unusual object, and Solar System comets are representative of interstellar comets in general. That null is now strained on three independent axes at once. D/H: 18.5× above the Solar System range. ¹²C/¹³C: 1.4–2.1× above solar. CO/H₂O: >10× above the Solar System range, in two out of two active interstellar comets. A single outlier can be dismissed on one axis; the same direction of deviation on three axes, in two objects, is a pattern. The second incumbent is the processing hypothesis: that 3I/ATLAS's enrichment was produced after ejection, by cosmic-ray irradiation during billions of years in interstellar space, rather than at formation. Roth et al. themselves include high cosmic-ray irradiation in their interpretation, and irradiation certainly contributes — but irradiation alone, without cold formation, has no published model that reaches 3.18% methane D/H; the authors state explicitly that it is uncertain whether current models of either star formation or disk chemistry can reproduce the number. The processing hypothesis therefore remains open but unquantified, and it makes a different prediction: if irradiation dominates, D/H should correlate with time spent in interstellar space (kinematic age) rather than with formation environment — a test the next few objects can run.

**Mechanism.** Deuterium fractionation is driven by the ion–molecule reaction H₃⁺ + HD ⇌ H₂D⁺ + H₂, which is exothermic by ~230 K and therefore runs strongly forward only below ~30 K, enriching the gas in H₂D⁺ and, through subsequent chemistry, the ices that condense from it. Methane, which forms through a different chemical pathway from water, inherits a larger enrichment — hence CH₄ D/H exceeding H₂O D/H by a factor of ~3–5 in both 67P and 3I/ATLAS. Carbon isotopes work the other way: ¹²C is primary (made directly in stars), ¹³C is secondary (made from ¹²C), so the Galactic ¹²C/¹³C ratio falls with cosmic time as ¹³C accumulates. A comet with ¹²C/¹³C of 123–191 formed when that ratio was higher — earlier — than when the Sun formed (~89). The two mechanisms are independent, which is what makes their agreement informative.

**Why this matters beyond one comet.** Interstellar objects are the only direct samples of planet-forming material from other stars. If the Cold-Core population dominates — if most interstellar comets carry ices formed at ≲30 K in metal-poor environments — then the typical planetary system in the Galaxy formed from colder, older material than ours did, and the Solar System's relatively warm, well-mixed chemistry is the exception rather than the rule. That changes the priors for exoplanet atmosphere models, for the delivery of volatiles to young planets, and for the abundance of the prebiotic feedstock (deuterated organics) available to nascent biospheres.

## 5. Limitations

These limitations bite, and they are stated before any policy recommendation is drawn.

1. **n = 3 objects, n = 1 with water D/H.** The entire two-population model rests on one measured interstellar water D/H and one measured interstellar methane D/H. The gap band could be a sampling artefact: the next object could land in it, and the model would fall. That is the point of stating the falsifier, not a reason to hide it.
2. **Single-epoch, single-instrument isotope measurements.** The Roth et al. methane D/H rests on JWST/NIRSpec spectra from 22–23 December 2025; two of five background exposures failed because of field stars, so the spectra were extracted without background subtraction. The authors released their spectra and modelling scripts publicly, which mitigates this — but no independent repeat of the CH₃D measurement exists.
3. **The age is model-dependent.** "As long ago as ~12 Gyr" (Cordiner et al.) and "7.6–14 Gyr" (Hopkins et al.) are inferences from Galactic chemical-evolution and kinematic population models, not measurements. The models depend on incomplete knowledge of star-formation history, gas flows and stellar yields, and the ¹²C/¹³C ratio also varies with distance from the Galactic centre. The comet's exact birthplace remains unknown, and its path cannot be traced back over billions of years.
4. **Selection bias.** Only bright, active interstellar objects are detected. 1I/'Oumuamua showed no coma at all; if a large fraction of interstellar objects are dry and rocky, the cometary sample is biased toward the cold, volatile-rich end — exactly the population this paper calls "Cold-Core". The model describes interstellar *comets*, not interstellar objects in general, and the paper does not claim otherwise.
5. **The Islamic canon was not read in full.** The classical works cited (Ibn al-Haytham's *Kitab al-Manazir*, Al-Biruni's *Al-Qanun al-Mas'udi*, Al-Battani's *Kitab al-Zij*, Al-Tusi's *Tadhkira*) were consulted through the programme's verified literature map, which records their dates, contents and availability; the full texts were not read for this paper. *Al-Qanun al-Mas'udi* has no complete modern English translation, and *Kitab al-Zij* survives in Arabic and Latin editions with only partial modern translations. Many classical Arabic astronomical manuscripts remain undigitised in libraries (Süleymaniye, Istanbul; Dar al-Kutub, Cairo; the British Library) with no online copy — the gap the programme's map names, and this paper names again. No claim is made to have read "every book"; the claim is to have used the relevant available canon and to have named what was not available.
6. **The bands are drawn from two data points.** The Cold-Core/Warm-Disk boundary values (0.3% and 0.05%) are set by the observed gap, not derived from a physical model of disk chemistry. A quantitative disk-chemistry model that predicts the band edges from first principles is the obvious next step and is not attempted here.

## 6. Policy implications

**Decision-maker 1 — ESA and NASA (Comet Interceptor, launch 2029).** Comet Interceptor will wait at the Sun–Earth L2 point for a dynamically new comet or an interstellar object, then fly by it. *Exact change:* the mission's target-selection criteria should explicitly prioritise an interstellar target over a dynamically new Solar System comet when one is available, and its payload operations plan should include an isotope-measurement mode (mass-spectrometer D/H and ¹²C/¹³C) as a Level-1 science requirement, not an opportunistic add-on. *Why:* a single flyby D/H measurement decides the two-population test years before Rubin accumulates a sample. *Falsifier:* if the flyby measures a gap-band D/H, the two-population model is abandoned and the mission's isotope requirement is downgraded. *Islamic principle:* *hifz al-'aql* — preservation of the intellect; the Qur'an asks "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" **Decision-maker 3 — the JWST and Hubble time-allocation committees.** 3I/ATLAS is receding and fading; its three JWST epochs may remain the most detailed look at its ice for years. *Exact change:* approve Director's Discretionary Time for rapid-response NIRSpec follow-up of the next interstellar comet within two weeks of discovery, before perihelion, so that inbound (cold, unprocessed) ices are measured rather than only outbound ones. *Why:* the August 2025 (inbound, 3.32 au) and December 2025 (outbound, 2.4 au) spectra of 3I/ATLAS already show the coma changing by factors of 7–10; inbound data are the cleaner record of formation conditions.

## 7. Conclusion

3I/ATLAS gave us the first isotopes ever measured in material from another planetary system, and they are extreme: methane D/H of (3.18 ± 0.30)%, water D/H of (0.98 ± 0.06)%, ¹²C/¹³C of 123–191. Our synthesis adds four numbers — 63× VSMOW, 18.5× 67P, a CH₄/H₂O D/H ratio of 3.24 that matches 67P's within a factor of 1.4, and a CO/H₂O ratio above the entire Solar System range in two out of two active interstellar comets — and one testable claim: interstellar comets come in two formation populations, Cold-Core and Warm-Disk, separated by an empty gap band in water D/H (0.05–0.3%). The next cometary interstellar object with a measured water D/H will confirm the model or kill it with a single number. That is the standard this programme holds itself to: a paper is finished when a telescope can prove it wrong. The Rubin Observatory, discovering roughly one to two interstellar objects a year, will run that test within the decade — and the observatories, agencies and countries that prepare for it now will be the ones that read the answer first.

**The memorable number: 3.18% — one hydrogen atom in every thirty-one in the methane of 3I/ATLAS is deuterium, the highest methane D/H ever measured.**
