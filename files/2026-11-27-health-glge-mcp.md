## Abstract

GlgE is the maltosyltransferase of the trehalose-to-α-glucan pathway of *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Mtb). Its inactivation kills the bacterium by self-poisoning: maltose-1-phosphate (M1P) accumulates to toxic levels through a self-amplifying feedback loop (Kalscheuer et al., 2010). No GlgE inhibitor has reached the clinic, and only one measured inhibitor potency exists in the published literature — α-maltose-C-phosphonate (MCP), IC50 230 ± 24 µM (Veleti et al., 2014). We asked whether AutoDock Vina docking at the GlgE M1P site can rank MCP above its own substrate and above an untested oxocarbenium mimic. ChEMBL holds a GlgE target entry (CHEMBL3112377) with zero bioactivities, so no measured series exists to learn from. No Mtb GlgE structure could be docked at a validated site: the Mtb maltose complex (PDB 4U33, 3.29 Å) carries six maltose copies with no identifiable donor-site centroid. We therefore docked on the inhibitor-bound surrogate used by the structural literature itself — *Streptomyces coelicolor* GlgEI-V279S, engineered to emulate the Mtb M1P site (PDB 4U31, 1.85 Å; Lindenberger et al., 2015) — at the MCP centroid. The oxocarbenium mimic DDGIM scored best (−7.72 kcal/mol), MCP second (−7.56), and the substrate M1P third (−7.13): a total spread of 0.59 kcal/mol, roughly one-third of Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise. Docking therefore cannot rank the only measured GlgE inhibitor against its substrate or an untested mimic, and all three ligands are non-permeable (TPSA 172–236 Å², logP ≈ −5, Veber fail). The falsifiable claim is that any future GlgE docking study that reports a ranking from a spread under 1 kcal/mol without measured potencies is reporting noise. The field's bottleneck is not computation but chemistry: one measured potency in fifteen years, and no whole-cell data. Pakistan — ~670,000 TB cases in 2024, 6.3% of the global burden — should fund MCP optimisation as a national drug-discovery line with a hard go/no-go on a sub-10 µM analogue.

## Introduction

Tuberculosis killed an estimated 1.23 million people in 2024, with 10.7 million falling ill (WHO Global Tuberculosis Report 2025). Pakistan recorded an estimated 670,000 cases in 2024 — 6.3% of the global burden, the fifth-highest national burden — and roughly 12,000 new multidrug-/rifampicin-resistant (MDR/RR-TB) cases emerge each year; resistance is present in ~2.3% of new cases and ~4.6% of previously treated cases, and nearly 40% of resistant cases are already pre-XDR. New drugs (bedaquiline, delamanid, pretomanid) have improved MDR-TB outcomes, but every one of them targets a mechanism the bacterium has already learned to evade, and none is a GlgE inhibitor. A target with a genuinely new killing mechanism is therefore worth pursuing — and GlgE is the most mechanistically distinctive validated target in Mtb that no drug touches.

The GlgE pathway converts trehalose to cytosolic α-glucan in four steps: trehalose is isomerised to maltose by TreS, phosphorylated to maltose-1-phosphate (M1P) by the maltokinase Pep2, transferred by GlgE (Rv1327c; UniProt P9WQ17; 701 amino acids; glycoside hydrolase family GH13_3, double-displacement retaining mechanism), and branched by GlgB. Kalscheuer et al. (2010) showed that inactivating GlgE kills Mtb rapidly *in vitro* and in mice, not by starving the cell of glucan but by self-poisoning: M1P accumulates, and a self-amplifying feedback loop upregulates the trehalose-forming enzymes, making more of the poison. The same study found a synthetic-lethal interaction with the glucosyltransferase Rv3032, so a GlgE inhibitor combined with an Rv3032 inhibitor would be, in the authors' words, extremely potent. GlgE has no human orthologue, so on-target human toxicity is in principle avoidable.

Fifteen years after that validation, the medicinal chemistry of GlgE is almost empty. The only published inhibitor with a measured potency is MCP, a non-hydrolysable C-phosphonate mimic of M1P, IC50 230 ± 24 µM against Mtb GlgE in a coupled phosphate-release assay (Veleti et al., 2014). A second first-generation compound, DDGIM (2,5-dideoxy-3-O-α-D-glucopyranosyl-2,5-imino-D-mannitol), an oxocarbenium-transition-state mimic, has a crystal structure but no published potency (Lindenberger et al., 2015). No ChEMBL bioactivity exists for GlgE at all. This paper asks a narrow, falsifiable question: **can Vina docking at the GlgE M1P site rank MCP above its substrate M1P and the untested mimic DDGIM by more than the method's own noise?** If it cannot, then the many docking-only GlgE papers in the literature are ranking noise, and the field should stop screening and start synthesising.

## Methods

**Target and structures.** Mtb GlgE is UniProt P9WQ17 (GLGE_MYCTU, Rv1327c, 701 aa). The LeadForge pipeline (UniProtKB → ChEMBL → PubChem → RDKit → Vina) was run with `--target P9WQ17 --ref maltose-1-phosphate`: it confirmed the accession but found ChEMBL target CHEMBL3112377 with zero bioactivities and no PDB entry for the accession, so no docking was performed by the pipeline. We then verified structures manually against RCSB. PDB 4U33 ("Structure of Mtb GlgE bound to maltose", 3.29 Å) exists, but its asymmetric unit contains six maltose copies across six chains with no identifiable donor-site centroid, so it was not docked. PDB 4U3C (Mtb GlgE with maltohexaose, 3.98 Å) was likewise not used. Docking was performed on PDB 4U31, "Sco GlgEI-V279S in Complex with maltose-C-phosphonate" (1.85 Å), the structure in which the V279S variant was engineered specifically to emulate the Mtb GlgE M1P binding site (Lindenberger et al., 2015). The grid centre was the MCP residue (GPM, chain C) centroid, [24.84, −31.26, 281.44], box 22.5 Å. This is a surrogate-site docking, not Mtb-native docking, and every score below carries that caveat.

**Ligands.** Three compounds: (i) M1P, the natural substrate (PubChem CID 167489, isomeric SMILES, full stereochemistry); (ii) MCP, the only known inhibitor (SMILES extracted from the 4U31 crystal structure — GPM chain C residue 1 plus the adjoining GLC residue — via RDKit, with the phosphonate valence corrected to CP(=O)(O)O; formula C13H25O13P); (iii) DDGIM (SMILES extracted from PDB 4U2Y, RZM chain A residue 701, via RDKit; formula C12H23NO9). Descriptors and PAINS alerts were computed with RDKit. Docking used AutoDock Vina (exhaustiveness 8, 3 poses), the same protocol as the other papers in this series.

**Literature.** Targeted searches of PubMed, RCSB, PubChem and the WHO Global Tuberculosis Report 2025 (Pakistan figures via its press coverage). No data, PMIDs, DOIs or citations were invented; every number traces to a source named here or to a computation in the accompanying JSON (`hidden_files/dock-HP-118-2026-11-27-redock.json`).

## Results

**There is no measured GlgE series to dock against.** ChEMBL target CHEMBL3112377 (GlgE) holds zero bioactivities, and the pipeline's similarity screen therefore returned only the query compound. The entire published, measured GlgE inhibitor record is a single compound, MCP, at 230 ± 24 µM. No whole-cell (MIC) data for any GlgE inhibitor was found.

**Docking on the emulated site cannot rank the three ligands.** Vina best scores (top-3 poses in parentheses): DDGIM −7.72 (−7.72, −7.50, −6.81), MCP −7.56 (−7.56, −7.29, −6.47), M1P −7.13 (−7.13, −6.94, −6.73) kcal/mol. The total spread is 0.59 kcal/mol; MCP beats its own substrate by only 0.43 kcal/mol, and the untested mimic DDGIM beats the only measured inhibitor by 0.16 kcal/mol. All three spreads sit far inside Vina's ±1–2 kcal/mol noise, and the pose-to-pose spread within a single ligand (up to 1.09 kcal/mol for MCP) exceeds the spread between ligands. Docking therefore reports no ranking at all: the measured inhibitor, its substrate, and an untested mimic are indistinguishable.

**All three ligands are non-permeable.** M1P: MW 422.28, logP −5.28, HBD 9, HBA 12, TPSA 236.06 Å², 2 Lipinski violations, Veber fail. MCP: MW 420.30, logP −5.17, HBD 9, HBA 11, TPSA 226.83 Å², 2 violations, Veber fail. DDGIM: MW 325.31, logP −5.14, HBD 8, HBA 10, TPSA 172.10 Å², 1 violation, Veber fail. All three are PAINS-clean. These are sugar phosphates and a sugar mimic: highly polar, charged at physiological pH, and — as the M1P pathway itself implies — they cannot cross the mycobacterial cell envelope without a transporter or a prodrug strategy. MCP's 230 µM enzymatic potency is therefore a starting point, not a lead: it is 230-fold weaker than a 1 µM lead threshold, before permeability is even considered.

**The structural literature is rich; the chemistry is not.** Experimental GlgE structures exist for both species: Mtb GlgE with maltose (4U33, 3.29 Å) and with maltohexaose (4U3C, 3.98 Å); Sco GlgEI-V279S with MCP (4U31, 1.85 Å) and with the DDGIM azasugar (4U2Y, 2.48 Å); Sco GlgE apo (3ZSS, 1.8 Å) and with M1P bound (4CN1, 2.55 Å) (Lindenberger et al., 2015). A homology model of Mtb GlgE with three binding cavities (primary plus two secondary sites) was published before any Mtb structure existed (Sengupta et al., 2014). The binding site is therefore well characterised — the gap is that only one compound has ever been measured against it.

## Discussion

The honest reading of this paper is a negative result with a constructive edge. The negative: Vina cannot rank GlgE ligands at the emulated Mtb site — the only measured inhibitor scores within noise of its own substrate, and an untested mimic scores best. Any study that reports a GlgE "lead" from a docking spread under 1 kcal/mol without measured potency is reporting the scoring function's noise, and the literature contains several such studies (including virtual screens against the pre-2015 homology model). The constructive edge: the experiment that would settle the question is cheap and defined — synthesise MCP analogues, measure IC50s against recombinant Mtb GlgE (the Veleti coupled assay), and only then dock. One measured series of ten compounds would do more for this target than another hundred docking runs.

Three facts should shape that chemistry. First, the self-poisoning mechanism means a GlgE inhibitor does not need to starve the cell; it needs only to block the enzyme long enough for M1P to reach toxic levels, and the feedback loop then amplifies the damage — so partial, reversible inhibition may be cidal, unlike most antibiotic targets where partial inhibition is merely static. Second, the escape route is known in advance: loss of GlgE function can be suppressed by upstream mutations that abolish M1P synthesis, so a GlgE inhibitor used alone invites resistance, and combination with an Rv3032 inhibitor (the synthetic-lethal partner) or with a standard regimen should be planned from the start, not after resistance appears. Third, permeability is the real barrier: every ligand in this study fails Veber, and the mycobacterial envelope is the hardest in bacteriology. MCP optimisation must therefore be prodrug-first (e.g., masked phosphonate) or transporter-targeted, or the programme will repeat the fate of many polar antibacterials — potent in the tube, inert in the cell.

For Pakistan, the stakes are concrete. With ~670,000 cases a year and ~12,000 new MDR/RR-TB cases, a new-mechanism drug that kills by self-poisoning — and that no current resistance mechanism touches — is worth a national chemistry effort. The cost of that effort is small beside the cost of the disease: one medicinal-chemistry group, one recombinant-enzyme assay, and a defined go/no-go.

**One memorable number:** 230 µM — the best measured GlgE inhibitor in the world is 230 times weaker than a 1 µM lead, and it is the *only* measured GlgE inhibitor in the world.

## Limitations

1. **Surrogate structure.** All docking is on Sco GlgEI-V279S (4U31), not Mtb GlgE. The V279S variant was designed to emulate the Mtb M1P site, but residue-level differences remain, and scores are site-emulation, not Mtb-native. The Mtb structure 4U33 could not be docked at a validated centroid.
2. **No measured series.** Only one published IC50 exists (MCP, 230 µM). With a single point, no docking–potency correlation can be computed; the ranking claim is a null result, not a validated failure.
3. **Vina noise.** The 0.59 kcal/mol spread is far below the method's ±1–2 kcal/mol error. No pose was inspected against the crystal MCP pose (no RMSD self-dock validation was possible for M1P/DDGIM, which have no Mtb co-crystal).
4. **SMILES provenance.** MCP and DDGIM SMILES were extracted from crystal coordinates via RDKit; the MCP phosphonate valence was corrected by hand from `[PH](O)(O)O` to `CP(=O)(O)O`. Connectivity is correct; protonation at pH 7.4 was left to the standard prep.
5. **No whole-cell data.** No MIC, no macrophage-infection, and no animal data exists for any GlgE inhibitor. Enzymatic IC50 is not antibacterial activity.
6. **Resistance escape.** Upstream mutations abolishing M1P synthesis can suppress GlgE loss; monotherapy would select for them.
7. **No laboratory verification.** This is a computational and literature study only. Nothing here is a drug, a dose, or a treatment recommendation.

## Conclusion

GlgE remains one of the best-validated and least-exploited TB targets: genetically essential, cidal by self-poisoning, synthetically lethal with Rv3032, and without a human orthologue. But fifteen years after validation, the field has one measured inhibitor (MCP, 230 µM), no whole-cell data, and — as shown here — a docking method that cannot distinguish that inhibitor from its own substrate. The next step is not more screening. It is synthesis: MCP analogues, measured IC50s, a prodrug strategy for the mycobacterial envelope, and a hard go/no-go. Until a sub-10 µM analogue with whole-cell activity exists, GlgE is a validated target with no lead — and that is a precise, actionable statement of where the field stands.

## §7 Policy implications

- **Decision-maker:** Pakistan National TB Control Programme (NTP) and the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP), with the Ministry of National Health Services.
- **Decision:** Fund a dedicated GlgE medicinal-chemistry work package — one chemistry group plus the recombinant-enzyme assay — inside the NTP research budget, rather than funding another docking-only screen.
- **Exact change:** Allocate ring-fenced funding for MCP analogue synthesis and IC50 measurement, with a published go/no-go: a sub-10 µM analogue with whole-cell (MIC) activity by 2028, or the line is stopped and the funds reallocated to scaling up bedaquiline/pretomanid regimens.
- **Reverse-if-Z:** If no analogue reaches sub-10 µM with whole-cell activity, or if upstream escape mutations arise at high frequency in the first resistance screen, stop the GlgE line and reallocate — the target is validated but undruggable on the evidence, and the money is better spent on regimens that work today.
- **Islamic principle:** *Hifz al-nafs* (preservation of life) and *la darar wa la dirar* (no harm, no reciprocated harm): a drug that turns the pathogen's own metabolism into its poison uses the enemy's strength against it without harming the host, which has no GlgE — the precise form of proportionate, life-preserving stewardship that the Islamic canon on antimicrobial stewardship requires (literature-map seed #11, AMR). Stewardship also forbids spending public funds on unmeasured claims: the go/no-go above is the administrative form of that principle.
